# Platform costs

What do the services, applications and teams a team runs cost per month? Steward collects the costs from the platforms it already reads – **AWS, Snowflake, Confluent Cloud, AI gateways** – and from **FOCUS files** of every other provider, keeps them in one cost model and shows them per service, application, team and platform: the last 12 months, the current month so far, a trend and a forecast, monthly budgets with notices, a monitor check for unusual increases, a scorecard part, a report section, a dataset and read endpoints of the public API.

**Showback, not FinOps.** Steward shows costs where people already look after their services. Invoices, chargeback between departments, commitment and rightsizing optimisation stay in a FinOps tool ([ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), _not planned_).

## The cost model (FOCUS)

Every cost line is a row of the **FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification (FOCUS)** of the FinOps Foundation, version 1.x – the column ids are FOCUS's own, so a FOCUS file and Steward's model speak the same language ([focus.finops.org](https://focus.finops.org/focus-specification/)):

| FOCUS column                                | In Steward                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `BillingPeriodStart`, `BillingPeriodEnd`    | The billing period (the calendar month of the charge when a file has none)                                           |
| `ChargePeriodStart`, `ChargePeriodEnd`      | The charge period – a day (Snowflake, Confluent, AI, FOCUS rows of an hour or a day) or a month (AWS)                |
| `BilledCost`                                | What is invoiced                                                                                                     |
| `EffectiveCost`                             | What it cost after discounts and amortisation – **what Steward shows** (`BilledCost` where a source has no other)    |
| `BillingCurrency`                           | ISO 4217 code of both amounts                                                                                        |
| `ProviderName` (`ServiceProviderName`, 1.3) | AWS, Snowflake, Confluent, AI gateway, or the file's provider                                                        |
| `ServiceName`, `ServiceCategory`            | e.g. _Amazon Relational Database Service_ / _Databases_, `WAREHOUSE_METERING` / _Analytics_, `KAFKA` / _Integration_ |
| `ResourceId`, `ResourceName`                | Confluent cluster or connector, AI model, the file's resource                                                        |
| `SubAccountId`                              | Snowflake account, Confluent environment, the file's sub account                                                     |
| `ChargeCategory`                            | `Usage`, `Credit`, `Adjustment`, … as the source says                                                                |
| `Tags`                                      | A JSON object of key–value pairs (string values; at most 50)                                                         |

Steward adds its **links**: the platform the cost belongs to, the component and the application ([mapping](#mapping)); services and owning teams follow from the application when costs are shown, so a change in the catalogue is reflected at once.

**Amounts are decimals with their currency.** They are kept as whole millionths of the currency unit (an integer, never a binary float; rounding half away from zero at the sixth decimal) and travel as decimal strings (`"1840.00"`). **Nothing is converted between currencies**: every total, chart, budget and check is per currency. A scope with costs in USD and EUR shows two sections.

**Shown with the decimals of their currency** (since 0.18.0). Pages, CSV files, report sections, notifications, monitor messages and audit entries round an amount (half away from zero) to the minor units of its currency in ISO 4217: no decimals for the yen (JPY), the won (KRW), the króna (ISK), the Chilean peso (CLP), the dong (VND) and the other currencies without minor units (BIF, DJF, GNF, KMF, PYG, RWF, UGX, UYI, VUV, XAF, XOF, XPF); three for the dinars of Bahrain, Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait, Libya and Tunisia and the Omani rial (BHD, IQD, JOD, KWD, LYD, TND, OMR); four for CLF and UYW; two for every other code. The API and the datasets keep every decimal (a yearly plan of 100000 JPY is `8333.333333` a month there, `8333` on the page). An amount typed by hand – a plan line, a cost entry, a budget – may not have more decimals than its currency: `1500.50 JPY` is refused, `1500` and `1500.00` are fine. The table is `CURRENCY_MINOR_UNITS` next to `ISO_4217_CODES` in `shared/src/costs.ts`.

## Sources

Each platform's costs are set up on its **Source** tab (_Costs → Platforms → the platform → Source_, or _Costs_ on the platform page) by whoever may manage the platform. A source is read with the rights of who last saved it (they must still be allowed to use the platform); every call goes through the platform's client and the outbound policy like every platform call.

| Source              | How                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Granularity | Read                               | Re-reads           | Permissions                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **AWS**             | Cost Explorer `GetCostAndUsage`, `UnblendedCost`, grouped by the application tag **and** by service in one request series ([AWS.md](AWS.md))                                                                  | month       | once a day (each request is $0.01) | the last 12 months | `ce:GetCostAndUsage`; the tag activated as cost allocation tag                                                                                     |
| **Snowflake**       | `SNOWFLAKE.ORGANIZATION_USAGE.USAGE_IN_CURRENCY_DAILY` (`USAGE_IN_CURRENCY`, `CURRENCY`, `SERVICE_TYPE`, `BILLING_TYPE`) of the platform's account, through the SQL API ([SNOWFLAKE.md](SNOWFLAKE.md))        | day         | once a day                         | the last 35 days   | the organisation's usage views (`GRANT IMPORTED PRIVILEGES ON DATABASE SNOWFLAKE`, an account with ORGANIZATION_USAGE; not for reseller contracts) |
| ↳ fallback          | `SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.METERING_DAILY_HISTORY` `CREDITS_BILLED` × the **price per credit** set on the source                                                                                                | day         | once a day                         | the last 35 days   | the ACCOUNT_USAGE views (as for the database readouts)                                                                                             |
| **Confluent Cloud** | The Costs API `GET /billing/v1/costs?start_date=&end_date=` – daily lines per resource, product and line type, US dollars, at most one month per request, up to three days ago ([CONFLUENT.md](CONFLUENT.md)) | day         | once a day                         | the last 35 days   | the Cloud API key's service account needs **OrganizationAdmin** or **BillingAdmin**                                                                |
| **AI gateways**     | The usage log's costs (the prices per 1,000 tokens set on the models) per day, model and process ([AI.md](AI.md))                                                                                             | day         | every hour (local, no call)        | the last 35 days   | – (the currency is set on the source, else _Cost settings → Currency of AI prices_)                                                                |
| **FOCUS files**     | A CSV file in FOCUS, uploaded with a preview or pushed from CI ([below](#focus-files))                                                                                                                        | row's       | when imported                      | the file's days    | who may manage the platform                                                                                                                        |

The first read of a connector reads 12 months back (as far as the source has them); later reads re-read the last 35 days (AWS: the 12 months it always reads). The job `costs` decides every few minutes which sources are due; _Read now_ reads one at once – AWS keeps to its daily read, as Cost Explorer charges every request. The read's outcome, its note (e.g. _ORGANIZATION_USAGE.USAGE_IN_CURRENCY_DAILY_) and an error are shown on the Source tab.

The **AWS** component readouts (cloud accounts of applications) and the cost model use the same read: the account is read once a day, now with one request series grouped by the tag and the service instead of two – the readout still shows its last six months.

Later, optional: Kubernetes through OpenCost (per namespace), Azure Cost Management.

### FOCUS files

AWS (Data Exports, FOCUS 1.0), Azure (Cost Management exports, FOCUS), Google Cloud (the FOCUS BigQuery view) and other providers export FOCUS. On a platform's Source tab, **Import a file** reads the CSV and shows a **preview** – nothing is stored yet: rows and cost lines, the period, totals per currency, the providers, how many lines link to an application, ignored columns, rows that are left out and why, whether the same file was imported before. The preview counts a line as linked only when you may see its application – a line of an application hidden from you counts as unassigned, like a name no application has. Then **Import**:

- **Replace these days** (default): lines of earlier imports of this platform for the file's days are replaced – a corrected file. Changed figures of closed days are audited as corrections.
- **Add**: earlier lines stay – for files of other accounts for the same days.

Reading: comma, semicolon or tab separated (from the header line), RFC 4180 quoting, a byte order mark, CRLF/LF/CR; one pass over the characters, no regular expression on the content. Required columns: `BilledCost`, `BillingCurrency`, `ChargePeriodStart`, `ChargePeriodEnd` (ids are matched case-insensitively). Limits: 25 MB, 500,000 rows, 300 columns, 16,000 characters per cell. Rows of an hour or a day are summed per day, provider, service, category, resource, sub account, charge category and tags. `Tags` must be a JSON object; keys that could change an object's prototype (`__proto__`, `constructor`, `prototype`) are dropped. The **same file** (its SHA-256) is refused a second time for a platform. Every import is audited (`cost.imported`) with the file name, SHA-256, size, rows, period, mode and totals; the list on the Source tab shows them. A large file is read and linked in chunks, so the server keeps answering other requests meanwhile; the lines are then written in one transaction.

### Push from CI

A **push token** belongs to one platform and can do nothing but send FOCUS files for it – the push-token pattern of database schemas ([DATABASES.md](DATABASES.md#tbls-from-ci)):

```sh
curl -sS -f -X POST "https://steward.example.com/api/hooks/costs/<platform id>?fileName=focus.csv&mode=replace" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $STEWARD_COST_TOKEN" \
  -H 'Content-Type: text/csv' \
  --data-binary @focus.csv
```

- Created on the Source tab (_Push from CI → New push token_) by who may manage the platform; shown **once**, only its SHA-256 is stored; optional expiry, allowed addresses (CIDR) and rate limit (6 a minute by default). Rotate and revoke there. Creating and rotating need a signed-in session (API tokens get `403 SESSION_REQUIRED`); revoking works with an API token too.
- It works only while its creator is active and may still manage the platform; its lines link with the creator's rights ([Mapping](#mapping)). API tokens and sign-in sessions are refused; a token of another platform, a revoked or expired one and a refused address all get the same `401`. The token is checked from the headers **before** the body is read.
- `mode` (`replace` or `add`) and `fileName` come from the query string. The answer names the import, the lines written and replaced and the corrections. Creating, rotating, revoking and every push are audited.

## Mapping

Each line is linked when it is written, and again once a day (a new component, a renamed application). In this order:

1. **Confluent Cloud**: the cluster (`ResourceId` `lkc-…`) names the **Kafka platform** whose variable `confluentClusterId` is that id – the cost belongs to that platform (_Kafka (demo)_ in the demo); failing that, the environment (`SubAccountId` `env-…`) and a Kafka platform with `confluentEnvironmentId` (and no cluster id). Its application is the one all **Kafka topic components** on that platform belong to, else the platform's own application.
2. **The tag** – the cost source's _tag that names the application_ (default `application`; for AWS the account's variable `tagKey`) – names a **component on the platform** (an AWS cloud account's name is its tag value) or else an **application by its name** (case-insensitive; a name two applications share links neither). A **FOCUS file** is written by a person, not read from the provider: its tag links by name only to an application **the importer may change** (for a push: the token's creator; the daily linking uses each import's importer with their rights of today) or one **tied to the platform** (the platform's own application, or one with a component on it). Any other name leaves the line unassigned – nobody books costs on another team's application.
3. **The platform's own application** (_Catalogue → application → integrations_).
4. Otherwise the line is **unassigned** – shown per platform (_Unassigned_ on the platform, its column in the platform list).

A **service** has the costs of its applications (an application in two services counts fully for each – the services' totals are not added up). A **team** has the costs of the applications it owns (its own owning teams, or for an application without, its services' teams) and the unassigned costs of the platforms it owns. A **platform** has every line that belongs to it.

## Late and corrected figures

A source re-reads its window and replaces what it read before: a line that comes again replaces the stored one, a stored line of the window that does not come again is removed. **AWS and Snowflake values change for up to 72 hours and until the month is closed**, Confluent's lines are complete after 72 hours. A line whose charge period had ended before it was last read and whose amount changes (or that goes away) is a **correction**: it is marked and audited (`cost.corrected`, per month and currency, before and after, the number of lines). A line of a period still running (the current month so far, a day not over) simply changes.

## What people see

**Catalogue → Costs**: services, applications, teams or platforms – one row per scope and currency with _this month so far_, the _forecast_, the _last month_, the _change_ (forecast against the last month) and the _budget_ (platforms: what is unassigned). Table or cards, sortable, searchable; platforms without costs or a cost source are left out. Totals per currency for the platforms (services and teams overlap, so they have none). The service, application and platform pages have a _Costs_ button.

One scope shows per currency:

- the **12 complete months and the current one so far** as bars (the current month hatched; a table twin with `EffectiveCost` and `BilledCost`);
- the **forecast** of the current month: each platform's amount so far ÷ the days its lines cover × the days of the month, added up (sources lag by different times); from the 3rd day of a month;
- the **trend**: the Theil–Sen line through the complete months (the trend functions of the dashboards) – change per month, in percent of the average month, _steady_ or _uneven_; from five months;
- the **budget** and its state, the costs **by provider and service**, the **applications** (of a service, team or platform) or **platforms** (of an application) behind them, what is **unassigned** (platforms), and the sources with their last read.

## Budgets

A **monthly budget** per service, application or team, in one currency (_Set budget_ on the costs page):

- Who may change the service or application sets it (a team's: administrators); every change is audited (`cost.budget_changed`, before and after, the comment).
- **Raising** a budget – a higher amount, another currency, or no budget any more – needs a **step-up**: the control point `cost.budget` of the [assurance policy](ASSURANCE.md), on by default, like the AI budgets; lowering one never asks.
- A budget covers the **whole** service or team, also the applications a reader may not see. So whoever does not see every application of the service or team sees **only the budget's state** – _within budget_, _80 % reached_, _budget reached_ – and no use, percentage or forecast (the costs page, the list, the scorecard, the report section, `/api/v1` with `stateOnly: true`, the notice): the use minus what they see would be the costs of the others. Who sees every application (administrators, auditors, owners of a service whose applications they all see) gets the figures.
- At **80 %** and **100 %** of the month's budget a notice goes out once per budget, month and level – event **Cost budget** (`cost.budget`, in the app and by e-mail by default, [NOTIFICATIONS.md](NOTIFICATIONS.md)) – to the named owners and the members of the owning teams (of a team: its members), else to the administrators; a recipient who does not see every application reads the state only. A raised budget may notify again in the month.
- A budget stops nothing – it is showback.

## Monitor check

**Cost change** (`type: cost`, [MONITORS.md](MONITORS.md)): the costs of a scope against the period before.

```yaml
checks:
  - id: shop-costs
    name: Shop costs not rising
    type: cost
    scope: application # service, application, team or platform
    target: app_shop # its id
    period: month # month: last month against the month before; monthToDate: this month so far against the same share of the month before
    maxIncreasePct: 20 # up by more than 20 % …
    maxIncrease: '500' # … or by more than 500 (in the currency compared)
    currency: USD # empty: the one with the highest costs
    failAs: degraded
    noData: unknown
```

It runs with the monitor owner's rights – also on its schedule, when no one is signed in: the scope must be one they (and whoever saves the monitor) may see, and a service or team counts only the applications they may see. Everybody who may see the monitor reads its message and value, so amounts appear there only when the owner may see them **and** _Hide amounts from viewers_ is off; with the setting on, the check reports the change in percent only (a limit `maxIncrease` still applies: "more than the amount allowed").

## Dashboards and the API

- **Scorecard**: the part **Costs** – _attention_ when the month's costs reached the service's budget, _open_ at 80 % or with a forecast above it, _OK_ below; shown only once a service has a budget ([SCORECARD.md](SCORECARD.md)). A reader who does not see every application of the service gets the state only ([Budgets](#budgets)).
- **Report section _Costs_** of [report schedules](REPORTS.md) – per service, application, team or platform the costs of the report period against the period before, per currency, with the budget (platforms: unassigned); a monthly schedule is the monthly cost report. Collected with the owner's rights.
- **Built-in dataset _Costs_** (`costs.overview`): one row per scope and currency with this month, last month, forecast, change, unassigned, budget percentage and state; metrics `thisMonth`, `lastMonth`, `forecast` of the platforms in the currency with the highest costs. Published by the job at most hourly. Its rows name every scope, so **administrators and auditors** see it (like the AI usage) – also through `/api/v1/datasets/costs.overview`.
- **Public API** (read-only, [API.md](API.md)): `GET /api/v1/costs?scope=service|application|team|platform` (paginated) and `GET /api/v1/costs/{scope}/{id}` – with the token owner's rights.
- **Internal API**: `GET /api/costs`, `GET /api/costs/:scope/:id`, `PUT /api/costs/budgets`, `GET|PUT /api/costs/settings`, `GET|PUT /api/costs/platform/:id/source`, `POST /api/costs/platform/:id/read`, the imports and push tokens below `/api/costs/platform/:id/…`, and the push `POST /api/hooks/costs/:platformId`.

## Planning per service

Beside what the platforms cost per month, a service team plans **what it will spend in a fiscal year** on the systems and solutions it uses and on service and support – licences, cloud, support contracts, hardware – and records what it incurred ([ADR 0026](adr/0026-service-cost-planning.md)). Lightweight and internal to the service: **no person-hours or effort, no approvals, orders or invoice processing, no chargeback, no currency conversion**. The [monthly budget](#budgets) stays as it is and is independent: it watches the costs read per month; the plan covers everything a service spends per fiscal year.

**The fiscal year** starts in one month for the whole instance – _Costs → Settings → Fiscal year starts in_ (administrators, default January, audited as `cost.settings_changed`). A fiscal year is named by the calendar year it starts in: _FY 2026_ when it starts in January, _FY 2026/27_ when it starts in, say, October (October 2026 to September 2027). Changing the start month later reads every plan in the new boundaries (a line keeps its year and its position in it; an entry counts in the year its date falls in) – decide it once. Before saving, the settings dialog asks and says how many cost entries would then fall into another fiscal year than their plan line (`GET /api/costs/settings/fiscal-year-change?startMonth=`); the audit entry records that count. Such an entry **keeps its link**: in its own year it counts as unplanned and shows _Plan line of FY …: the title_ (`planLineYear` in the API), and the line no longer counts it. It can still be changed while it keeps that link – a typo in the reference, say – or be linked to a plan line of its year; a new link always needs a line of the entry's fiscal year.

**Where:** _Catalogue → the service → Cost plan_ (with the Costs module on). Pick the fiscal year; _Add plan line_, _Add cost entry_, _Copy to FY …_, _Export (CSV)_ of the lines or the entries. _Costs read_ opens the costs Steward reads for the service (_Costs → Services → the service_, also the service's _Costs_ button), and that page links back with _Cost plan_. The dialogs save with Enter; a cost entry on a plan line takes the line's currency (and, when nothing is chosen yet, what the line is for); a new entry is dated today in your browser's time zone. Removing a line or an entry asks first.

**Plan lines** – per service and fiscal year, at most 200:

| Field                 | What                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Title                 | e.g. _Historian licences_                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Category              | Licences, Cloud, Service and support, Hardware, Other                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| For                   | Optional: an **application of the service**, a **system** one of its applications runs on, or – for a cloud line – a **platform** you may see; plus the **supplier** and the contract the line pays for (0.17, [SUPPLIERS.md](SUPPLIERS.md#contracts-and-the-notice-by-date); since 0.18 a contract covering the service – choosing another adds the service to it when you may change it, a contract you may not see shows as _a contract you may not see_ and is kept on save, [SUPPLIERS.md](SUPPLIERS.md#contracts-and-the-services-they-cover)), else free text for the vendor or contract |
| Amount, currency      | A decimal with its ISO 4217 code (checked against the ISO 4217 list, so a typo such as `EUX` is refused rather than opening a currency of its own), with no more decimals than the currency has (none for JPY)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Cadence               | **Yearly** (the year's amount, spread evenly over its months), **monthly** (every month), **quarterly** (every third month, 0.18), **once** (in one month of the year)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Only part of the year | Monthly, quarterly and yearly lines (0.18): a **first and a last month** in the fiscal year – a licence bought in March, the last months of a contract. Monthly: the amount in each month from–to; quarterly: every third month from the first month, up to the last; yearly: the amount is what the line costs in those months, spread evenly over them. None: the whole year                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Covers                | One-off lines (0.18): optionally the days a **prepaid** payment covers (_Covers 1 Feb 2026 – 31 Jan 2029_) – counted in the fiscal year it is paid, see [Contract payments](#contract-payments-and-commitments)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Contract end/renewal  | Optional; shown under _Renewals in the next 90 days_                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Note                  | Free text                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

A yearly amount is spread in the currency's **minor units** (0.18): 1,000.00 EUR is 83.33 or 83.34 a month, 100,000 JPY whole yen – the months add up to the line's amount exactly. A part-year line keeps its months as positions in the fiscal year (the fourth to the ninth month), like the month of a one-off payment, so changing the fiscal-year start keeps them in place.

**Cost entries** – a date, an amount (negative for a credit note) and a currency, optionally a plan line of the same fiscal year and currency (without one the entry is **unplanned**), an application or system of the service, a **supplier and contract** (0.18; on a plan line they default to the line's – a supplier you may not see is shown as _a supplier you may not see_ and kept on save, an archived one cannot be chosen again), a **reference** (invoice or order number) and a note. At most 2,000 per service and fiscal year – also when an entry is moved into a year by changing its date.

**Book** (0.18, German _Buchen_): the receipt button of a monthly, quarterly or one-off line (not a yearly line – its months are a spread, not payments) opens a prefilled entry: the **month** (the current one, or an earlier month of the year without an entry on the line), the **date** (the payment date of a [contract line](#contract-payments-and-commitments), else today for the current month and the month's last day for an earlier one), the **planned amount** and currency, the line, its supplier, contract and target; reference and note are optional; Enter saves. A month that has an entry on the line already asks first (_Already booked for October: 1,000.00 EUR – book again?_); a month still to come cannot be booked. The plan marks the payments of the months that are over and of the current month with no entry on their line as **Not booked**. Steward never books on its own: an entry says money was spent, and that is a person's statement. Instead, an **optional reminder** (event `cost.booking_due`, **off by default**, turned on per person under _Profile → Notifications_): on the third day of a month, the owners of a service who turned it on get the contract payments of the previous month that have no entry yet – one message per service, at most once a month ([NOTIFICATIONS.md](NOTIFICATIONS.md#events)). Booking needs a signed-in session (`POST /api/costs/plans/:serviceId/lines/:lineId/book`) and is audited as `cost.entry_added` with `via: "book"`.

**The year view**, per currency (amounts are never converted) and per line:

- **Planned (year)** and **planned to date** (up to and including the current month);
- **incurred to date**: entries dated up to today – and, for a matched cloud line, the costs read (today and the current month are those of the instance's time zone, `TZ`, so an entry dated today counts from local midnight);
- **expected (year)**: for a _monthly_ or _quarterly_ line month by month (a part-year line plans nothing outside its months; an entry booked there still counts) – the months that are over as incurred, the current month and those to come as booked if something is booked for them, else as planned; for a _yearly_ or _one-off_ line at least the plan (what is booked counts against it – an annual invoice paid early is not expected again for the remaining months);
- the **difference** (expected − planned): with a sign, in a quiet orange when more is expected than planned;
- a **month chart** of planned and incurred with a table twin, the lines **by category** or **by application or system**, **renewals** in the next 90 days and the **unplanned** entries. On a phone the lines and entries are cards.

**The costs read.** The cloud and platform costs the cost model already links to the service's applications appear as **cloud and platform (read)** per month. A plan line of category **Cloud** for an **application** or a **platform** takes those costs: the months that are over count as incurred, the current month with its [forecast](#what-people-see), the months to come as planned. Each cost belongs to one line at most – the oldest line for its application and currency, else the oldest for its platform and currency (a platform: the share of the service's applications); a second line for the same shows _Costs read: other line_ and counts only its entries. What no line takes stays in the row _cloud and platform (read)_, expected at the current month's forecast for the months to come. A line for a system is not matched.

**Copy to the next fiscal year** copies every line (refused when the next year already has lines that were not made from a [payment schedule](#contract-payments-and-commitments)); a renewal date before the new year moves on by a year, a one-off payment keeps its month in the year and a part-year line its months; entries are not copied. With contracts (0.18): a line made from a payment schedule is not copied – the schedule's proposal for the new year is added instead (when it has payments there and is not planned there yet); a line that names a contract without a schedule (0.17 style) is left out when the contract ends before the new year, copied up to the end's month (monthly and quarterly lines) or copied and marked _Contract ends …_ (yearly and one-off lines) when it ends in the new year. The message lists what was copied, proposed, shortened and left out (also the audit entry).

**Who:** who may change the service (its editors, not while it is archived) changes its plan and entries; who may see the service reads them. Every change is audited – `cost.plan_line_added`, `cost.plan_line_changed`, `cost.plan_line_removed` (its entries stay, as unplanned), `cost.plan_copied`, `cost.entry_added`, `cost.entry_changed`, `cost.entry_removed`. **No step-up**: unlike raising a budget (which notices and the scorecard measure against), a plan commits nothing, notifies nobody and stops nothing – it is the team's own planning, often changed line by line in one session; the audit trail records each change. Amounts follow _Hide amounts from viewers_. **The costs read follow the rule of the module**: a reader who does not see every application of the service gets no cloud figures – only a note that costs were read – and matched lines show their entries only.

**Team rollup:** a team's costs page (_Costs → Teams → the team_) sums the plans of the team's services per fiscal year and currency – the services that are not archived and have plan lines or cost entries in that year (the same services as the dataset and the scheduled export). A service of two teams counts for both (marked _Shared with another team_), so the teams' sums are not added up.

**Elsewhere:** scheduled exports write every service's plan lines and entries of a fiscal year as CSV to S3 ([EXPORTS.md](EXPORTS.md)); `GET /api/v1/services/{serviceId}/cost-plan?fiscalYear=` ([API.md](API.md); not paged – the limits of 200 lines and 2,000 entries per service and fiscal year bound the answer, also that of the MCP tool); the built-in dataset **Cost plans** (`costs.plans`: one row per service and currency of the current fiscal year – services not archived with plan lines or entries in it – with planned, to date, incurred, expected, difference and unplanned; status _warning_ when more is expected than planned; administrators and auditors, published at most hourly); the MCP tool `get_service_cost_plan` ([MCP.md](MCP.md)); internal routes `GET /api/costs/plans/:serviceId?fiscalYear=`, `GET …/export?fiscalYear=&what=plan|entries`, `POST …/lines`, `PUT|DELETE …/lines/:lineId`, `POST …/copy`, `POST …/entries`, `PUT|DELETE …/entries/:entryId`, `GET /api/costs/team-plans/:teamId?fiscalYear=`.

### Importing entries (CSV)

_Import entries (CSV)_ on the cost plan (German _Kostenbuchungen importieren (CSV)_), for invoice lists and ERP exports – who may change the service, in a signed-in session:

1. **Upload** a CSV file: UTF-8 (a byte-order mark is fine), separated by commas, semicolons or tabs (detected as for FOCUS files), with a header row. At most 5 MB, 5,000 rows and 100 columns.
2. **Map the columns**: date, amount, currency (or one currency for the whole file), reference, note; optionally the plan line (title or id), the supplier (name or a spelling), the contract (title or number) and the application or system (name). The **date format** is chosen, never guessed – `YYYY-MM-DD`, `DD.MM.YYYY`, `DD/MM/YYYY` or `MM/DD/YYYY` – and so is the **decimal separator** (`.` or `,`; the other one is read as a thousands separator). The service's last mapping is offered again.
3. **Preview**: every row with what it becomes – the fiscal year, the plan line (named, or the only line of the named contract in that year and currency, else _unplanned_), the supplier and contract – and its **problems**: no or impossible date, not a number, more decimals than the currency has, an unknown currency, a plan line of another year or currency, a supplier, contract or application that is unknown or that you may not see, over the limit of 2,000 entries per service and fiscal year, a **duplicate**. The preview stores nothing; the server reads the file again on import. A file with the same SHA-256 imported before says so.
4. **Import** the rows without problems (duplicates are left out unless ticked one by one) – in one transaction, all or nothing. The import is kept as a record (file name, SHA-256, rows, the mapping, who and when); its entries carry it, and **Remove import** removes those of its entries that are still there, in one step. 100 records are kept per service (older ones keep their entries and lose _Remove import_).

**Duplicates:** a row is a duplicate when the service has an entry – or an earlier row of the file has one – with the same **reference** (letter case and surrounding spaces aside, not empty), **date**, **amount** and **currency**. A row without a reference cannot be checked and is marked _No reference_, not left out. Routes: `POST /api/costs/plans/:serviceId/entries/import/preview`, `POST …/entries/import`, `GET …/imports`, `DELETE …/imports/:importId`; audited once per import as `cost.entries_imported` (file name, SHA-256, mapping, rows read, added, skipped by reason, sums per currency) and `cost.import_removed`. `examples/costs/entries-import.csv` is an invented invoice list to try it with.

## Contract payments and commitments

Since 0.18.0 a supplier contract ([SUPPLIERS.md](SUPPLIERS.md#contracts-and-the-notice-by-date)) can carry **payment schedules**; from them Steward proposes the plan lines of each fiscal year the contract covers, a person confirms them, and Steward shows what is still owed ([ADR 0030](adr/0030-contract-payments-and-recorded-costs.md)). Lightweight, as the rest of the plan: no approvals, purchase orders, invoice processing, chargeback, amortisation or currency conversion – Steward is not the system of record for finance. All of it belongs to the Costs module; contracts stay core records of suppliers.

### Payment schedules

_Suppliers → the supplier → a contract → Payments_ (German _Zahlungsplan_), or _Add payments_ in a service's [Contracts](#commitments) section. Up to 20 per contract and 200 per paying service:

| Field                    | What                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Title                    | e.g. _Base fee_, _Setup_, _Additional users_                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Paying service           | **One service** whose cost plan gets the lines. A contract paid by two services has a schedule per service, each with its own amount – no shares, no splitting                                                                                                           |
| Category, for            | As on plan lines (default _Service and support_); optionally an application or system of the paying service                                                                                                                                                              |
| Amount, currency         | The amount per payment in contract year 1 and its ISO 4217 currency – the currency of every line made from it (a contract billed in two currencies has two schedules)                                                                                                    |
| Billing                  | **Monthly**, **quarterly**, **yearly**, **once**, or **dated payments** (up to 120, each a date, an amount and optionally _covers until_)                                                                                                                                |
| First payment            | A date (default: the start); later payments fall on the same day every 1, 3 or 12 months – on the last day of a shorter month (the 31st stays the 31st after February)                                                                                                   |
| Start, planned until     | Default: the contract's start, and the end **follows the contract** – its end date; for an automatic renewal it runs on term by term. An own end date caps it; a contract with a fixed end (or renewed by agreement) is never passed. A schedule spans at most ten years |
| Amount per contract year | Optional amounts for contract years 2–10 (a year without one takes the year before). Contract years count from the start in twelve-month steps. _+ x % per year_ fills them in, each rounded to the currency's minor units; what is stored is what is shown              |
| Note                     | Free text                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

A payment counts in the **month of its date** – no pro rata for part months (a supplier that bills the first month pro rata is entered with dated payments). Steward computes payment dates and amounts from the schedule, nothing else.

**Who:** who may change the contract (since 0.18 its owners, designers, administrators, the supplier's owners who see it, editors of its covered services – [SUPPLIERS.md](SUPPLIERS.md#contracts-and-the-services-they-cover)), or who may edit the paying service and see the contract. The **paying service must be covered by the contract** (ADR 0031): a schedule for another service adds it to the contract when the person may change the contract, and is refused otherwise (`COST_SCHEDULE_SERVICE_NOT_COVERED`); a service that pays a schedule cannot leave the contract until the schedule is moved or removed. On the supplier page, on the contract and in _Costs → Commitments_ grouped by supplier, a schedule shows only when you may see the contract and its paying service. A schedule exists for a reader only through its paying service: lists, totals and the supplier page neither name nor count schedules of services the reader may not see; a reader who sees the service but not the supplier sees _a contract of a supplier you may not see_ – amounts, no supplier, contract title or dates. Amounts follow _Hide amounts from viewers_ of the paying service. Schedules are writes of people: a signed-in session (`POST /api/costs/schedules`, `PUT|DELETE /api/costs/schedules/:id`; `GET /api/costs/schedules?contractId=|serviceId=`), no step-up, audited on the service as `cost.schedule_added`, `cost.schedule_changed` (before and after) and `cost.schedule_removed`, naming supplier and contract. Removing a contract removes its schedules (the dialog names the paying services first, each removal is audited); the lines made from them keep their amounts and supplier and become ordinary lines. Merging suppliers moves contracts with their schedules; archiving a supplier keeps them – the money is still owed – but no new schedule is added to its contracts.

**Example.** Monthly 1,000.00 EUR from 1 February 2026 to 31 January 2029, +3 % per contract year, fiscal year from October: FY 2025/26 gets one monthly line February–September at 1,000.00; FY 2026/27 two lines, October–January at 1,000.00 and February–September at 1,030.00; FY 2027/28 October–January at 1,030.00 and February–September at 1,060.90; FY 2028/29 October–January at 1,060.90.

### Contract lines in the cost plan

- **Proposed, confirmed by a person, per fiscal year.** The cost plan of a year lists the payment schedules of the service with payments in that year and no lines for it yet (_Contract payments to plan_), with the lines each would add; _Add to plan_ adds the selected ones in one step (`POST /api/costs/plans/:serviceId/proposals`, audited as `cost.contract_lines_added`). Nothing is created without a person. Proposed lines count against the 200 plan lines per service and year – a proposal that would pass it is refused with the number.
- **What a proposal adds**, per schedule and year – one line per contract-year part, so a price change inside the year gives two lines: monthly billing a monthly line with its first and last month, quarterly a quarterly line, yearly a one-off line in the month of the payment, once a one-off line, dated payments a one-off line per payment. Title _contract – schedule_, the schedule's category and target, the supplier and the contract, the amount and currency. The line remembers its schedule and the months it was made from.
- **Kept in sync, never silently.** When the contract or the schedule changes later – an end date, a renewal, an amount, the fiscal-year start – the lines of the current and later fiscal years whose proposal changed show **Contract changed** (_Vertrag geändert_); the panel shows each line before and after, and **Update lines** (_Positionen aktualisieren_) applies the selected changes in one step (`POST …/contract-updates`, audited as `cost.contract_lines_updated` with before and after per line): a changed part changes its line, a part that is gone removes its line – its entries become unplanned, listed first – and a new part adds one. Fiscal years that are over are never offered: history stays as planned. A line changed by hand keeps its schedule; _Update lines_ shows the hand-made value as _before_. _Unlink from contract_ in the line's dialog makes it an ordinary line without hints.
- **Renewals** of a contract line are the contract's notice-by date and term end, not the line's own renewal field (hidden for contract lines).
- **Double planning**: a hand-made line for a contract that also has a schedule with payments in the year shows _Two lines for this contract_ – a hint, not a block. A 0.17-style line for a contract that ends in its year shows _Contract ends …_.
- **Book** takes the schedule's payment date in the month as its date ([Recording incurred costs](#planning-per-service)).

### Prepaid payments

A payment that covers more than its month – a yearly or one-off payment of a schedule, a dated payment with _covers until_, or any one-off line with **covers from/to** – is planned and counted **in the fiscal year it is paid**, with a note _Covers 1 Feb 2026 – 31 Jan 2029_. In the later fiscal years it covers, the cost plan shows it as a quiet row _Prepaid in FY 2025/26_ with its amount, not counted in planned, incurred or expected – so the team sees why nothing is planned there. No amortisation, no accruals: the plan shows cash by the year it leaves.

### Commitments

**What is owed** (German _Verpflichtungen_), per payment schedule, from its payments dated **today or later** (the instance's time zone):

- **to contract end**: until the schedule's end – its own end date, else the contract's end date; for a contract that renews automatically or has no end date, until the earliest exit (no later end is agreed);
- **to the earliest exit** (_frühester Ausstieg_): until the end of the term that follows the next notice-by date – for a contract with a fixed end or renewed by agreement its end date, for a contract without an end date today plus its notice period. Both are capped by the schedule's end.

Entries are not subtracted: a commitment is what the contract says is still to pay, not what is unbooked – scheduled payments, not liabilities. Both figures are shown per fiscal year and in total, per currency. With the example of [SUPPLIERS.md](SUPPLIERS.md#contracts-and-the-notice-by-date) (ends 31 January 2027, 90 days' notice, renews by 12 months), a schedule of 1,000.00 EUR on the first of each month until 31 January 2029 owes on 11 November 2026 26,000.00 EUR to contract end and 14,000.00 EUR to the earliest exit (31 January 2028, notice by 2 November 2027).

**The notice warning:** _Notice by 2 Nov 2027 – FY 2027/28 not planned yet_ when an automatically renewing contract's next notice-by date is ahead and the fiscal year in which its next term would start has no plan lines of the paying service – once that date passes, the next term is owed whether planned or not. Orange within 90 days, like the supplier's notice state.

**Where:**

- **The service's cost plan**, section _Contracts_: the contracts the service pays, per schedule the billing, the next payment, owed this fiscal year (remaining), later years, to contract end and to the earliest exit, the notice-by date with its warning, _Contract changed_ and the months _Not booked_. _Add payments_ opens the schedule dialog with the service filled in (choose the supplier and the contract).
- **Costs → Commitments** (`/costs/commitments`, beside services, teams and platforms): every schedule of the services you may see, grouped by supplier, service or team, columns for the current and the next four fiscal years and _later_, totals per currency, the warnings first; filters team, service, supplier and _ending within … days_; _Export (CSV)_.
- **The supplier page**: per contract its schedules and what they owe (services you may see), the supplier's total per currency, and what was incurred per fiscal year from entries that name the supplier – as sums; entries are not listed under _Used by_.

Schedules of services the reader may not see are neither named nor counted – also in the totals, which add only the amounts the reader sees. `GET /api/costs/commitments?teamId=&serviceId=&supplierId=&currency=&endingWithinDays=` and `…/export` (CSV); `GET /api/v1/cost-commitments` (paged; filters `serviceId`, `teamId`, `supplierId`, `currency`) and the commitments in `GET /api/v1/services/{serviceId}/cost-plan` ([API.md](API.md)); `paymentSchedules` per contract in `GET /api/v1/suppliers/{supplierId}`; the MCP tool `list_cost_commitments` ([MCP.md](MCP.md)); the built-in dataset **Commitments** (`costs.commitments`: one row per schedule of a service that is not archived, with this and next fiscal year, to contract end, to the earliest exit and the notice-by date, status _warning_ while the notice warning applies; administrators and auditors, published at most hourly); and `commitments.csv` in the scheduled export part _Cost plans_ ([EXPORTS.md](EXPORTS.md), formulas defused). The ISMS summary stays as it is: it lists notice-by dates already, and amounts do not belong in an ISMS report.

## Who sees costs

- A scope is only reachable for those who may see it: a hidden service, application, team or platform answers like a missing one; lists, breakdowns, reports and the check neither name nor count it. A service or team covers only the applications you may see – one you may not see is left out of its totals; a platform's total is its bill, so there the applications (or, for an application, the platforms) you may not see are one row _Others you may not see_ without a name; sources you may not see are left out. Budgets always use the whole service or team – so a reader who does not see all of it gets only the budget's state, never its figures ([Budgets](#budgets)); the totals never reveal hidden applications by subtraction.
- **Amounts**: administrators and auditors; the named **owners** and the members of the **owning teams** of a service or application; the members of a team; who may manage a platform or is a member of its owning teams. Everybody else who may see the scope sees the amounts too – unless an administrator turns on **Hide amounts from viewers** (_Costs → Settings_): then they see percentages and budget states only. Changing the setting is audited (`cost.settings_changed`).
- Viewers see no platforms (as everywhere); auditors see every platform's costs.

## Decisions

The open questions of #226, decided for 0.9.0:

- **Costs without a tag or link are shown as _unassigned_ per platform** – not left out: a platform's total is its bill, and what nobody claims is visible where it can be fixed (a tag, a component, the platform's application).
- **No currency conversion in this step.** Totals are per currency; a later step may add fixed monthly rates set by an administrator.
