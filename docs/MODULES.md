# Modules and maturity

Steward has a core and optional areas, the **modules**. An administrator switches a module off in _Settings → Modules_. This page lists the modules, what switching one off does, and how mature each area and each connector is. The decision behind it: [ADR 0016](adr/0016-module-registry.md).

## Switching a module off

- Everything is on in a new installation and after an upgrade. Only the plant registry and its map are off until an administrator turns them on.
- Switched off, a module is gone for everyone: its sidebar entries, tabs and pages, its settings, its connectors in _Add platform_, its search results and its Home tiles. Its pages say _This area is switched off_; administrators get a link to _Settings → Modules_. To keep a module from some people only, restrict who uses it instead (below).
- Its routes answer `404` with the code `MODULE_OFF` (`PLANT_MODULE_OFF` for the plant registry), in the internal API and in the public API (`/api/v1`).
- Its background jobs do not run. _System status_ shows them as _Module switched off_.
- Its data stays. Switched on again, everything is back as it was.
- Switching needs an administrator in a signed-in session and a fresh confirmation (step-up, control point `modules.switch` in the [assurance policy](ASSURANCE.md)). Every switch is in the audit log (`module.enabled`, `module.disabled`).
- The plant registry is switched in its own card on the same page ([PLANT.md](PLANT.md)); the map of sites is on while the registry is on and a map source is chosen.

API: `GET /api/settings/modules` lists the modules, `PUT /api/settings/modules/:id` with `{ "enabled": false }` switches one. Both are for administrators; the switch needs a session (no API token).

## Who may use a module

A module that is on is not needed by everyone. Since 0.21.0 an administrator decides per module who uses it ([ADR 0034](adr/0034-module-access.md)):

- **Everyone signed in** – the default for every module, new ones included. An upgrade changes nothing.
- **Selected teams and users** – the module is _restricted_: only the users and teams on its list use it (a team: its members). A single sign-on group reaches a module through the team it is mapped to.
- **Administrators** always use every module; **auditors** always, as viewers – evidence must not depend on who was given a module.
- The **status pages** cannot be restricted (they publish), and the **map of sites** follows the plant registry. Every other module can be restricted; a new module declares it in `shared/src/modules.ts` (`access`).

Someone who does not use a module meets it like a switched-off one, with a code of its own:

- Its sidebar entries, tabs, pages, Home tiles, connectors in _Add platform_, built-in datasets and search results are not shown. Its pages say _You do not use this module_ – _Ask an administrator if you need it._ `GET /api/auth/status` says per module `on`, `off` or `notYours` (`capabilities.moduleAccess`).
- Its routes answer `404` with the code `MODULE_NO_ACCESS` (`{ module }`), in the internal API and in the public API (`/api/v1`) – decided by the matched route, after authentication and before a step-up or the route's own code.
- **MCP:** its tools are not listed and a call is refused. A token acts with its owner's module access, checked on every request.
- **Notifications:** its events (for example `cost.budget`, `plant.change_proposed`) are not sent to them, in no channel; held messages are checked again when quiet hours or a digest release them. A team's own channel gets them only while the team uses the module. Addresses outside Steward count as not using a restricted module.
- **Reports:** a section of a module the schedule's owner does not use is left out. A recipient who does not use a restricted module gets a copy of the report without its sections (one more report per run at most); the full report is not theirs to open. The schedule form says which recipients are affected – named users, teams that are not on the module's list and e-mail addresses outside Steward – before you save (`POST /api/report-schedules/recipients-preview`).
- **Core pages** leave out the module's parts: a service's _Costs_, _Scorecard_ and _Responsibilities_ tabs, a platform's or an application's costs and contracts, the module's check types in the monitor editor. A monitor check of the module (cost change, data contract, version behind, page watch) keeps running – it is the monitor's – but shows them only its status: _A check of a module you do not use_, no message, no value, in the monitor's page, its results and its history, and in the monitor's notifications to them (also the monitor's own message when that check is its root cause).
- **Links** from a core item they may see to an item of the module are counted, not named: _An item of a module you do not use_ – no title, id, link or note. Saving the core item keeps the link; they cannot add one, and the link picker does not offer the module's items.
- **Export parts** of the module are not offered to them; scheduled exports and backups run as the instance, as before.
- Its background jobs keep running: they are the instance's.

### Changing who uses a module

_Settings → Modules_ shows each module that can be restricted with its access (_Used by: Everyone signed in_ or _Selected teams and users_). **Who may use it** opens the dialog:

1. Choose **Everyone signed in** or **Selected teams and users**, and pick the teams and users. A team means its members; a single sign-on group reaches the module through the team it is mapped to ([TEAMS.md](TEAMS.md#modules-through-teams)).
2. Before you save, the dialog shows **who gains** and **who loses** the module, and the **teams that own items of the module but would not use it** (their members keep their rights on those items but cannot open them).
3. **Save.** Widening – opening a restricted module, or adding a team or user – asks you to confirm that it is you (step-up, control point `access.grant`). Narrowing asks no step-up, but the dialog asks you to confirm it once more, with the list of who loses the module. The sidebar, Home and the search follow at once.

Changing who uses a module needs an administrator in a signed-in session (no API token). Every change is an access change in the audit log (`access.module_access_changed`, with the mode before and after, who was granted and who removed; a widening carries the step-up's proof). A deleted team stays on the list as an entry that matches nobody, so deleting a team never widens a restricted module – and a new team that takes its name does not inherit it ([TEAMS.md](TEAMS.md)).

### Reviewing who uses a module

- **Why do I have access?** on a site of the plant registry or a data contract starts with the module layer: _Uses Plant registry through team Invented plant engineering; module role Manager (through team Invented plant engineering)_, or _Does not use Data contracts_ – without the module, the item's rights do not apply. A granted module role is a reason of its own (_Module role Manager of team …_).
- **Person access reviews** of a Steward user list the restricted modules and whether the person uses each, and through what, and the module roles granted to them (0.23.0) – read when the review is opened, since module access is changed in Steward, not on a platform ([COMPLIANCE.md](COMPLIANCE.md)).
- The **assurance report** has a section _Module access_: who uses each restricted module at the end of the period, and every change in it with who made it and how it was confirmed ([ASSURANCE.md](ASSURANCE.md)).
- **Evidence packs** of auditors and administrators hold `access/module-access.json` with the same data for the pack's period.
- The **test sign-in** of a single sign-on provider shows the restricted modules a person would use and the module roles the mappings give ([SSO.md](SSO.md#test-sign-in)).

API (administrators, signed-in session): `GET /api/settings/modules/:id/access`, `PUT /api/settings/modules/:id/access` with `{ "mode": "selected", "grants": [{ "subject": "team:plant engineering" }] }` (or `{ "mode": "everyone", "grants": [] }`), and `POST /api/settings/modules/:id/access/preview` with the same body – who would gain and who would lose the module, and which teams own items of it but would not use it. `GET /api/settings/modules` names each module's `accessMode` and the module `roles` it declares.

## Module roles

Since 0.23.0 a module that owns items or settings of its own has **module roles** ([ADR 0034](adr/0034-module-access.md) part 4): **viewer**, **contributor** and **manager** (German _Leser_, _Mitwirkender_, _Verwalter_). Each includes the lower ones. A module may declare fewer; a module whose data follows core items (scorecard, version watch …) declares none.

| Module                         | Viewer                     | Contributor                                    | Manager                                                                                      | Core roles by default                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Plant registry (`plant`)       | sees sites and catalogues  | also proposes changes (drafts and change sets) | also manages sites, adds sites, manages the catalogues; the registry's content settings (\*) | designer → manager, operator and approver → contributor, auditor and viewer → viewer                              |
| Costs (`costs`)                | –                          | –                                              | the costs managers: instance price books, instance calculators, exchange rates               | designer → manager                                                                                                |
| Data products (`dataProducts`) | sees products and elements | also creates products and data objects         | also changes and manages them, domains, layers                                               | designer → manager, every other working role → viewer (team roles included, [DATA-PRODUCTS.md](DATA-PRODUCTS.md)) |

**Where a person's module role comes from – the highest of:**

- their **core roles**, through what the module declares (`fromCoreRoles` in `shared/src/modules.ts`). These are exactly the rules before 0.23.0, so **an upgrade changes nobody's rights** – the tests compare every user's rights on every item before and after;
- a module role **given to them**: in the user form (_Users → Edit → Module roles_) or in the module's access dialog;
- a module role **given to a team they are in** (the module's access dialog). It applies to the team's members across the whole module, like an instance role – it names people, not the team's items;
- a module role given **by single sign-on**: a mapping of the provider gives it, synchronised at each sign-in like roles ([SSO.md](SSO.md#groups-and-roles)).

A module role is the default of the module's kinds, like an instance role: on items without owning teams (and not _Restricted_ or limited by an access list) it gives its rights, on items of owning teams only to their members, and shares add as always ([TEAMS.md](TEAMS.md)). On a **restricted** module a grant with a role also means "uses it"; taking the role away keeps the person on the list. Someone who does not use a module has no module role in it.

**Giving module roles.** _Settings → Modules → <module> → Who may use it_ shows, for a module with roles, a role per team and user – _From core roles_ (on a restricted module: uses it, with the role their core roles give), _Viewer_, _Contributor_ or _Manager_. Open to everyone, the list holds the teams and users with a module role. Before you save, the preview names **whose module role changes** (_Vera Viewer: Viewer → Manager_) besides who gains and who loses the module. Giving a role or a higher one widens access: it asks for the step-up `grantAccess` (control point `access.grant`); lowering or removing one is confirmed in the dialog. Grants that single sign-on keeps are listed but not changed here. Every change is an access change in the audit log (`access.module_access_changed` with `roles: [{ subject, before, after }]`, and `via` `userForm`, `sso` or `roleAlias` when it was not made in this dialog); the assurance report's section _Module access_ and the evidence packs' `access/module-access.json` list the module roles and their changes. Changing module roles needs a signed-in session (API tokens get `SESSION_REQUIRED`).

**Module settings (manager settings).** A module may declare settings as content that its managers change (ADR 0034 decision 11): the plant registry's enterprise name, display names, own fields and review intervals – \* _Plant registry → Sites → Registry settings_ (`GET`/`PUT /api/plant/settings`, signed-in session). Only a **granted** manager role counts here, not one that comes from core roles, so designers gained no settings at the upgrade. Switching the module, who uses it, the map, connectors with credentials, retention and everything behind a step-up stay with administrators (`403 MODULE_SETTING_ADMIN_ONLY`).

**Module-only users.** Someone whose only grants are module roles – no core role that works in Steward, none in a team – reaches only those modules' routes, their profile and `/api/v1/me`; every other route answers `403` (`PLANT_VIEWER_ONLY` for the plant registry, `MODULE_ONLY_USER` for other modules), and MCP clients are not for them. A status role adds the status pages. This is what the plant viewer was since 0.12.

**The former role _Plant viewer_.** The instance role `plant` became the plant registry's **contributor** grant: at the first start of 0.23.0 every account with the role gets the grant (an account of a single sign-on provider that synchronises roles: as a synchronised grant), each audited (`via: roleAlias`), and the role is no longer stored or offered. For one release the API still accepts `roles: ["plant"]` in `POST`/`PUT /api/users` and in single sign-on mappings and turns it into the grant (the answer carries a `Deprecation` header); an access-list entry or share for `role:plant` still names everyone granted the plant registry's contributor role or more. The alias goes in a later release.

API: `PUT /api/settings/modules/:id/access` takes `{ "subject": "team:plant engineering", "role": "manager" }` (`role` null or left out: uses it); the preview answers `roleChanges`. `POST`/`PUT /api/users` take `moduleRoles: [{ "module": "plant", "role": "contributor" }]` (`null` takes it away) and `GET /api/users` lists each person's own `moduleRoles`. `GET /api/auth/status` says per module the person's `moduleRoles` and the modules whose manager settings they change (`moduleSettings`). `GET /api/access/explain` gives the reason `moduleRole` and, in `module`, the person's `role` and where it comes from (`roleThrough`).

## Maturity

- **stable** – used and tested broadly; changes are announced in the change log.
- **beta** – complete, but little used so far. Expect rough edges.
- **preview** – new; it may still change in ways that need you to act.

For connectors, _verified against_ says what the tests talk to: a **real** system of that product, only Steward's own **mock** of it (`server/src/mock/`), or **none** (the connector talks to no product of its own). A connector tested only against a mock is at most beta. Connectors and areas added in 0.9.0 or later are preview. _Settings → Modules_, _Add platform_ and the platform page show the label; the tooltip says what it means.

## What a person sees

The sidebar shows an area only when the person may see something there or may add something. The server decides this with the same rights as the pages (`GET /api/me/visibility`). An empty list says _Nothing here you may see_ when entries exist that the person has no access to; it never says how many or which. A dashboard widget whose data the person may not read says so instead of _No data yet_.
