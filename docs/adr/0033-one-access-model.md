# 0033. One access model: owning teams and shares on every resource

- **Status:** Accepted – the owner accepted every recommendation under _Owner decisions_ (2026-11-12); built in 0.21.0 (phase 1: shares, the _Access_ tab, owners as implied Edit, _Restricted_; services, applications, systems, suppliers, device profiles and KPI definitions) and 0.23.0 (phase 2: the access lists of processes, platforms with their identities, monitors and plant sites migrated to shares; records, credentials, ticket references, dashboards and data contracts with shares; corrective action findings in team scope; auditors' View on platforms – see _Built in 0.23.0_ below)
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Since:** 0.21.0 at the earliest (phase 1, see _Phases_)

## Context

The owner asked three questions: device profiles and KPI definitions have no access restriction while sites and assets have one – why? Can something a team owns also be shared with another team, or with a user outside it? And is the permission concept consistent?

Since 0.3.0 one module decides every right on a resource and says why ([ADR 0006](0006-owning-teams-policy-module.md), `server/src/auth/policy.ts`): a resource's **owning teams** are its scope (`resource_teams`), **roles** say what someone may do – in the instance or in a team – and rights only add up. What grew around that module since is not one model. Four kinds have an **access list** with levels of their own, ten fields name **owners or responsibles** who get see-and-change (step-up since 0.19.0, #195), supplier contracts have a **scope of their own** ([ADR 0031](0031-supplier-contracts-and-contacts-scope.md)), and several kinds cannot be shared at all. The matrix below is what the code on `main` (0.19.0) does, checked in the modules named; where the docs say otherwise, the code is described and the difference is listed under _Inconsistencies_.

### Kinds that can have owning teams

`OWNED_KINDS` in `shared/src/api.ts`; the owning-teams panel, `GET /api/access/explain` (_Why do I have access?_) and `POST /api/owning-teams/bulk` work for all of them. Changing owning teams is for administrators: adding a team, or removing the last one, asks for the step-up `grantAccess` (control point `access.grant`) and is audited as `access.owning_teams_changed`, also per resource in a bulk change.

### How the interfaces apply it

- **Web app and internal API:** a hidden item answers `404` like a missing one; lists are filtered row by row in the service through the policy functions, with request-scoped indexes (`teamIndex`, memberships, `withServiceLinks`, `siteRights`, `processPermissionMap`) so a list costs a few queries, not one per row (security review 0.19.0 H2). Unique names answer neutrally (`SERVICE_NAME_TAKEN` …).
- **`/api/v1`** (read-only) and **MCP** call the same service functions with the token owner's rights; a plant viewer reaches only `/api/v1/plant/…`, and MCP not at all.
- **Exports by a person** (CSV, YAML, the plant export, evidence packs) carry what the person may see. **Scheduled S3 exports** run as the system – administrators only.
- **Search:** the command palette searches the lists the app loads, each already scoped; link pickers and the plant's _Explore data_ offer only visible items.
- **Notifications, daily summary, reports:** recipients are filtered by `canSeeResource` when a message is sent and again when a held message is released; report sections and the start page count only what the reader may see.

### Inconsistencies

1. **One access list, two meanings.** On a resource without owning teams an entry _restricts_ (only the entries, administrators, the owner – and auditors except on platforms); with owning teams the same entry _adds_ an exception. Removing the last entry of an unowned resource reopens it to every role's defaults.
2. **Four ways to share across teams, none of them everywhere.** Access lists (processes, platforms, monitors, sites), named owners and responsibles (catalogue, suppliers, contracts, records, credentials, ticket references, duties, measures, corrective actions), contract owners and covered platforms (suppliers) – and nothing at all for dashboards, data contracts, device profiles and KPI definitions.
3. **No read-only share outside the four access-list kinds.** Naming an owner always gives see _and_ change; a team that wants another team to _read_ its service, record or credential has to make it an owner or ask an administrator for a team. An owner is also a responsibility (reminders, notice dates, four-eyes) – two concerns in one field.
4. **Six vocabularies of levels:** `view/run/edit/approve/manage`, `use/manage` (no `view`), `view/acknowledge/edit/manage`, `view/propose/manage`, `view/test/edit/manage` (a setting called `manage`) and `view/edit/archive/delete` for credentials.
5. **Device profiles and KPI definitions cannot be restricted** while the sites and assets that use them can; every designer changes every team's profiles.
6. **Dashboards:** members of the owning teams only read them – a team's designer cannot change the team's dashboard, only its creator can – and nothing shares one outside the team.
7. **Auditors** "view everything" ([TEAMS.md](../TEAMS.md)) – except platforms.
8. **Who may change access** differs per kind: `manage` holders (designers on sites but not on processes), anyone who may edit the item (named owners), administrators only (owning teams, dashboards, data contracts).
9. **Step-ups differ:** sites ask on every change, processes, platforms, monitors and identities when a right is gained, named owners when outside the owning teams, owning teams when added or emptied.
10. **Seven audit actions** for one kind of event: `process.access_changed`, `platform.access_changed`, `platform.identity_access_changed`, `monitor.access_changed`, `site.access_changed`, `access.named_subjects_granted`, `access.owning_teams_changed`.
11. **Deleting a team widens access.** `resource_teams.team_id` is `ON DELETE CASCADE`: a resource whose only owning team is deleted belongs to the whole instance afterwards (`server/test/team-scopes.test.ts`, _are removed with their team_, expects it), without a preview or step-up – while [TEAMS.md](../TEAMS.md#setting-it-up) says that deleting a team never widens anyone's access.
12. **Corrective action findings** of a team-owned service are read by every instance designer and approver (the service's name is hidden from them, the finding is not).
13. **Publications outside the model:** status pages (every signed-in user, a link), report recipients (the owner's view to users, teams and e-mail addresses) and datasets (the publishing process's readers) pass data on without being shown in _Why do I have access?_. They are deliberate; they are not shares.

## Decision

**Owning teams stay the scope. Every top-level resource kind gets shares – a user, a team or a role, given View, Edit or Manage, with the kind's action rights beside the level – in one table, one API, one _Access_ tab and one _Why do I have access?_. Shares replace the per-kind access lists. Owners and responsibles become responsibility; an owner implies an Edit share and is shown as one. Device profiles, KPI definitions and dashboards get owning teams and shares. Widening access asks for the `access.grant` step-up and is audited as an access change; team scope applies on every surface.**

### 1. Scope and shares

- **Scope** – who a resource belongs to – is one of three: **the whole instance** (no owning teams: instance roles give their defaults, as today), **teams** (owning teams: their members have their instance and team roles), or **only its shares** (_Restricted_: no owning teams, and instance roles give nothing). _Restricted_ is what an access list on an unowned resource means today, made explicit: removing the last share no longer reopens the resource.
- **A share** gives one subject – `user:`, `team:` or `role:` – a **level** and, where the kind has them, **action rights**. Shares only add; there are no deny rules (ADR 0006). A share may have an **end date** (decision 4).
- **Rights** are the union of: administrator (everything); auditor (View); team roles in an owning team; instance roles when the scope is the whole instance; shares; implied shares of owners and responsibles (part 2). The policy module computes them as today, with two new reasons, `share` and `implied`.

| Kind                           | View                                                | Edit                                          | Manage (Edit, and:)                              | Action rights beside the level              |

Children keep following their parent (versions, runs, schedules, components, editions, assets, assignments, collections …); a child has no shares. Platform identities keep their own `use` right, managed on the platform's _Access_ tab. Supplier contracts keep ADR 0031's scope until phase 3.

**Manage by default** – without a share – goes to administrators and to whoever may grant today: the `manage` holders of processes, platforms, monitors and sites (owners, entries; designers on sites), and the editors who may name an owner from outside the owning teams today (designers among the members of catalogue items, suppliers and records; designers and operators on credentials; editors of ticket references). Dashboards: their owner. Device profiles, KPI definitions and data contracts: designers among the members (decision 13). A subject named from outside gets Edit, not Manage (decision 3).

### 2. Owners and responsibles become responsibility

- Owners, responsibles and the owners of duties, measures and corrective actions stay **fields** – they get reminders, notice dates and are kept from approving their own work. They are no longer a way to grant.
- Each implies a share – **Edit** for an owner or responsible of the item, the rights it gives today for duties, measures and actions – computed from the field when the item is read, never stored twice. The _Access_ tab lists it as _Edit – owner_ (_Bearbeiten – Eigentümer_), not removable there: removing the owner removes it.
- Naming an owner from **outside** the owning teams is a widening like a share (part 5): it needs Manage on the item and the step-up, and the form says so before saving (`POST /api/access/named-outside` stays). Naming a covered subject needs Edit, as today.
- A process's and a monitor's single owner keeps every right (implied Manage with all action rights), as today.

### 3. Device profiles, KPI definitions and dashboards

- **Device profiles and KPI definitions** become owned kinds (`device_profile`, `kpi_definition`) with owning teams and shares. Without owning teams they behave exactly as today – every reader of the registry sees them, designers and administrators manage them, proposers propose – so an upgrade changes nothing. New ones get no owning team, whoever creates them, like suppliers (decision 9).
- **Dashboards** keep their owning teams and get shares; the owner keeps Manage. Whether a designer among the members may change a team's dashboard by role is decision 11.
- **Data contracts** get shares; their `manage` right is renamed to the action right _relax the breaking-change rule_ (decision 12).

### 4. The plant catalogue when a reader cannot see a profile

The catalogues belong to no site, so for the first time an asset a reader sees can use a profile they may not see.

- **The asset keeps working.** Its assignment, checks, delivered data points, KPI coverage, data collections, data flow, lineage and live status are computed on the whole resolved model, as today. A result that would name an item of the hidden profile says _a data point of a device profile you may not see_ (_ein Datenpunkt eines Geräteprofils, das Sie nicht sehen dürfen_) with its state.
- **Counted, not named.** The profile is _a device profile you may not see_ – no id, name, manufacturer, model, link, bases or other assets. Lists, filters, facets, the coverage view and _Explore data_ count hidden profiles and KPI definitions (_and 2 profiles you may not see_), never name them; a profile page counts the assets of sites the reader may not see, as it does since 0.7.0.
- **What the asset shows of the model** is decision 10: the recommendation is that the interfaces and data points the asset delivers stay visible on the asset as its own model, marked _from a device profile you may not see_ – they are what the device delivers, and the person who assigned the profile saw it (as an order form shows the terms it inherits from a master it may not see, ADR 0031 part 3). The asset's own parts – endpoints, differences, publications, KPI instances, firmware – are the asset's and stay visible.
- **A derived profile with a hidden base** shows its inherited items as its own, marked _Inherited from a base profile you may not see_, without the base's name, id, documents or other derived profiles. Only who sees a base may extend it.
- **Files follow the profile.** The profile's documents (the _Documents_ panel, _Upload file_) and the files of its file-transfer interfaces are the profile's: an asset inherits them for readers who may see the profile; others see _1 document of a profile you may not see_. A document uploaded on a profile page gets the profile's owning teams.
- **KPI definitions** naming a hidden profile in an input show _a device profile you may not see_ and the data point ids the input stores; coverage counts only visible assets. A KPI instance whose definition is hidden shows _a KPI definition you may not see_; its checks still run.
- **Saving and importing:** a form or import that keeps a hidden profile or KPI definition on an asset keeps it; choosing one the person may not see answers like a missing one (`PLANT_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`); a new id taken by a hidden entry is refused neutrally (_This id cannot be used_). The export carries the visible catalogue only, and a _replace_ import never removes what the importer may not see. Restricting a profile shows a preview of who keeps seeing its model through assets and derived profiles.

### 5. Widening access: step-up and audit

- **Widening** is any change after which a subject has a right it did not have: a new share or a higher level, a new action right, an outside owner, an owning team added, the last owning team removed, _Restricted_ switched off, a longer end date. Narrowing never asks. One function decides it for every kind (`gainsRight` of `step-up-rules.ts`, generalised to scope, shares and implied shares), in the route's step-up rule and again in the service before anything is written (as `requireNamedGrant` does since 0.19.0).
- It asks for the step-up `grantAccess` (control point `access.grant`); API tokens get `SESSION_REQUIRED`. Sites ask only on widening from then on.
- **Nobody shares more than they hold:** a share may not carry a level or action right its author lacks (administrators excepted).
- **One audit family:** `access.shares_changed` (before, after, `granted`, `removed`, the step-up's proof), `access.scope_changed` (_Restricted_), `access.owning_teams_changed` and `access.named_subjects_granted` (as since 0.19.0), `access.share_expired` (system). The per-kind actions are no longer written; the assurance report and evidence packs read old and new entries.

### 6. Team scope everywhere

A share is one more reason in the policy module, so every surface that asks the module follows. What the rules already require stays, and holds for shared items as for owned ones: a reader never sees, counts or is told about what they may not see – in lists, counts, CSV and YAML exports, `/api/v1`, MCP tools, notifications and their release, the daily summary, report sections, evidence packs, the scorecard, backlinks, link pickers, search, unique-name refusals and _Used by_. The tests get one matrix: for each kind, a user who has only a share sees the item on every surface, and a user without it sees it on none (`team-scopes`, `route-matrix`, `api-v1`, `mcp`).

### 7. One table, one API, one UI

- **Data** (additive migrations in `server/src/migrations/teams.ts`): `resource_shares` (`kind`, `resource_id`, `subject` – the subject strings used everywhere else, so renames, aliases and migration packages work unchanged – `level`, `actions` JSON, `expires_at`, `created_by`, `created_at`; primary key `(kind, resource_id, subject)`, indexes on `subject` and `(kind, resource_id)`), `resource_restrictions` (`kind`, `resource_id`). Classified in `server/src/migration/tables.ts`; `resource_shares.subject` joins `USER_REFS` as kind `acl`, so a deleted team stays an entry that matches nobody.
- **API:** `GET /api/access/:kind/:id` (scope, owning teams, shares, implied shares, what the caller may change), `PUT /api/access/:kind/:id/shares`, `PUT /api/access/:kind/:id/scope`, `POST /api/access/:kind/:id/preview` (who gains and who loses, like the owning-teams preview), `GET /api/access/shared-with?subject=` (administrators and auditors: everything shared with a user or team – for leavers and access reviews). `GET /api/access/explain` and the owning-teams routes stay. `/api/v1` gains nothing in phase 1: share lists name people and are read in the app.
- **UI:** one _Access_ tab (_Zugriff_) on every kind: scope, owning teams, _Shared with_ (_Geteilt mit_ – not _Freigabe_, which means approval in Steward's German), implied shares, and _Why do I have access?_ (_Warum habe ich Zugriff?_), listing reasons as today plus _Shared with team Payments: View_ and _Owner: Edit_.
- **Code:** `server/src/auth/policy.ts` (`decide`, `catalogueAccess`, `recordAccess`, `teamScoped` read shares and restrictions), new `server/src/auth/shares.ts` (store, request index, widening check, audit), `server/src/auth/scope-routes.ts`, `server/src/auth/step-up-rules.ts`, `server/src/auth/named-grants.ts` (implied shares), `server/src/plant/access.ts` and `server/src/plant/catalogue.ts`, `server/src/dashboards/service.ts`, `shared/src/access.ts` (new: levels, action rights per kind, share types), web `web/src/components/access/AccessTab.tsx` (new), replacing the access-list editors of processes, platforms, monitors and sites.

## Migration

**Existing access lists and named owners become shares one to one: nobody gains or loses access at the upgrade.** The migration copies; it does not reinterpret.

| Kind                                                                         | Today                           | After the upgrade                                                                                                      | Why the rights are the same                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Process, monitor, site                                                       | `acl` JSON entries; owner       | one share per entry with **exactly its rights** (level and action rights); _Restricted_ when `acl` had entries         | without owning teams: _Restricted_ gives nothing by role, as a non-empty list did; with owning teams: shares add, as entries did; the flag is set for owned ones too, so removing their teams later behaves as today; owner unchanged |
| Platform, platform identity                                                  | `acl` entries (`use`, `manage`) | shares with the same rights; _Restricted_ as above; identities keep closed-by-default                                  | as above; auditors still get no right on platforms (decision 8 is a separate change)                                                                                                                                                  |
| Service, application, system, supplier, credential, ticket reference, record | named owners or responsibles    | **no data moves**: each implies the share it gave (see and change); credential owners still neither archive nor delete | the implied share is computed from the same field by the same rule                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Duty, measure, corrective action                                             | named owners                    | unchanged fields, shown as implied shares on their item                                                                | the same rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Dashboard, data contract                                                     | owning teams, owner             | no shares; owner implied Manage                                                                                        | owning teams and role rules unchanged                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Device profile, KPI definition                                               | no scope                        | no owning teams, no shares                                                                                             | unowned kinds without shares keep their role rules                                                                                                                                                                                    |

- Entries with **rights outside the presets** (a process entry with `run` but not `view`, a monitor entry with `manage` but not `edit`) keep exactly those rights; the tab shows them as _Custom_ until someone changes them. Empty entries (no rights) are dropped, as `setSiteAccess` drops them today. `role:` subjects stay (decision 7).
- **Proof:** the test suite builds an instance with every kind, owned and unowned, with and without lists, owners, `role:`, deleted-team and alias subjects, and compares `explain` for every user before the migration (the 0.20 functions, kept in the test) and after; the property test of `step-up-rules.ts` covers the widening check. At the upgrade a background self-check compares old and new rights of every active user on every migrated resource in batches and reports a difference under _Settings → Health_ (it changes nothing).
- **What does change** is who may grant, not who has access: Manage holders of kinds that could not be shared get the _Access_ tab; a subject named from outside gets Edit, not Manage (decision 3).
- **Additive:** the `acl` columns stay (no longer read once migrated); migration packages carry `resource_shares`; a package from an older instance has its `acl` columns turned into shares on import.
- **Deprecated aliases for one release:** `PUT /api/processes/:id/access`, `/platforms/:id/access`, `/platforms/:id/identities/:identityId/access`, `/monitors/:id/access` and `/plant/sites/:siteId/access` keep their bodies and write shares; responses keep `acl`, computed from the shares; each answers with a `Deprecation` header. `acl` in imported monitor and process files is read into shares.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep the per-kind lists and add lists to profiles, KPI definitions and dashboards.** The least work, but a seventh vocabulary, the two meanings of a list stay, and owners stay the only cross-team share for most kinds. Rejected.
2. **Roles only – more teams.** Sharing with one person would need a team per exception; teams multiply and stop meaning teams, and there is still no read-only exception. Rejected: teams stay the normal case, shares the exception.
3. **Full ABAC or ReBAC** – Zanzibar-style relation tuples (OpenFGA, SpiceDB, Ory Keto) or policies in Cedar or Rego (OPA). Expressive, but: a service or engine to run and secure (a new dependency, ADR 0002's single instance gains nothing from a distributed store); every resource and membership written twice, to the database and to the tuple store, in step with each transaction; Steward's lists filter rows in one process, while a tuple store answers list questions with `ListObjects`-style reverse lookups; _Why do I have access?_ would need expand APIs; policies in a second language for every reviewer. Steward's model already is a small relation model – resource → owning team → member with role, resource → share → subject, child → parent – kept in TypeScript in one module, on the same database and in the same transaction. Revisit when several services must share decisions, sharing crosses instances, or conditions beyond an end date are needed.
4. **Deny rules or negative shares.** Rejected, as in ADR 0006: rights only add up, which keeps the explanation honest.
5. **A share column on each table** (the `acl` JSON renamed). Rejected: one table gives one index, one audit, one mapping for migration packages, and one answer to _what is shared with Dana?_.
6. **Owners stored as share rows.** Rejected: two sources to keep in step; implied shares are computed from the field.

## Consequences

- One mental model and one tab: scope by teams, exceptions by shares, responsibility by owners. A read-only share exists everywhere; a leaver's or mover's review reads one table.
- Device profiles and KPI definitions can be kept to a team; the asset that uses one keeps working for everyone who sees the asset.
- The levels are coarser than some kinds' rights; action rights keep what matters (`use`, `run`, `approve`, `acknowledge`, `propose`, `test`), and migrated entries keep their exact rights.
- Every decision reads one more index; every surface must keep asking the policy module – a private check elsewhere stays a review finding (ADR 0006).
- The docs are rewritten around the model: [TEAMS.md](../TEAMS.md), [USERS.md](../USERS.md), [PLANT.md](../PLANT.md), the access sections of each kind's page.
- Owners still get access with their responsibility. Revisit if an owner without access is needed, or if lists must be filtered in SQL.

## Security notes

- **Widening is confirmed and audited** in one place (part 5); API tokens never widen; the service checks again before writing.
- **No escalation through sharing:** nobody shares a level or action right they do not hold; a share never widens route access – status viewers cannot be given shares, plant viewers only on plant kinds (`SHARE_SUBJECT_UNSUITABLE`).
- **End dates are checked when deciding**, not only by the job that removes expired shares.
- **Who sees the share list:** Manage holders, administrators and auditors; everyone else sees only their own reasons. The hint for outside subjects stays limited to who may change the item.
- **Deleting a team** (inconsistency 11) must not widen: a resource whose last owning team is deleted becomes _Restricted_ – its shares, owners, administrators and auditors – and the team's delete dialog lists such resources and asks for the step-up. This is a fix worth doing before phase 1.
- **Deleted teams and deactivated users** stay in shares as entries that match nobody, as in access lists today; _Shared with_ marks them, access reviews list them.
- Share subjects are personal data: pseudonymisation and migration packages reach `resource_shares` through `USER_REFS`.

## Performance

- Today a list decides row by row in TypeScript over indexes read once per request. Shares add one index of the same kind: the shares of the **requester's** subjects (`SELECT … FROM resource_shares WHERE subject IN (…)`, index on `subject`) and the restricted resources of the kinds in use – two queries per request, then a lookup per row. Implied shares come from columns the list reads anyway.
- Questions about one resource (previews, notification recipients, access reviews) read its shares by `(kind, resource_id)`.
- One bulk entry point – `rightsOf(user, kind, rows)` returning a map – for lists, counts, exports and pagination, so no surface falls back to a query per row.
- A performance test with 20 000 resources, 5 000 shares, 300 teams and 2 000 users: list endpoints of every kind stay within 10 % of the 0.20 times. No SQL-side filtering in the first phases – it would write the rule a second time; revisit only if that test fails.

## Phases

1. **0.21.0 – the model and the catalogues:** tables, `shares.ts`, scope _Restricted_, the request index, the widening check and the audit family, the _Access_ tab and _Why do I have access?_; device profiles and KPI definitions with owning teams, shares and part 4; services, applications, systems and suppliers with shares (owners shown as implied Edit); the team-deletion fix.
2. **0.22.0 – the rest:** access lists of processes, platforms (with identities), monitors and sites migrated with their aliases; records, credentials, ticket references, dashboards and data contracts get shares; duties, measures and corrective actions show implied shares; corrective action findings in team scope (decision 14).
3. **0.23.0 – clean-up:** aliases removed; supplier contracts' owners split into owning teams and shares, ADR 0031's other rules unchanged.

## Owner decisions (accepted as recommended)

1. **Level names.** _Recommendation:_ View, Edit, Manage (_Ansehen_, _Bearbeiten_, _Verwalten_); the list is _Shared with_ (_Geteilt mit_), not _Freigabe_.
2. **Whether `use` on platforms and `run`/`approve` on processes stay separate.** _Recommendation:_ yes, as action rights beside the level for every kind that has them (`use`, `run`, `approve`, `acknowledge`, `propose`, `test`) – using a production tenant is not editing it, and an operator who runs a process should not change it.
3. **Whether Edit may re-share.** _Recommendation:_ no – only Manage changes shares or names an owner from outside the owning teams. Editors who may name outside owners today get Manage by role, so they lose nothing; a subject named from outside gets Edit and can no longer name further outsiders.
4. **End dates of shares.** _Recommendation:_ optional, at most one year ahead; the Manage holders get a reminder 7 days before; an expired share stops at once and is removed and audited (`access.share_expired`); extending is a widening.
5. **Shares to users outside any team.** _Recommendation:_ allowed – the contact person in another department is the point; such users appear in access reviews like any other.
6. **Guests and people without an account.** _Recommendation:_ no guest role and no share links; a share needs an account (an SSO guest is an account). The status page link stays the only view without signing in.
7. **`role:` subjects.** _Recommendation:_ kept – migrated lists use them (`role:operator: run`); offered in the UI as _Everyone with the role …_ only on _Restricted_ resources.
8. **Auditors on platforms.** _Recommendation:_ give auditors View on every platform, as on every other kind – in its own change with a release note, not in the migration.
9. **New device profiles and KPI definitions.** _Recommendation:_ no owning team, whoever creates them, like suppliers – they describe device models every site may use; teams restrict them deliberately.
10. **A hidden profile on a visible asset.** _Recommendation:_ the asset shows the interfaces and data points it delivers as its own model, without naming the profile (part 4). The alternative – only counts (_3 interfaces, 120 data points of a profile you may not see_) – protects a profile's register map better but leaves the asset page empty for its own readers.
11. **Designers on team dashboards.** _Recommendation:_ a designer among the members of a dashboard's owning teams may edit it by role, like other team items – a widening of role defaults, released with a note.
12. **Data contracts' `manage` right.** _Recommendation:_ rename it to the action right _relax the breaking-change rule_ (approvers, as today), so Manage means the same everywhere; the API keeps `manage` as an alias for a release.
13. **Manage by default on kinds that could not be shared** (dashboards, data contracts, device profiles, KPI definitions). _Recommendation:_ the owner of a dashboard; designers among the members (or all designers, while unowned) for the others.
14. **Corrective action findings in team scope.** _Recommendation:_ yes in phase 2: a finding of a team-owned service is read by that service's readers, the named owners, auditors and administrators – no longer by every instance designer and approver.
15. **_Restricted_ on kinds without an access list today.** _Recommendation:_ allowed on every kind; set by administrators, and by Manage holders of processes, platforms, monitors and sites (who can restrict today); switching it off is a widening.
16. **Report recipients and status pages.** _Recommendation:_ stay publications, not shares; the report schedule form says which recipients may not see every item of the report (like the hint for outside owners), audited with the schedule, no step-up.
17. **How long the old access-list APIs live.** _Recommendation:_ one release after the migration (0.22.0 deprecated, removed in 0.23.0).

## Built in 0.23.0 (phase 2)

- **Access lists migrated** (`access-001-acl-shares`, `server/src/migrations/access.ts`, a list of its own after the tables it reads): one share per subject with exactly its rights, `role:admin` entries and entries without rights dropped, _Restricted_ where the list had entries (not identities). A share that no level and action rights express keeps its exact rights in `resource_shares.rights` (_Custom_, shown so on the tab; changing the share replaces it). The proof is `server/test/acl-shares-migration.test.ts`: every user's rights on every item of each kind, decided by the 0.22 rules (kept in the test, reading the `acl` columns) and by the policy module now, after the migration's own SQL – on SQLite and PostgreSQL – plus the conversion of an upgrade fixture (v0.19.0).
- **Deciding**: `processAccess`, `platformAccess`, `identityAccess`, `monitorAccess`, `siteAccess` and `platformDataVisible` read shares and _Restricted_ (`PROCESS_SHARE_MODEL`, `PLATFORM_SHARE_MODEL`, `IDENTITY_SHARE_MODEL`, `MONITOR_SHARE_MODEL`, `SITE_SHARE_MODEL` in `server/src/auth/policy.ts`); the `acl` they are still passed is ignored. Every surface that asks them follows (lists, counts, search, exports, `/api/v1`, MCP, notifications, reports); lists read shares once per kind and request.
- **Deviations from the table in part 1, for now:** platforms and sites offer **View and Manage** only – their `manage` right still covers what Edit would (configuration, entries) and splitting it is a change of each kind; an identity's share is View with `use`, and who manages the platform changes it (also without using the identity – as the list was). Platforms gained the right `view` (any right includes it).
- **Deprecated aliases** (`server/src/auth/acl-shares.ts`): the five `PUT …/access` routes keep their bodies, write shares and the scope through the shares core, decide the step-up as the lists did (role defaults an emptied list gives back count; sites ask only on widening now), answer with `Deprecation` and a successor `Link`, and are session-only; `acl` in responses is computed from the shares, for who manages the item. The owner of a process or monitor is changed there (`{ owner }` alone), audited as `access.owner_changed`.
- **UI**: the _Access_ tab replaces the access-list editors of processes, platforms, monitors and sites and the identity's list; processes and monitors show their owner above it.
- **Records, credentials, ticket references, dashboards** (`server/src/auth/item-shares.ts`, `teamScoped` and `recordAccess` with `ITEM_SHARE_MODEL`): View, Edit, Manage; Restricted keeps instance roles out (auditors still view); an owner or responsible implies Edit, a dashboard's owner Manage. Manage by role – the internal right `manage`, left out of the kinds' public `permissions` – goes to who named outside owners before (designers among the members of a record, designers and operators of a credential, a ticket reference's editors, a dashboard's owner); only Manage names an owner or responsible from outside the owning teams now (`mayGrant`), and deleting a dashboard needs Manage. **Decision 11**: a designer among the members of a dashboard's owning teams edits it.
- **Data contracts** (`server/src/datacontracts/shares.ts`): **decision 12** – the former `manage` is the right `relax` (an action right of a share, _relax the breaking-change rule_), still listed as `manage` in `permissions` for one release; Manage (the internal right `share`) goes to designers among the members, all designers while it has no owning teams (decision 13).
- **Decision 14**: a corrective action finding of a service with owning teams or Restricted is read by the service's readers (members, shares, owners), the finding's and actions' owners, the named contract's owners, auditors and administrators – no longer by every instance designer and approver; designers outside the service's editors no longer change it.
- **Decision 8**: auditors view every platform (`DEFAULT_PLATFORM.auditor`, and `view` as the auditor right of `platformAccess` for team-owned and Restricted ones); reading a platform's access change entries still needs `use` or `manage` (or the auditor role).
- **Performance**: a request reads the shares of the reader's subjects for every kind in one statement and the Restricted resources in another (`share-store.ts`); lists of every kind stay at a fixed number of statements (statement-count tests in `acl-shares-migration.test.ts`, `item-shares.test.ts`, `hot-pages-statements.test.ts`). A renamed plant site keeps its shares and scope (`plant/rename.ts`).
- **Not yet built**: the implied shares of duty, measure and corrective-action owners on their parent's _Access_ tab (their rights are not levels – view a finding, mark a measure done – so they need an entry type of their own); the background self-check under _Settings → Health_; splitting Edit from Manage on platforms and sites.
