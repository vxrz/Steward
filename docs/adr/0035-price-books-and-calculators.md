# 0035. Price books, cost drivers and calculators

- **Status:** Proposed – the owner decides the questions under _Owner decisions_; nothing is built yet
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Since:** 0.21.0 at the earliest (phase 1, see _Phases_); builds on [ADR 0026](0026-service-cost-planning.md), [0030](0030-contract-payments-and-recorded-costs.md), [0031](0031-supplier-contracts-and-contacts-scope.md) and [0032](0032-cost-attribution-platform-plans.md), and on [0033](0033-one-access-model.md) and [0034](0034-module-access.md) for rights

## Context

The owner asked for **price calculators** for data integration and pipeline costs and for consumption, whose calculation basis follows the **published prices** and the **discounts** a contract gives – primarily for Snowflake and Confluent, but generic: _any item can be added and its costs defined by freely chosen drivers (throughput, volume, storage, requests, hours, seats …) with a cost per unit, "otherwise we cannot be flexible enough to account for everything"_.

What Steward has today:

- **The cost model reads money after the fact** ([COSTS.md](../COSTS.md)): FOCUS rows with `BilledCost` and `EffectiveCost` per day or month from AWS, Snowflake, Confluent, AI gateways and FOCUS files. It keeps **no quantities and no unit prices**: `CostLine` (`server/src/costs/store.ts`) has no `PricingQuantity`, `PricingUnit`, `ListUnitPrice` or `SkuId`, and the Confluent reader (`confluentLines` in `server/src/costs/readers.ts`) keeps neither the `original_amount` and `discount_amount` it receives nor the line's `price`, `quantity` and `unit`.
- **Two price lists already exist, each built for one case:** the Snowflake fallback multiplies `METERING_DAILY_HISTORY` credits by a **price per credit** set on the cost source (exactly, in whole millionths with `BigInt`), and AI models carry **prices per 1,000 tokens** that price the usage log ([AI.md](../AI.md)). Neither has versions, a validity, a source or a discount.
- **Plans hold amounts typed by hand** (ADR 0026), **payment schedules and volumes** hold what a contract says (ADR 0030, 0032). Nothing says _how_ an amount was reckoned, so when a price changes nobody notices, and nobody compares the reckoning with the bill once the thing runs.
- **The boundaries** of ADR 0026, 0030 and 0032: lightweight, no chargeback, no invoices, **no currency conversion**, not the finance system of record; [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md): _no FinOps suite_ – commitment and rightsizing optimisation stay in a FinOps tool.

What teams do instead: a spreadsheet with list prices copied from a vendor page, a vendor calculator that does not know the discount, the result typed into a plan line. The spreadsheet is stale after the next price list, its formulas are nobody's, and the negotiated price sits in a mail.

Forces:

- **Generic.** Drivers differ per provider and grow: credits per warehouse size, eCKU-hours, GB written, GB-months stored, partitions, connector tasks, requests, tokens, seats. A fixed list of Snowflake and Confluent fields would be outgrown in a release.
- **Exact money.** Amounts are whole millionths with their currency, never a binary float (COSTS.md). JSONata – Steward's expression language – computes in binary doubles. And **unit prices are finer than millionths**: per-request and per-token prices have seven or more decimals.
- **Contracted prices are confidential.** Vendor contracts often forbid disclosing negotiated rates; a contract's scope is its own (ADR 0031), and a platform team's capacity contract is the platform team's (ADR 0032 part 4). A service team estimating a Snowflake pipeline usually may **not** see the contract whose rates apply.
- **Team scope:** a reader never sees, counts or is told about what they may not see – also not by dividing a total by a quantity.
- **Outbound calls** go through the outbound policy, read-only, size-capped.

**How others do it.** FOCUS has the columns for prices (`ListUnitPrice`, `ContractedUnitPrice`, `PricingUnit`, `PricingQuantity`, `PricingCategory`, `SkuId`, `SkuPriceId`, `ChargeCategory`, `ChargeFrequency`, `ListCost`, `ContractedCost`). Cloud price lists publish SKUs with a unit, a price per block (_per 10K_, _per 1M_) and tiers (Azure Retail Prices API, AWS Price List Query API, Databricks `system.billing.list_prices`); Snowflake publishes the **contracted** rate per service type in `ORGANIZATION_USAGE.RATE_SHEET_DAILY`. Billing engines separate **meters** (what is counted) from **rate cards** (what a unit costs, with tiers and commitments). Infracost estimates infrastructure-as-code changes from a pricing database and writes a JSON breakdown. The common shape: _quantity of a driver × price of an item in a versioned price list, adjusted by the contract_.

## Decision (proposed)

**Prices live in versioned price books of items, each priced per unit of a freely chosen driver; discounts are conditions of supplier contracts; calculators turn typed inputs into quantities of items with JSONata, and Steward prices the quantities exactly; an estimate keeps its inputs, the calculator version and the prices it used, is recalculated – never silently replaced – when prices change, becomes plan lines or a volume when a person confirms it, and is set against what is read once the item runs.** All of it belongs to the Costs module. Calculators never compute money; price books never know who uses them; contracts keep their terms.

Code (planned): new `shared/src/costPrices.ts` (units, tiers, blocks, credit chains, the pricing arithmetic – pure functions on whole numbers, like `shared/src/costVolumes.ts`), `shared/src/costCalculators.ts` (the calculator schema and its checks), `server/src/costs/price-books.ts` (books, versions, diffs, the CSV import), `server/src/costs/price-sources.ts` (the readers of part 2), `server/src/costs/calculators.ts`, `server/src/costs/estimates.ts` (calculation, recalculation, plan lines, estimate against actual), `server/src/costs/estimate-notices.ts`, `server/src/costs/fx.ts`, `server/src/suppliers/contract-conditions.ts`; web `web/src/pages/costs/PriceBooks.tsx`, `Calculators.tsx`, `web/src/components/costs/Estimates.tsx`, and a _Prices_ section in the contract dialog.

### 1. Items and drivers – the generic core

A **price book entry** is an item with its price. User-defined items are first-class: a book written by hand has the same fields as one read from a provider, and there is no _other_ bucket.

| Field                   | What                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | FOCUS                                    |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Item                    | An id unique in the book (the provider's SKU where there is one: `KAFKA_NETWORK_WRITE`, `warehouse-M`) and a name; optionally a price id when one SKU has several prices (region, edition, tier set)                                                                                                              | `SkuId`, `SkuPriceId`                    |
| Provider, service       | The book's provider; a service name and category per item (`Kafka` / _Integration_, `Warehouse` / _Analytics_)                                                                                                                                                                                                    | `ServiceName`, `ServiceCategory`         |
| Driver                  | **What is counted**: a name and a unit – `GB written` in `GBy`, `storage` in `GBy.mo`, `throughput` in `MBy/s`, `messages` in `{msg}/s`, `requests` in `{request}`, `runtime` in `h`, `eCKU-hours` in `{eCKU}.h`, `seats` in `{seat}.mo`, `credits` in `{credit}`, `tokens` in `{token}`. Any driver can be added | `PricingUnit`                            |
| Charge                  | **Usage** (per unit used), **recurring** (per unit per month, e.g. seats, a base fee) or **one-off** (setup)                                                                                                                                                                                                      | `ChargeCategory`, `ChargeFrequency`      |
| Price, block, currency  | A decimal per **block** of 1, 1,000 or 1,000,000 units (Azure's _10K_, a price _per 1M requests_), with up to 12 decimals, in an ISO 4217 currency – **or in credits**: a quantity of another item of the same book (below)                                                                                       | `ListUnitPrice` or `ContractedUnitPrice` |
| Tiers                   | Optional: **graduated** (each band at its price) or **volume** (the whole quantity at the price of the band reached), per month                                                                                                                                                                                   | `SkuPriceDetails`                        |
| Included, minimum       | A quantity free per month (applied before the tiers); a minimum charge per month                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | –                                        |
| Valid from, to          | When the price applies – a price increase announced for February applies to the months from February                                                                                                                                                                                                              | –                                        |
| Attributes, source note | Region, edition, size and other key–value pairs (at most 20); where the price comes from (a URL, a document number, the source read)                                                                                                                                                                              | `RegionId`, `Tags`                       |

- **Units are UCUM where possible** (_The Unified Code for Units of Measure_): `By` with SI and binary prefixes (`GBy` and `GiBy` are different units), `h`, `d`, `mo`, `/s`; counted things are annotations in braces (`{request}`, `{msg}`, `{credit}`, `{eCKU}`), shown with their name. Steward ships a **suggested** list of drivers and units for the forms; a driver is only a name and a unit, so a new one needs no release.
- **Conversion** only inside one dimension: prefixes (`MBy` → `GBy` → `TBy`, decimal and binary kept apart), time with **730 hours per month** (the convention of the cloud price lists, not UCUM's mean month of 730.5 hours – stated on every estimate). Annotations never convert: `{msg}` is not `{request}`. A quantity whose unit cannot become the item's is refused (`COST_PRICE_UNIT_MISMATCH`).
- **Prices in credits** (a credit chain): an item may be priced in units of another item of the same book – _warehouse size M_: 4 `{credit}` per `h`; _compute credit_: a currency amount per `{credit}`. Snowflake credits and Databricks DBUs are this shape; the chain is at most two steps deep and never circular (`COST_PRICE_CHAIN`). A calculator then counts hours of a size and never needs to know the credit's price.
- **Arithmetic**: a quantity is rounded half away from zero to millionths of its unit, then everything is whole numbers (`BigInt`): included quantity, tiers, blocks, the credit chain, the minimum; the amount becomes whole millionths of the currency, rounded half away from zero – the method of the Snowflake fallback today – and is shown with the currency's minor units (ADR 0030). The same function runs on the server and in the browser.

### 2. Price books and their sources

- A **price book** has a provider (free text, or a Steward **supplier**, or a **platform**), a currency, a kind – **list** (published prices), **contracted** (the prices of one contract, read or derived from the provider) or **internal** (the rates a platform team publishes to the teams that use the platform: a rate card) – and a source.
- **Versions.** A book's entries change only by a new **version**: a save by hand, a CSV import, or a source read that found a change (the SHA-256 of the normalised entries; an unchanged read only records that it read). Versions are immutable; each stores its **difference** to the one before – items added and removed, prices changed with before, after and the change in percent – shown as _What changed_ and listed on the book. Fifty versions are kept per book, and any version an estimate uses.
- **Manual books** carry _last checked_ (date and person) and _review every … days_ (default 90): when the time is up, the book's maintainers get **_Prices to review_** (`cost.price_review_due`, in the app, once per book and period). _Mark as checked_ records the check without a new version.

| Source                     | How                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Kind       | Bound to                                         | Read                                     |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| **By hand**                | The book's form, item by item                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | any        | –                                                | –                                        |
| **CSV file**               | Upload with column mapping and preview, like the entry import (ADR 0030): item, name, driver, unit, charge, price, block, currency, tiers (`0:0.10;1000:0.08`), included, minimum, valid from and to. 5 MB, 20,000 rows. _Replace the book_ or _add_; both make a version                                                                                                                                 | any        | –                                                | when imported                            |
| **Snowflake rate sheet**   | `SNOWFLAKE.ORGANIZATION_USAGE.RATE_SHEET_DAILY` of the platform's account through the SQL API: the latest `EFFECTIVE_RATE` per service type, usage type and currency (compute per credit, storage per TB-month, data transfer per TB). **The discount is included**; `CONTRACT_NUMBER` matches the number of a Steward contract when one has it. Not for reseller contracts                               | contracted | the Snowflake platform (its client and identity) | once a day, with the cost source         |
| **Confluent, derived**     | From the Costs API lines the cost source reads, now keeping each line's `price`, `quantity`, `unit`, `original_amount` and `discount_amount`: per product, line type, unit and network access type of the **last complete month**, the effective price (Σ amount ÷ Σ quantity), the list price (`price`) and the discount (Σ discount ÷ Σ original). Labelled _observed_ – only what was used has a price | contracted | the Confluent platform                           | once a month, when the month is complete |
| **Databricks list prices** | `system.billing.list_prices` (`sku_name`, `usage_unit`, `pricing.default`, `currency_code`, start and end time)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | list       | a Databricks platform (not in Steward yet)       | weekly                                   |
| **Azure Retail Prices**    | The public `prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices` with an OData filter of **selected services and regions** and a currency; `unitOfMeasure` gives the block (`1 Hour`, `10K`), `tierMinimumUnits` the tiers. No credentials                                                                                                                                                                                 | list       | a connector platform without credentials         | weekly                                   |

- **Platform-bound** readers (Snowflake, Confluent, AWS, later Databricks) use the platform's client and identity and read with the rights of who saved the source, as cost sources do; **source-bound** readers of public prices (Azure, the exchange rates of part 3) are a connector platform without credentials, like _endoflife.date_. Every request goes through the outbound policy and only reads – `GET` for public lists, read-only statements through the SQL API, `GetProducts` for AWS – at most 25 MB per read and 20,000 entries per book; more fails the read with `COST_PRICE_SOURCE_TOO_LARGE` and keeps the last version. A failed read keeps the last version and says why on the book.
- **The Snowflake fallback's price per credit** becomes an entry of the platform's contracted book (migrated as version 1); the fallback reads it from there. The AI models' token prices stay where they are in this record (decision 17).
- **List books for Snowflake and Confluent** are by hand or CSV: neither publishes its list prices through an API. Steward ships **no vendor prices** (they would be stale and wrong somewhere); it ships the **technical factors** – credits per hour per Snowflake warehouse size – in the example books, with the date they were checked (decision 19).

### 3. Discounts and conditions – on the supplier contract

**Conditions** belong to the contract that grants them, not to the price book: the contract is where the terms, the term, the visibility and the commitment already are (ADR 0030–0032). A contract gets a section **_Prices_** (_Preise_, Costs module on) with up to 100 conditions:

| Condition             | What                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Percent off list      | A percentage off the list book's price, for a provider, a book, a service, a category or one item                                                                            |
| Negotiated unit price | A price (and block, currency) for one item, replacing the list price                                                                                                         |
| Tiers                 | Own tiers for an item                                                                                                                                                        |
| Commitment            | Tiers of a percentage by the **committed amount** of a volume schedule of the same contract (ADR 0032) – _15 % off from 100,000 USD a year committed_ – read from the volume |
| Valid from, to        | Within the contract's term; none: the contract's term                                                                                                                        |

- **An order form inherits** its master agreement's conditions while it has none of its own – as for the SLA ([SUPPLIERS.md](../SUPPLIERS.md#master-agreements-and-order-forms)).
- **Which price applies**, per item and month, the most specific first: a negotiated unit price → a contracted book linked to the contract (the rate sheet already includes its discount, so percentages do not apply on top – a condition that would is flagged) → percent off the list price (the most specific scope; two conditions of the same scope for one item are refused, `COST_CONDITION_OVERLAP`) → the list price. Each estimate line says **how its price was found** (_list price of Snowflake list (v7) – 12 % off by contract SDC-2026-014_).
- **Currencies.** An estimate has one currency. A price in another one is converted with a **rate of a date**: the **ECB euro reference rates** (published on TARGET working days, cross rates through the euro; a public source through the outbound policy) or a **manual rate** set by the costs managers. The rate, its date and its source are stored on the estimate and shown on every converted line. Nothing else converts: the cost model, plans, commitments and volumes stay per currency (ADR 0026), and a plan line made from an estimate carries the estimate's currency (decision 10).

### 4. Calculators

A **calculator** is a template: typed inputs, named intermediate values, and lines that each name an item and a JSONata expression for its **quantity**. Versioned (a published version is immutable; drafts are tried with _Calculate_), with owning teams and shares (ADR 0033), exported and **synced from Git** as `kind: costCalculator`, one per file, like monitors ([GIT-SYNC.md](../GIT-SYNC.md)).

```yaml
kind: costCalculator
id: snowflake-pipeline
name: Snowflake pipeline
version: 3
inputs:
  - { key: size, label: Warehouse size, type: select, options: [XS, S, M, L, XL], default: S }
  - { key: minutes, label: Runtime per run, type: number, unit: min, default: 12, min: 0, max: 1440 }
  - { key: runs, label: Runs per day, type: integer, default: 24, min: 0, max: 1440 }
  - { key: serverless, label: Serverless credits per month, type: number, unit: '{credit}', default: 0 }
  - { key: stored, label: Data kept, type: number, unit: GBy, default: 500 }
let:
  hours: inputs.minutes / 60 * inputs.runs * const.hoursPerMonth / 24
lines:
  - { item: { provider: Snowflake, sku: "{{ 'warehouse-' & inputs.size }}" }, quantity: let.hours, unit: h } # priced in credits
  - { item: { provider: Snowflake, sku: serverless }, quantity: inputs.serverless, unit: '{credit}' }
  - { item: { provider: Snowflake, sku: storage }, quantity: inputs.stored, unit: GBy.mo, group: Storage } # the book prices TB-months
```

- **Formulas produce quantities, never money.** Steward prices each line with part 1's arithmetic; the expression cannot round a price, apply a discount or reach a contract. A line names an item by **provider and SKU**, not a book: which book prices it is the estimate's choice (part 5), so one calculator serves list prices, contracted prices and internal rates.
- **Inputs**: `number`, `integer`, `select`, `boolean` and `table` (CSV rows – one line per topic or per table, as process inputs), each with a label, a unit, a default, bounds and help. **Bindings**: `inputs`, `let`, `const` (`hoursPerMonth` 730, `daysPerMonth`) and the selected items' attributes – no platform variables, secrets, datasets, HTTP or the clock.
- **Evaluation** uses the engine's guard rails (`server/src/engine/expr.ts`), tightened: 500 ms per calculation, a sequence limit of 10,000, at most 200 resulting lines, quantities finite and not negative (a credit line is an item with a negative price, not a negative quantity). A broken formula answers `COST_CALCULATOR_FORMULA` with the line and the message.
- **Examples** (`examples/costs/calculators/`, invented prices in the demo only): **Snowflake pipeline** (warehouse size × runtime × runs, serverless credits, storage, optionally data transfer); **Confluent topic and cluster** (eCKU-hours or a dedicated cluster's CKU-hours, ingress and egress GB from throughput × retention hours, storage GB-month from throughput × retention × replication as the provider bills it, partitions above the included ones, connector task-hours and throughput); **throughput × price** (one item, one driver, a rate and a period – the generic calculator for anything else).
- **Lines by hand.** An estimate may also have lines without a calculator: an item of a visible book and a quantity. Whatever has a price can be estimated without writing a formula.

### 5. Estimates

- **Belongs to a service or a platform** – the owners of cost plans (ADR 0032) – and is optionally **for** an application or system of the service, a platform, and later a data product. An application gets no estimates of its own, as it gets no plan: two services would estimate it twice. Its _Costs and contracts_ panel lists the estimates that name it (ADR 0032 part 1).
- **Stores** the inputs, the calculator and its version, lines by hand, the currency, the date from which it applies, the **price-book versions, conditions and exchange rates used**, and the result: per line the quantity and unit, the list and the applied unit price, the amount, how the price was found; totals **per month** (usage and recurring), **one-off**, and **per month over the next 36 months** when prices change within them (a valid-from in the future). The result is never recomputed on read.
- **Prices are chosen per estimate**: by default, per provider, the contracted or internal book the estimate's editor may see, else the list book; the editor may pick another visible book.
- **Recalculation, never silent.** A new version of a book an estimate uses, or a changed condition, recalculates the estimate in the job `costs` against the new prices and stores the result beside the current one: _+8 % since the price update of 3 Feb 2027 (Snowflake rate sheet v12)_. The estimate's owners (the plan's owners: a service's named owners and owning teams' members, a platform's managers and owning teams' members) get **_Estimate changed_** (`cost.estimate_changed`, in the app and by e-mail by default, quiet-hours group _Budgets and costs_) once per estimate and price version when the total moves by at least the threshold (5 % by default, _Costs → Settings_); figures only to who may see them (part 7). _Accept new prices_ makes the recalculated result current; the last 20 results stay as the estimate's history.
- **Plan lines from an estimate** – proposed per fiscal year and **confirmed by a person**, the pattern of contract schedules (ADR 0030): recurring and usage amounts as a monthly line from the estimate's start month (category _Cloud_ by default, one line per calculator group), one-off amounts as a once line, two lines where a price changes inside the year. The line remembers the estimate and the months it was made from (`estimate_id`, `estimate_basis`); an accepted change shows _Estimate changed_ with _Update lines_; a line changed by hand keeps its estimate. It is a **planned item without a contract**: no supplier until someone names one.
- **Into a volume.** _Size a volume_ on a contract's volume schedule (ADR 0032) proposes the sum of chosen estimates of the payer – same currency – as the volume per contract year; a person confirms it, the schedule names the estimates, and _Estimate changed_ shows there too.
- **Estimate against actual.** From a **live from** date, an estimate is set against what is read: it declares its **match** – a platform and its resource ids (`lkc-…`, a connector's `lcc-…`), service names, a tag value or its application – and shows per complete month estimated, read and the difference, per line where the match is per item. Confluent's cluster and connector lines match directly; Snowflake's bill is per account and service type, so a pipeline on a shared warehouse matches only by **measured** use (later: warehouse credits from `WAREHOUSE_METERING_HISTORY`, query-tag credits from `QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY`, priced by the same book). With measured quantities the difference splits into **quantity** (used more than estimated) and **price** (prices changed). **_Over the estimate_** (`cost.estimate_exceeded`) tells the owners once per estimate and month when a complete month exceeds the estimate by the threshold.
- **Measured inputs** (later): an input may be bound to a dataset metric (the 30-day mean of `kafka.throughput/bytesInPerSec`) – read with the estimate owner's rights, shown with its value and date – so measured throughput × price becomes a consumption-based calculation that follows the measurement.

### 6. Not built: an infrastructure-as-code estimator – Infracost instead

Steward does not parse Terraform or price cloud resources from code. It **imports Infracost's JSON** (`infracost breakdown --format json`): each cost component becomes an estimate line with its name, unit, monthly quantity and price, marked _Infracost price of …_; resources and projects become groups; the file's currency is the estimate's. Upload with a preview, or **push from CI** with a push token per estimate – the pattern of FOCUS push tokens (one estimate, nothing else, shown once, rotate and revoke, audited). 10 MB and 5,000 components per file. Steward never calls Infracost's pricing service; percent-off conditions apply on top when the provider matches a supplier's contract (decision 18).

### 7. Rights, team scope and confidentiality

| Action                                                                                                           | Who                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Read a list book, use it in an estimate                                                                          | everyone who uses the Costs module (ADR 0034) – list prices are public                                                                                                   |
| Maintain an instance book (no owning teams), an instance calculator, FX rates, the review and threshold settings | the **costs managers**: administrators and designers until module roles exist; then the Costs module's manager role (ADR 0034 part 4), designers mapped to it by default |
| Maintain a book or calculator with owning teams                                                                  | as ADR 0033: Edit through a team role or a share                                                                                                                         |
| Maintain a platform's contracted or internal book, its source                                                    | who may manage the platform and its owning teams' members – as the platform's cost plan                                                                                  |
| Read a contracted book; a contract's conditions                                                                  | who sees the contract (ADR 0031, 0032 part 4) **and** its amounts; for a platform's book: who sees the platform's amounts – they read its bill already                   |
| Change a contract's conditions                                                                                   | who may change the contract                                                                                                                                              |
| Publish an internal rate card to other teams                                                                     | its Manage holders, as a **share** (ADR 0033) – a widening: the step-up `access.grant`, audited `access.shares_changed`                                                  |
| Create, change, recalculate, accept an estimate; plan lines from it                                              | who may change the owner's cost plan                                                                                                                                     |
| Read an estimate                                                                                                 | who may read the owner's cost plan; amounts by the module's rule (_Hide amounts from viewers_)                                                                           |

- **Contracted prices stay with the contract.** The conditions – percentages, negotiated prices, tiers, commitments – are seen only by readers of the contract with its amounts. A team that may not see the contract uses the **internal rate card** its platform team publishes, or the list price.
- **An estimate never gives a price away by division.** A reader of the estimate who may not see a book or condition it used sees that line **at the list price**, marked _Priced with rates you may not see – shown at list price_ (`priceHidden`), and the totals as _at list prices for some lines_; with no list price, the line's amount is left out and the total marked incomplete. This is ADR 0032's rule for prepaid drawdown (`drawnHidden`): the figures would otherwise show what is hidden.
- **Plan lines carry amounts** to every reader of the plan. Creating plan lines from an estimate priced with rates some readers of the plan may not see asks first (_Readers of this plan will see these amounts_); the amount is then the team's statement, as any plan line.
- **Calculators** hold no prices: a reader of a calculator learns nothing confidential. Items a calculator names that the reader's books do not have show as _no price_ (`COST_PRICE_MISSING`), never as someone else's price.
- **Sessions:** every new write needs a signed-in session; API tokens and MCP read. The audit records every change of a price (before and after), a condition and an estimate: `cost.price_book_added|changed|removed`, `cost.price_book_version_added` (source, counts per kind of change), `cost.price_book_checked`, `cost.calculator_added|changed|published|removed`, `cost.estimate_added|changed|accepted|removed|imported`, `cost.estimate_lines_added` and `cost.estimate_lines_updated` (plan lines), `cost.fx_rate_set`, `supplier.contract_conditions_changed`; shares as `access.shares_changed`.

### 8. Data model (additive migrations)

- `costs-006-price-books`: `cost_price_books` (id, name, kind, provider, `supplier_id`, `supplier_contract_id`, `platform_id`, currency, source and its settings JSON, review days, last checked by and at, current version), `cost_price_book_versions` (book, number, SHA-256, entries, the difference JSON, source run, created by and at), `cost_price_items` (version, `sku_id`, `sku_price_id`, name, service, category, driver, unit, charge, price as a decimal string, block, currency or credit item, tiers JSON, included, minimum, valid from and to, attributes JSON; index on `(version_id, sku_id)`), `cost_fx_rates` (date, base, quote, rate as a decimal string, source).
- `costs-007-calculators-estimates`: `cost_calculators`, `cost_calculator_versions` (definition YAML, its SHA-256, published by and at), `cost_estimates` (owner kind and id, for kind and id, title, calculator and version, inputs JSON, lines JSON, currency, applies from, live from, match JSON, current result JSON, books, conditions and rates used JSON, created and updated by and at), `cost_estimate_results` (history, at most 20 per estimate), `cost_estimate_notices` (estimate, kind, price version or month – sent once), `cost_estimate_push_tokens`; `cost_plan_lines.estimate_id`, `estimate_basis`; `cost_schedules.estimate_ids`.
- `suppliers-006-contract-conditions`: `supplier_contract_conditions` (contract, kind, scope JSON, value, tiers JSON, volume schedule, valid from and to, created and updated by and at).
- Books and calculators are owned kinds (`cost_price_book`, `cost_calculator` in `OWNED_KINDS`) with shares from their first release. Person columns are history; the tables move with migration packages and backups; classified in `server/src/migration/tables.ts`.

### 9. Interfaces and limits

- **Internal:** `/api/costs/price-books[/:id[/versions[/:n]]]`, `…/:id/import/preview`, `…/:id/import`, `…/:id/read`, `…/:id/check`; `/api/costs/calculators[/:id[/versions]]`, `POST /api/costs/calculate` (a dry run: nothing stored); `/api/costs/estimates?ownerKind=&ownerId=`, `…/:id/accept`, `…/:id/proposals`, `…/:id/actual`, `…/:id/infracost[/preview]`, `/api/hooks/costs/estimates/:id`; `/api/costs/fx-rates`; `PUT /api/suppliers/:id/contracts/:contractId/conditions`.
- **Public, read-only:** `GET /api/v1/cost-estimates` (paged; `serviceId`, `platformId`), `GET /api/v1/cost-estimates/{id}`, `GET /api/v1/price-books` and `/{id}` (books the token owner may see, conditions never). The cost plans gain lines' `estimate`.
- **MCP:** `list_cost_estimates`, `get_cost_estimate`, `list_price_books`, and `calculate_cost_estimate` – a dry run with the caller's books and rights that stores nothing (decision 20).
- **Dataset** `costs.estimates` (one row per estimate: monthly total, one-off, the change since the last accepted prices, estimate against actual; administrators and auditors, at most hourly, like `costs.plans`); the export part _Cost plans_ gains `estimates.csv` (formulas defused).
- **Limits:** 500 books; 20,000 entries per version; 50 versions kept per book (and those in use); 100 conditions per contract; 200 calculators; per calculator 50 inputs, 100 line definitions, 4,000 characters per expression, 64 KB per definition; 200 lines per estimate; 200 estimates per owner; 20 results kept per estimate; the job recalculates at most 500 estimates per run.
- **New error codes** (English and German): `COST_PRICE_UNIT_MISMATCH`, `COST_PRICE_CHAIN`, `COST_PRICE_MISSING`, `COST_PRICE_SOURCE_TOO_LARGE`, `COST_CONDITION_OVERLAP`, `COST_CALCULATOR_FORMULA`, `COST_CALCULATOR_OUTPUT`, `COST_FX_RATE_MISSING`, `COST_ESTIMATE_LIMIT`.

## Security notes

- **Outbound reads** go through the outbound policy like every platform call: reading calls only (as in part 2), no redirects across origins, 30 seconds and 25 MB per read, at most 50 pages per source; public sources carry no credentials; platform-bound reads use the platform's identity (mark it read-only, ADR 0009). A refused host says so on the book.
- **Parsing** of CSV, provider answers and Infracost files: one pass, no regular expressions on content, decimals as text until they become whole numbers, keys like `__proto__` dropped, attributes and tags in `Map`s; every count and size capped before it is stored.
- **Formula evaluation** runs in the engine's sandbox with tightened limits (500 ms, depth, sequence, the regex guard) and bindings that hold no secrets, platform variables or network access; the output is checked against a schema before it is priced. A formula cannot reach money, contracts or other teams' data – it sees its inputs and the attributes of items its estimate may use.
- **Integrity of prices:** a price changes plans and decisions, so every change is a version with its difference and its author, audited before and after; source reads record their run; an estimate pins the versions it used, so a figure can always be traced to the price behind it.
- **Confidential prices** never cross the contract's scope (part 7); widening a book is a share with the step-up; nothing is derived from a hidden price for a reader who may not see it.
- **Push tokens** for Infracost follow the FOCUS push tokens: one estimate, hashed, optional expiry, allowed addresses, rate limit, checked from the headers before the body is read.

## Performance

- Estimates store their result; lists and pages read it and never calculate. A calculation reads its books with one statement per book (entries by `(version_id, sku_id)`) and the conditions of the contracts involved once.
- Recalculation runs in the job `costs` in batches, yielding between estimates, at most 500 per run; only estimates that use the changed version or condition are touched (an index on the books used).
- Differences are computed once, when a version is written. Source reads are daily (Snowflake), monthly (Confluent) or weekly (public lists), never on a page load.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Spreadsheets** (keep estimating outside Steward, attach the file). Rejected: no versions of prices, no link to contracts or plans, no recalculation or comparison – the problem the owner describes.
2. **A fixed catalogue for Snowflake and Confluent** (fields per provider). Rejected by the owner's brief: every new driver would need a release. Snowflake and Confluent are calculators and sources on the generic core.
3. **Formulas that compute money.** Rejected: binary floats in amounts, discounts hidden in expressions, and a price changed in a formula is invisible to the price book's history.
4. **Discounts on the price book** (a contracted copy of every list book). Rejected as the only way: the terms, the validity, the commitment and the visibility are the contract's. A contracted book stays for what a provider reports with the discount included (Snowflake's rate sheet, Confluent's observed rates).
5. **Infracost only.** Rejected: it prices infrastructure code at list prices, not data pipelines, throughput or contracted rates; it is the right tool for its part and is imported (part 6).
6. **Vendor calculators** (Confluent's and the cloud providers' pricing calculators). Rejected as the basis: list prices only, no versions in Steward, no link to plans or bills; their results can still be typed as lines by hand.
7. **OpenCost** for Kubernetes. Out of scope here: it allocates running costs, it does not estimate; it stays a possible cost source (COSTS.md, _later_).
8. **A rating engine** (meters, rate cards, invoices). Rejected: billing is a finance system; Steward estimates and compares.

## Consequences

- A team reckons a pipeline or a topic once, in a calculator anyone can read, priced from versioned books and the contract's terms; when prices change it is told by how much, and the plan line it confirmed shows _Estimate changed_.
- Platform teams publish internal rate cards without disclosing contract terms; service teams estimate with the rates that apply to them.
- Once an item runs, the estimate meets the bill, and – with measured quantities – the difference says whether the use or the price moved.
- **ADR 0026's _no currency conversion_ is relaxed for estimates only**, with the rate and its date on every converted line; **ADR 0034's** statement that the Costs module declares no roles changes: it gets the manager role for instance books and calculators.
- More concepts: drivers and units, books and versions, conditions, calculators, estimates. Forms offer the suggested drivers and units; the examples show each concept once.
- **Risks:** a stale manual book – review reminders and _last checked_ on every line priced from it; an estimate read as a quote – labelled _Estimate_ everywhere, with its price date; observed Confluent rates that move with the mix of use – labelled _observed_, per month; a list price that overstates for a team without a rate card – the estimate says _at list price_; formulas that are wrong – calculators are versioned, reviewed through Git where synced, and every line shows quantity and price.
- Revisit if teams need shared tiers across estimates (an account's whole use placing a new item in a lower tier), allocation of a platform's capacity to the estimates that use it (chargeback stays out, ADR 0026), or prices from FOCUS files' `ListUnitPrice` and `ContractedUnitPrice`.

## Phases

1. **0.21.0 – the core:** items, drivers, units and credit chains; books by hand and from CSV with versions, differences and the review reminder; the Snowflake fallback's price migrated into a book; calculators (definition, editor, dry run, Git sync, the three examples); estimates with calculators or lines by hand; plan lines from estimates with _Estimate changed_; manual exchange rates; rights by core roles and owning teams; `/api/v1` and MCP reads. Shares on books and calculators come with ADR 0033 phase 1.
2. **0.22.0 – contracts and sources:** the Snowflake rate sheet and Confluent's observed rates; conditions on contracts with inheritance and commitments; internal rate cards shared to other teams; recalculation and _Estimate changed_ notifications; ECB rates; _Size a volume_; estimate against actual from the matched bill and _Over the estimate_; the Costs manager role with ADR 0034 phase 2.
3. **Later:** Azure, AWS and Databricks sources (the last with a Databricks platform); Infracost import and push; measured inputs from datasets; measured actual from warehouse metering and query tags; data products as targets; AI model prices as a book; FOCUS price columns kept by the cost model.

## Owner decisions

1. **Generic core or provider fields.** _Recommendation:_ generic – items priced per unit of a free driver, user-defined items first-class; Snowflake and Confluent are sources and calculators on top, and Steward ships only a suggested list of drivers.
2. **Units.** _Recommendation:_ UCUM codes where possible, annotations (`{msg}`, `{credit}`) for counted things, conversion only within a dimension, 730 hours per month – shown on every estimate.
3. **Formulas compute quantities, Steward computes money.** _Recommendation:_ yes – amounts stay exact, discounts stay in contracts, and price history stays in books.
4. **Precision of unit prices.** _Recommendation:_ decimals with up to 12 places and a block of 1, 1,000 or 1,000,000 units; amounts stay whole millionths of the currency, rounded half away from zero.
5. **Prices in credits.** _Recommendation:_ yes, at most two steps (size → credits → currency) – the shape of Snowflake and Databricks pricing, and a calculator never needs the credit's price.
6. **Where discounts live.** _Recommendation:_ on the supplier contract (_Prices_), inherited by order forms from their master; contracted books only for rates a provider reports with the discount included.
7. **A cost or FinOps role.** _Recommendation:_ no new instance role – the Costs module's **manager** role of ADR 0034 for instance-wide books, calculators and rates (designers mapped to it by default, so 0.21's rule carries over), owning teams and shares for team books, the platform's managers for platform books.
8. **Who sees negotiated prices.** _Recommendation:_ only readers of the contract with its amounts (and of a platform's contracted book: who sees the platform's amounts); estimate readers who may not see them get list prices, marked – never a contracted figure they could divide.
9. **Internal rate cards.** _Recommendation:_ yes – a platform team publishes the rates that apply to its consumers as a book shared with their teams (step-up, audited), without the contract's terms.
10. **Currency conversion.** _Recommendation:_ in estimates only, ECB reference rates or a manual rate, with rate, date and source on every converted line; the cost model, plans, commitments and volumes stay per currency.
11. **What an estimate belongs to.** _Recommendation:_ a service or a platform, as cost plans, optionally for an application, system, platform or (later) data product – no estimates owned by applications, which would be counted twice.
12. **Recalculation.** _Recommendation:_ automatic in the background, stored beside the current result, accepted by a person; notified once per estimate and price version at a 5 % change (an instance setting).
13. **Plan lines from estimates.** _Recommendation:_ proposed per fiscal year and confirmed, with _Estimate changed_ and _Update lines_ – the pattern of contract schedules; no line without a person.
14. **Estimate against actual.** _Recommendation:_ a declared match per estimate (platform, resource ids, service names, tag or application) against the bill read, from a live-from date; measured quantities and the split into quantity and price later.
15. **The Snowflake fallback's price per credit.** _Recommendation:_ migrated into the platform's contracted book as version 1; the cost source reads it from there.
16. **Confluent rates.** _Recommendation:_ observed effective rates from the last complete month of the Costs API, labelled _observed_, plus a list book by hand for what was not used yet.
17. **Public price lists and the AI models' prices.** _Recommendation:_ Azure (public connector), AWS (`GetProducts` with selected services and regions, never bulk files) and Databricks (with a Databricks platform) later; AI token prices stay on the models until then.
18. **Infracost.** _Recommendation:_ import and push its JSON as an estimate with its prices, contract percentages on top where the provider matches; no repricing of its SKUs and no call to its pricing service.
19. **Shipped examples.** _Recommendation:_ calculators and technical factors (credits per warehouse size), dated; no vendor money prices – the demo's prices are invented and say so.
20. **MCP.** _Recommendation:_ read tools plus `calculate_cost_estimate` as a dry run with the caller's rights that stores nothing; assistants do not save, accept or plan estimates.
21. **Module.** _Recommendation:_ all of it in the Costs module – books, calculators, estimates and the contract's _Prices_ section switch off with it; contracts stay core records.
