# Data products

> **Preview, off by default.** An administrator switches the module on under _Settings → Modules → Data products_ and may restrict it to selected teams and users (_Who may use it_, [ADR 0034](adr/0034-module-access.md)). This page describes what 0.22.0 builds (phase 1) and phase 2, built in 0.24.0 – [lineage from tools](#lineage-from-tools), [quality results](#quality-results), the [scorecard over time](#scorecard-history), [consumers, access and governance](#consumers-access-and-governance), [measured use](#measured-use) and [estimates](#estimates), [proposals from assistants](#proposals-from-assistants) and the exchange of products; what is still to come is listed [at the end](#phase-2). The design and its reasons are in [ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md).

**Try it:** start the demo (`npm run demo`), _Load demo data_ on Home, switch the module on – the invented shop's domains, data objects, discovered elements and three products are there ([The demo](#the-demo)).

Steward documents data products from the IT service team's side: what a domain offers, which data it holds and how sensitive that data is, what the product is made of on Snowflake, Kafka, SQL databases, APIs and file stores, how data moves through it, and who uses it. Steward keeps a thin model of its own and links out to catalogues (DataHub, OpenMetadata, Collibra) instead of becoming one.

## The model

| Entry            | What it is                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Domain**       | A business domain (_Sales_) or a sub-domain (_Sales → Orders_) – two levels – with owners and owning teams                                                   |
| **Data object**  | What the data **is** – _Customer_, _Order_: a definition, synonyms, key attributes, a CIA rating, a classification, personal data, legal basis and retention |
| **Data product** | What a domain **offers**: a type, owners, a rating, a lifecycle, environments, ports                                                                         |
| **Element**      | A concrete thing on a platform in one environment: a table, a view, a topic, an API, a path                                                                  |
| **Membership**   | An element in a product with a role: _input_, _internal_ or _output_; one element can be part of several products                                            |
| **Port**         | A product's interface (input or output) with a version and its own lifecycle; per environment a **binding** names its elements and its data contract         |
| **Consumer**     | A registration on an output port: who (a team, a user, an application, a service, a product or a text), the purpose, since when, the agreement               |
| **Step**         | How data moves: inputs → a runner → outputs, with transformation notes (documentation only – Steward runs nothing)                                           |

## Data objects

A data object says what the business data **is** – _Customer_, _Order_, _Machine measurement_ – independent of where it is stored. Data objects are the module's **business glossary**: there is no second list of terms ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) decision 5); an external glossary entry (Collibra, Purview, a wiki page) is linked as an external reference.

- **Definition and words:** a key, a name, a **definition** (Markdown), up to 20 **synonyms**, a domain, a lifecycle (_draft_, _active_, _deprecated_, _retired_) and up to 100 **key attributes** (name, description, whether personal – a light list, not a schema: the schema lives in the data contract).
- **Rating:** a **CIA rating** on the instance's CIA scale – rated and re-rated exactly as applications, with a justification per dimension; a change of the scale flags it for re-rating – and a **classification**: _public_, _internal_, _confidential_, _strictly confidential_ (four fixed keys beside the CIA rating, not mapped onto it).
- **Personal data:** _none_, _personal_ or _special categories_ (GDPR Art. 9) with the categories, the data subjects, the **legal basis** (Art. 6(1)(a)–(f)), the purposes, the **retention** (an ISO 8601 duration such as `P10Y`, or text), the deletion rule and **regulatory flags** (_GDPR_, _trade secret_, _export control_, _SOX_, _NIS2_, _product liability_ suggested; others typed). Data objects describe categories of data, never persons.
- **Roles:** the **data owner** (a user), the **stewards** (users or teams) and the **technical custodians** (teams) – responsibilities with reminders, not rights (decision 6): owner and stewards see the object, changing it follows its owning teams.
- **Where it lives:** elements **implement** a data object (_Link element_ on the object's page or the element's), products **expose** it through their output ports (declared on the port, or implemented by the bound elements) – the object's page lists both, products you may not see counted.
- **The rating floor:** a product exposing an object should be rated at least as high – see [Data products](#data-products).
- **Personal data in fields** (0.24.0): the object's page lists the personal and classified fields of the contracts bound to the output ports that expose it ([Personal data in fields](#personal-data-in-fields)).

## Data products

A product has a key (unique, used by the file format and the ODPS `id`), a name, a description and purpose, a **type** (_source-aligned_, _aggregate_, _consumer-aligned_), a domain, **product owners**, support, a **criticality**, a **support tier**, a version and a business value.

- **The rating floor.** Per dimension a product's CIA rating – and its classification – should be at least the highest of the data objects it exposes (declared on its output ports or implemented by their elements). Lower is allowed with a reason (only aggregated data leaves the product); without one the product page, the list and the scorecard warn. Ratings waiting for a re-rating do not count.
- **Lifecycle:** _proposed_ → _draft_ → _active_ → _deprecated_ → _retired_ (the ODPS statuses), with the date of each change, a sunset date and a successor. _Active_ needs a product owner and an output port, _deprecated_ a sunset date, _retired_ a confirmation while consumers are registered or consuming products are active. Retiring – like every lifecycle change – is done in the app (not with an API token).
- **Environments** come from _Settings → Environments_; production first. A product exists in each environment its members and bindings name (_planned_, _available_, _withdrawn_).

## The element catalogue

An element has a platform (or none for APIs and other things), a **kind** (`snowflake.table`, `kafka.topic`, `sql.view`, `api.openapi`, `files.path`, …), a qualified name unique per platform, kind and environment (case does not matter), a parent (database → schema → table), a layer and a description. Elements are entered, imported or **discovered** (below). Discovered elements keep what people wrote: a new read changes only the technical fields – name, kind flags, parent, external id, OpenLineage dataset – and the readout. An entered element that a discovery source finds again is looked after by that source from then on.

## Discovery

**Discovery sources** read elements from the platforms Steward already connects to – metadata only, with the platform's identity (mark it read-only, [PLATFORM-SIGN-IN.md](PLATFORM-SIGN-IN.md#read-only-identities-and-the-operation-filter)), as a background job. _Data → Discovery_ in the app; _API:_ `/api/data-products/discovery-sources`.

| Platform                                                             | What a source reads                                                                                                                         | Elements                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Snowflake** ([SNOWFLAKE.md](SNOWFLAKE.md#data-products-discovery)) | the databases and schemas of its scope (`SHOP`, `SHOP.SALES`): the catalogue views and `SHOW … IN DATABASE`                                 | databases, schemas, tables, views (secure and materialized as flags), dynamic tables, streams, tasks, stages, pipes         |
| **Kafka** ([KAFKA.md](KAFKA.md#data-products-discovery))             | the cluster's topics under its prefixes (none: all; internal `_…` topics left out) and the platform's Schema Registry                       | topics with their topic id, partitions, replication, `cleanup.policy`, `retention.ms` and the latest `<topic>-value` schema |
| **Databases** (PostgreSQL, MariaDB/MySQL, SQL Server)                | the platform's **latest schema snapshot** ([DATABASES.md](DATABASES.md#database-schemas)) – no connection of its own; a scope names schemas | schemas, tables, views (column counts only)                                                                                 |

Confluent Cloud clusters are discovered as the Kafka platforms that connect to them.

**A source** has a platform, a **scope**, **include and exclude prefixes** (plain comparisons of the qualified name without case – no patterns; `SHOP.SALES.` keeps the database `SHOP` and the schema `SHOP.SALES` as their containers), **environment rules** (a prefix or suffix of the database, schema or topic → an environment, e.g. suffix `_DEV` → `dev`; the first that matches, else the platform's environment), the Snowflake **mode**, the **identity** it reads with, the **owning teams** of what it finds (default: the platform's) and how often it reads – daily by default, at most hourly, at least weekly. At most 50 sources per instance.

**Who:** the module's managers (designers by default, and whoever an administrator grants the manager role) and the platform's managers set sources up – if they may use the platform: a source **runs as the person who last saved it**, and that person's use right on the platform and the identity is checked again before every run; a source whose person lost the right stops and says so (_The source runs as …_). Who has a right on the platform sees its sources, unless the owning teams of what they find exclude them. Every change and _Read now_ needs a signed-in session and is in the audit log (`datadiscovery.source_created`, `…_updated`, `…_deleted`, `datadiscovery.run`, `datadiscovery.run_failed`). Owning teams that reach people the platform's teams do not – or none, the whole instance – ask for a confirmation (control point `access.grant`) and are recorded as an access change (`access.owning_teams_changed`). New owning teams apply to the elements the source adds from then on.

**A run** (the job `data-products` every 5 minutes for the sources that are due, one at a time; or _Read now_):

- reads in partitions and writes in batches of 250 elements per transaction, with a turn of the event loop between batches – an unchanged element writes only its readout;
- **renames** an element when the platform's id of it is known under a new name (Snowflake `TABLE_ID` in _account usage_ mode): people's fields, memberships and links stay; in _information schema_ mode a renamed table is a new element and the old one goes missing;
- marks the source's elements it did not find **missing** – only after a **complete** run. A run that stops at 50,000 elements, at the instance's 250,000, after 10 minutes, or reads a cut schema snapshot is _truncated_ and marks nothing. A missing element in no product is removed after 30 days; in a product it stays and is flagged there;
- fills the **OpenLineage** namespace and name of each element (`snowflake://<account>` with `DB.SCHEMA.TABLE`, `kafka://<first bootstrap server>` with the topic, `postgres://<host>:<port>` with `<database>.<schema>.<table>`, likewise `mssql://` for SQL Server and `mysql://` with `<database>.<table>`), for matching run events later;
- keeps the newest 30 runs per source with their counts (added, changed, renamed, unchanged, missing, removed) and why a run failed, stopped early or could not read a part (a Schema Registry: the topics stand without schema versions).

**Metadata only.** Snowflake: `INFORMATION_SCHEMA` (SCHEMATA, TABLES, VIEWS – secure ones), `ACCOUNT_USAGE` (DATABASES, SCHEMATA, TABLES, VIEWS – and OBJECT_DEPENDENCIES after a complete run, for [proposed steps](#lineage-from-tools)) and `SHOW DYNAMIC TABLES | STREAMS | TASKS | STAGES | PIPES`, of whose output only metadata columns are kept – never a definition; Kafka: the metadata and DescribeConfigs requests, never a consumer group, an offset or a message; the Schema Registry: `GET /subjects` and the latest version of a subject (its id, version and type, not the schema). Names from a scope go into Snowflake statements as a bound `IDENTIFIER(?)` or, for `SHOW`, as a checked and quoted identifier; every other value is a binding. Every request goes through the outbound policy – the account's and the registry's origins are checked first – and connects to the checked address. The tests check the statements and calls against these lists.

## Lineage from tools

> **0.24.0** ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) phase 2, part 9 _Steps from tools_). Tools that run the pipelines know how data moves; Steward takes what they report as **proposals** and a person decides – nothing is applied silently, and Steward stays documentation (it runs no step, parses no SQL, keeps no column lineage).

Three sources propose steps between the elements of the catalogue:

| Source                                                    | What it reads                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | A proposal is                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **OpenLineage** (a lineage source of kind _OpenLineage_)  | [OpenLineage](https://openlineage.io) run events (spec 2-0-x) pushed by Airflow, dbt-ol, Spark, Flink and other clients: of an event only the job's namespace and name, the event type and time, the run id, and the namespace and name of each input and output dataset – of the facets only the job type's `integration` (to name the tool). Job events (static lineage) are taken too; dataset events are taken and not kept | a job (`namespace/name`): its input datasets → its output datasets                 |
| **dbt** (a lineage source of kind _dbt_, on one platform) | `manifest.json`: models, seeds, snapshots and sources with their relation (database, schema, alias or identifier), `depends_on` (through ephemeral models), tests and the node they check. `run_results.json`: each model's outcome and each test's status and failing row count. Never compiled or raw code, descriptions, columns, docs or messages                                                                           | a model or snapshot: the relations it depends on → its own                         |
| **Snowflake object dependencies** (a discovery source)    | in _account usage_ mode, after a complete run, `SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.OBJECT_DEPENDENCIES` of the objects in the source's scope – table-like objects on both sides (tables, views, materialized and dynamic tables, external tables, streams)                                                                                                                                                                                 | a referencing object (a view, a dynamic table): the objects it references → itself |

**Matching.** OpenLineage datasets are matched to elements by the **OpenLineage namespace and name** discovery gives every element (`snowflake://<account>` with `DB.SCHEMA.TABLE`, `kafka://<bootstrap server>` with the topic, `postgres://<host>:<port>` with `<database>.<schema>.<table>`) – names compared without case. A tool that names a platform differently (`snowflake://invented-org-shop` for the account `DEMO12345`) is matched through the source's **namespace aliases** (the tool's namespace → the elements'). dbt relations are matched by qualified name on the source's platform (`DB.SCHEMA.NAME`, or `SCHEMA.NAME` on a database platform). Datasets no element matches are shown on the proposal as the tool sent them (the first 20 per side, the rest counted) – discover the element, or add an alias.

**The inbox.** _Data → Discovery → Proposed steps_ lists the proposals of every source you may see, newest first, by status:

- **Open** – waits for a decision. **Accept** makes a step with the tool as its runner (`dbt`, `Airflow`, … and the job's key), its kind and name changeable on the way; the step belongs with the products of its outputs, as one written by hand: who may change those products accepts it, and only who sees every element it names. **Reject** keeps it away until the tool names other datasets for the job.
- **Already documented** – the documented steps say the same (every input → output way is a way of a step): nothing to do. Its runs still count (below).
- **Accepted** – a step was made from it. When the tool later reports other datasets for the job (a _complete_ run's, or a new manifest's), the proposal opens again as **Changes a documented step**; accepting it changes that step's ends (ends you may not see stay), rejecting keeps the step as it is.
- **Rejected**.

**Runs as evidence.** The last run of each job is kept – its state (_started_, _running_, _complete_, _aborted_, _failed_, _other_), its time and the last _complete_ run – and passed to the step it became or that documents it (`lastRun` on the step: the pipeline's steps carry it). An older event never replaces a newer run; a START, RUNNING or other event adds the datasets it names, a COMPLETE event (and a job event) says the job's datasets as they are. Events themselves are not kept.

**Quality results.** A dbt test's result is a [quality result](#quality-results) (source `dbt`) of each element it checks: pass → passed, warn → warning, fail → failed, error → error (skipped tests are not kept), the failing row count as its value and the run's `invocation_id` as its run, with the ISO/IEC 25012 dimension of its generic test (`not_null` → completeness, `unique` and `relationships` → consistency, `accepted_values` → accuracy; other tests by their name) – through the one writer of quality results (`recordQualityResults` in `server/src/dataproducts/quality.ts`) that Data Metric Functions, contract tests and the checks use too. Deleting the source forgets the dbt results that came through it.

### Lineage sources

_Data → Discovery → Lineage from tools → New lineage source_ (the module's managers, in a signed-in session): a name, the kind (_OpenLineage_ or _dbt_ – kept once saved), for dbt the platform its models live on (Snowflake or a database platform you may see), namespace aliases (at most 20), the **owning teams** (who sees the source, its tokens and its proposals; none: every user of the module; a new source is its creator's teams' – other teams or none ask for a confirmation, `access.grant`, and are an access change) and whether it **takes pushes** (paused: pushes answer 409, nothing changes). At most 50 sources. Every change is audited (`datalineage.source_created`, `…_updated`, `…_deleted`); deleting a source deletes its tokens, proposals and quality results – the steps made from its proposals stay.

**Push tokens** (`sdl_…`) follow the cost and schema push tokens: bound to one source, they can do nothing but push to it; the secret is shown once and only its SHA-256 is kept; allowed addresses (IP or CIDR), a rate limit (120 a minute by default, at most 1,200 – dbt files have a lower one of their own: six a minute per source, pushed and uploaded together, `DATA_LINEAGE_DBT_RATE`) and an expiry (at most two years); at most five per source. A token stops working when it expires, is revoked, or its creator is deactivated or may no longer manage the source. **Rotating** gives a token a new secret and keeps everything else – its creator too: it still pushes with the rights of the person who created it and stops with them, whoever rotated it. To move a token to yourself, create a new one and revoke the old. Creating and rotating need a signed-in session (`datalineage.token_created`, `…_rotated`, `…_revoked`). API tokens are refused at the push address – they carry a person's rights, a push token only this one.

**The push** – `POST /api/hooks/data-lineage/{sourceId}` with `Authorization: Bearer sdl_…` and `Content-Type: application/json`:

| Address                                          | Body                                                                       | Limit |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| `…/{sourceId}` and `…/{sourceId}/api/v1/lineage` | an OpenLineage event, or an array of at most 100 (an _OpenLineage_ source) | 1 MB  |
| `…/{sourceId}/dbt/manifest`                      | `manifest.json` (a _dbt_ source)                                           | 40 MB |
| `…/{sourceId}/dbt/run-results`                   | `run_results.json` (a _dbt_ source)                                        | 20 MB |

Everything that needs only the headers is checked **before the body is read**: the module is on, no session cookie, the token (of this source, not revoked or expired, its creator still allowed), the address, the rate (429 with `Retry-After`), the content type (415) and the length (413). Then the body is **validated**: an event must be an OpenLineage 2-0-x event (`schemaURL` `https://openlineage.io/spec/2-0-x/OpenLineage.json…`, an ISO 8601 `eventTime`, the run id, the job's namespace and name, at most 500 datasets per side) – else 400 `DATA_LINEAGE_EVENT_INVALID` naming where (the event is never echoed), and a push of several events is taken whole or not at all; a dbt file must carry its dbt schema version (`…/dbt/manifest/vN.json`, `…/dbt/run-results/vN.json`) and at most 50,000 nodes – else 400 `DATA_LINEAGE_DBT_INVALID`. A refused push after the token was accepted counts on the source (_refused_, the last error in your language on the source's page). An OpenLineage push costs a fixed number of database statements per event, whatever the number of its datasets (one read matches all of them, in at most 200 namespaces per event – the datasets of further namespaces stay unmatched, and the answer's `warnings` and the push's audit entry `datalineage.pushed` say how many namespaces were not matched); dbt files are written in batches of 250 proposals per transaction with a turn of the event loop between.

**Configuring the tools** (the source's page shows these with the token when it is made):

```bash
# OpenLineage clients (Airflow's provider, dbt-ol, the Spark listener …): the address with a slash at the end
OPENLINEAGE_URL=https://steward.example/api/hooks/data-lineage/dpl_…/
OPENLINEAGE_ENDPOINT=api/v1/lineage
OPENLINEAGE_API_KEY=sdl_…

# dbt in CI, after dbt build
curl -sS -f -X POST 'https://steward.example/api/hooks/data-lineage/dpl_…/dbt/manifest' \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $STEWARD_LINEAGE_TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' --data-binary @target/manifest.json
curl -sS -f -X POST 'https://steward.example/api/hooks/data-lineage/dpl_…/dbt/run-results' \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $STEWARD_LINEAGE_TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' --data-binary @target/run_results.json
```

A dbt source also takes the two files **uploaded** on its page (its managers, in a signed-in session). Pulling them from a Git sync source is not offered: `target/` is a build output that is rarely committed – CI pushes it instead. Every dbt file is audited (`datalineage.imported` with the counts and the SHA-256 of the body received); an OpenLineage push is audited when it proposes or changes something (`datalineage.pushed`), not for every run of a known job.

**The lineage picture** of an element draws open proposals between its elements – and one step further – as **proposed ways**: dotted, never moving, named _Proposed_ in words, in the legend and in the steps table; documented ways stay as they are beside them. Who may not see a proposal's source sees no proposed way; elements they may not see stay nameless as for steps. `/api/v1/data-products/{id}/lineage?element=` and the MCP tool `get_data_lineage` mark them (`proposed: true`) too.

**API** (internal, the module's switch and access): `GET|POST /api/data-products/lineage-sources`, `GET|PUT|DELETE …/{id}`, `GET|POST …/{id}/tokens`, `POST …/{id}/tokens/{tokenId}/rotate`, `DELETE …/{id}/tokens/{tokenId}`, `POST …/{id}/dbt/manifest | /dbt/run-results` (the file as the body); `GET /api/data-products/lineage-proposals` (`?sourceId=&status=open|accepted|rejected|documented&origin=&elementId=&cursor=`, 100 a page), `GET …/{id}`, `POST …/{id}/accept` (`{ name?, kind? }`) and `…/{id}/reject` – decisions are session-only (`datalineage.proposal_accepted` with the step's `dataproduct.step_added` or `…_changed`, `datalineage.proposal_rejected`).

## Ports, contracts and consumers

An output port is bound per environment to up to 20 elements of that environment and optionally to a **data contract** (the version it is held to and its ODCS server). Bound elements become members with the port's role. An input port names where its data comes from: another product's output port, an element of a source system, or a text. Consumers register on output ports; deprecating a product or a port tells them, and so does a breaking change of the contract a port follows (0.24.0, [Notifications](#notifications)).

**Objectives** of a port (and what a binding sets differently): freshness in minutes, availability in percent over 30 days, **completeness** in percent (0.24.0 – the share of the completeness [quality results](#quality-results) of its elements that passed over 30 days), an update schedule and retention.

### Personal data in fields

Field-level personal data comes from the contract, not from Steward (0.24.0, [ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) part 2): a property of a bound contract's schema object is **personal** when it carries `customProperties: [{ property: personalData, value: true }]` (or an ODCS `classification` of `pii`, `personal`, `gdpr` or `sensitive`), and **classified** when its `classification` is anything but `public`.

```yaml
schema:
  - name: orders
    properties:
      - name: customer_email
        logicalType: string
        classification: confidential
        customProperties: [{ property: personalData, value: true }]
```

The product page (_Overview_ – the output ports' bindings, the version each is held to or the current one), an element's page (the output bindings that expose it) and a data object's page (the output ports that expose it, declared or through their elements) list such fields per contract version, with the ports and environments it is bound to – `GET /api/data-products/:id/personal-fields?environment=`, `/api/data-elements/:id/personal-fields`, `/api/data-objects/:id/personal-fields`. A contract you may not see is **counted, never named**, and its fields are not shown; ports of products you may not see are not named. In the scorecard the personal fields are evidence of _confidentiality_: personal fields while none of the product's exposed data objects holds personal data make it _partial_ (the product does not say what it carries).

## Costs

**Showback, not chargeback** ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) part 11): a product's costs are an attribution of the cost lines Steward already reads ([COSTS.md](COSTS.md)) – never booked, never part of a cost plan, and the sum over products is not a budget. Platform plans keep their whole bill ([ADR 0032](adr/0032-cost-attribution-platform-plans.md)).

- **Matches** say which lines of a platform a product takes, per environment: by **resource id** (a Confluent cluster `lkc-…` or connector `lcc-…`, an AWS resource), **sub-account** (a Snowflake account, a Confluent environment), **service name**, a **tag** (key and value) or the **application** the lines are mapped to. A match on an **element** – a topic's cluster, a connector that runs a step – is inherited by every product the element is a member of, in the element's environment. Who may change the product (or the element) adds and removes matches, on platforms they may see; at most 1,000 matches per instance.
- **Direct and shared lines.** A line one product matches (in one environment) is its **direct** cost. A line several match is **shared** and split by the **allocation** of its match: **equal** between the products (and environments) that match it, or **by weight** – a percentage per product, split equally between its environments. Weights add up to at most 100 % (`DATA_COST_ALLOCATION`); what no product takes is shown as **not attributed**. When different matches claim one line, the most specific decides (resource, tag, application, service, sub-account). Amounts are split in whole millionths of the currency by the largest remainder, so the parts never add up to more than the line. Since 0.24.0 a line can also be split **by measured use** ([below](#measured-use)).
- **Who sets the split:** who may **manage the platform** – splitting a platform's bill is the platform team's statement – in a signed-in session (no API token), audited as `dataproduct.cost_allocation_changed` on the platform with the rule and the weights before and after. No step-up: an allocation grants nobody sight of anything. Weights of products the manager may not see stay as they are; such products are counted, never named.
- **What a reader sees** (ADR 0032's rule): a platform's part only where they see that platform's amounts – its managers and owning teams' members, administrators and auditors, everybody else who may see the platform unless _Hide amounts from viewers_ is on; viewers see no platforms. A part they may not see is left out of the lines and the totals and marks the month **incomplete** (`amountsHidden`) – never a figure from which the hidden part could be computed. Allocation weights and what is not attributed are shown to who sees the platform's amounts. A match on a platform the reader may not see shows neither the platform nor its value.
- **Value beside cost:** the product's value statement, tier and optional yearly amount stand next to its costs – no ratio is computed, nothing is netted.
- **Snowflake** bills per account and service type: a Snowflake-based product has costs through a dedicated account, tagged FOCUS lines, an equal or weighted share of an account, or – since 0.24.0 – its **measured use** of the account's warehouses or query tags.

### Measured use

Since 0.24.0 ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) part 11, phase 2) who may manage the platform can split a shared line **by measured use** – _Change the split_ on a product's _Costs_ tab, rule _By measured use_ (`PUT /api/data-products/cost-allocations` with `rule: measured`, `measure` and per product its `subjects`):

| What is measured (`measure`)                           | Read from                                                                                                                                                | Subjects per product                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Snowflake credits per warehouse (`snowflakeWarehouse`) | `SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.WAREHOUSE_METERING_HISTORY` (`CREDITS_USED` per warehouse and month)                                                            | its warehouses                                                   |
| Snowflake credits per query tag (`snowflakeQueryTag`)  | `SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY` (`CREDITS_ATTRIBUTED_COMPUTE` per query tag and month); the account's warehouse credits as the total | its query tags                                                   |
| Confluent bytes per topic (`confluentTopic`)           | the Confluent Cloud Metrics API, `io.confluent.kafka.server/received_bytes` per topic of the cluster, by day                                             | its topics – none given: the topics that are its member elements |

- **Read daily** by the job `data-products`, for the current and the previous month, per platform, measure and cluster, as the person who last set the allocation – their **use** right on the platform is checked before every read, a read that fails keeps the quantities read before and shows why on the allocation. Every request goes through the platform's client and so the outbound policy: the SQL API with metadata views only (the declared tags are bind parameters, never part of a statement) and, for Confluent, `POST /v2/metrics/cloud/query` on the Metrics API (`https://api.telemetry.confluent.cloud`, or the platform variable `metricsUrl`) with the platform's Cloud API key – which needs the _MetricsViewer_ role, and the Metrics API's address among the platform's trusted origins. A Confluent cluster's lines that belong to a Kafka platform (variable `confluentClusterId`) are measured through the Confluent Cloud platform that read them. Only the declared subjects and each scope's total are kept (`dp_cost_usage`), never query text or anything else of the views.
- **Priced with the price books** ([PRICE-BOOKS.md](PRICE-BOOKS.md)): where the platform's contracted price book has a price for the measure in the line's currency – the price per credit (`compute-credit`, also the Snowflake fallback's price) or Confluent's `KAFKA_NETWORK_WRITE` per GB – a product takes its quantity × that price (exact, in millionths); without a price it takes its **share of the measured total** of the line (its credits of all the account's warehouse credits, its bytes of the cluster's). Priced query tags leave the warehouses' idle time unattributed; priced bytes only the network part of a cluster's line.
- **Never above 100 %:** parts that would add up to more than the line are scaled down to it; what no product takes is shown as not attributed. Each line says how its share was found (_Snowflake credits per warehouse · priced with the price book_). Products a manager may not see keep their subjects; amounts the reader may not see keep the month _incomplete_ – the measured figures with them.

### Estimates

Since 0.24.0 an **estimate** of a service or a platform ([PRICE-BOOKS.md](PRICE-BOOKS.md#estimates)) can be _for_ a data product the estimate's editor may see (_For a data product_ on the estimate's page; `for: { kind: dataProduct, id }`). The product's _Costs_ tab then shows **Estimated against attributed**: the estimates made for it – as their owner's plan lets the reader read them; one they may not read is neither listed nor counted – and per month and currency the expected amount with its range beside what is attributed, with the difference and whether the month fell within the range (`GET /api/data-products/:id/estimates?environment=&months=`). A product is never the target of a plan line: lines from such an estimate belong to the service's or platform's plan only. The MCP tool `get_data_product_costs` returns the estimates and _estimated against attributed_ too, and the dry run `calculate_cost_estimate` takes `compareWith: { dataProductId }` to set its variants beside a similar product's attributed monthly average and estimates.

`GET /api/data-products/:id/costs?environment=&months=` (production first; 1–24 months, default 12): per month the lines – platform, direct or share, the rule, the share, the amount and the match – totals per currency, the flags, the product's own and inherited matches and its value. `POST /api/data-products/:id/costs/matches`, `DELETE …/matches/:matchId`; `GET|POST /api/data-elements/:id/cost-matches`, `DELETE …/:matchId`; `GET /api/data-products/cost-allocations?platformId=` (shared lines with their products, visible ones named, hidden ones counted) and `PUT /api/data-products/cost-allocations` (`{ platformId, kind, value, tagKey, rule: equal | weight, weights: [{ productId, pct }] }`). The tab costs a fixed number of statements whatever the number of products, matches and lines (one grouped read of the lines per chunk of platforms).

## Scorecard

Computed on read per product and environment (production first), in bulk, never typed (decision 9) – `GET /api/data-products/:id/scorecard?environment=`. Each part has a state – _met_, _partial_, _not met_, _no evidence_, _not applicable_ – and its **evidence** (i18n keys `evidence.<part>.<fact>` with counts and statuses, never the name of a contract, check or product the reader may not see):

| Part               | Met when                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Documented**     | Description, product owner, domain, type and at least one exposed data object                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Rated**          | All three CIA dimensions rated (none waiting for a re-rating) and at least the floor of the exposed data objects – below with a reason counts                                                                                                     |
| **Ports**          | Every output port has a binding in the environment                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Contracted**     | Every output port's binding has a data contract that is lint clean (the version it is held to) and whose last test passed                                                                                                                         |
| **Observed**       | Every element of the output bindings has a linked freshness check                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Healthy**        | The product's monitor in the environment is up (degraded: partial); without a monitor, the output elements' worst health                                                                                                                          |
| **Objectives met** | Over the last 30 days, with 30 days of data: the monitor's uptime at least the availability objective; the freshness checks green in at least that share of their hourly samples (99 % without an availability objective). No objectives: not met |
| **Dependencies**   | No input port consumes a deprecated or retired port                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Costs known**    | Cost matches exist in the environment (own or inherited)                                                                                                                                                                                          |

**ISO/IEC 25012** dimensions with the evidence Steward has: _currentness_ (the freshness checks' health), _availability_ (the monitor's 30-day uptime against the objective), _compliance_ (the contract tests), _traceability_ (the steps lead each output element back to an input or to data from outside the product, within four steps), _understandability_ (definitions of the exposed data objects), _confidentiality_ (the rating against the floor, and – 0.24.0 – the personal fields of the bound contracts and whether a port access review was approved within 12 months), and since 0.24.0 _accuracy_, _completeness_ and _consistency_ from the [quality results](#quality-results) of the output elements (the latest result of each test in 30 days: any failed – not met, any warning – partial, all passed – met); the other dimensions say _no evidence yet_. _Objectives met_ also judges the completeness objective of each output port (the share of passing completeness results over 30 days).

### Scorecard history

Once a day (the job `data-products-scorecard`, asked hourly) Steward stores every product's scorecard per environment – its maturity and the state of each part and dimension, no evidence, no names (`dp_scorecard_snapshots`) – and keeps **400 days**; quality results older than 400 days go at the same time. The product's _Overview_ shows the **maturity over 90 days** as a step line (one value per day, the levels 0–5 on the axis, the day and level on hover) with a **table** beside it (_Picture_ / _Table_; phones start with the table) – `GET /api/data-products/:id/scorecard/history?environment=&days=` (7–400 days; a fixed number of statements whatever the range). Snapshots are taken without a reader – a check you may not see counts by its status, as on every page – and shown only to who may see the product.

### The report section

Scheduled reports ([REPORTS.md](REPORTS.md)) have a section **Data products** – the module's report section (ADR 0034: recipients who do not use the module get the report without it): per product the owner of the schedule may see (at most 300, by name; or the products chosen), in its production environment or the one configured, the maturity at the start of the period (the snapshot of that day or the one before) and now, the scorecard parts met, the healthy part, the quality tests of its output elements in the period (passed and failed, without the volume checks) and the versions with breaking changes of its bound contracts the owner may see; at a glance the products and failed tests. Configuration: `{ "productIds": null | [ids], "environment": null | "prod" }`.

**Maturity** follows: **1** documented and rated, **2** observed, **3** contracted, **4** objectives met for 30 days, **5** a valid [endorsement](#endorsement-and-maturity-5) on top of 4; each level needs the ones below. Health comes from the module's checks (stored check states – a page load never calls a platform).

## Checks and health

Steward watches data products with its **monitors** ([MONITORS.md](MONITORS.md)): checks are linked to elements, a product gets a monitor with root causes along its pipeline, and health is read from the stored check states – a page never calls a platform.

### The check type _Data element_

The monitor check type `data-element` reads an element of the catalogue on its platform with the **monitor owner's use right** and the platform's default identity, like every platform check; the owner must see the element. It reads **metadata only** – never a data value, never a `SELECT` on a table (decision 13 of the ADR).

| Mode                 | Snowflake                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Kafka                                                                                                                                                                                                 | SQL database                             |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **freshness**        | table or view: `LAST_ALTERED` of `<db>.INFORMATION_SCHEMA.TABLES`; dynamic table: the data timestamp of its last successful refresh (`DYNAMIC_TABLE_REFRESH_HISTORY`; a failed last refresh is a warning); task: its last successful run (`TASK_HISTORY`, the last 7 days; a failed last run is a warning); schema or database: the newest table (_any_) or the oldest (_every_) | topic: the newest message's time (Kafka 3.0+; else when the end offsets last moved), optionally a consumer group's lag (degraded above `maxLag`); bundle: its topics, the newest (_any_) or every one | – (link a check of your own)             |
| **schema**           | `INFORMATION_SCHEMA.COLUMNS` of the table against the contract                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | the subject `<topic>-value` (latest, or a pinned `schemaVersion`): Avro record fields or JSON Schema properties; Protobuf is not compared (unknown)                                                   | the latest schema snapshot (no new read) |
| **volume** (0.24.0)  | table or dynamic table: `ROW_COUNT` (or `BYTES`) of `<db>.INFORMATION_SCHEMA.TABLES` – the same catalogue row as freshness                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | topic: its messages – the sum of its partitions' end offsets (ListOffsets; no consumer group, no message read)                                                                                        | –                                        |
| **quality** (0.24.0) | table, view or dynamic table: the newest result of each Data Metric Function in the window, from `SNOWFLAKE.LOCAL.DATA_QUALITY_MONITORING_RESULTS` (`qualitySource: dmf`); or the recorded [quality results](#quality-results)                                                                                                                                                   | the recorded quality results                                                                                                                                                                          | the recorded quality results             |

Fields: `element` (Steward's id), `mode`, `maxAgeMinutes` (default 1440), `each` (`any` or `every`, for a schema, database or bundle), `group` and `maxLag` (a Kafka topic), `schemaVersion` (Kafka schema mode), `failAs` (down or degraded), `warningAs` (degraded or up); volume: `measure` (`rows` – a topic's messages – or `bytes`), `minRows`, `minGrowth`, `maxChangePct`, `windowMinutes` (default 60); quality: `qualitySource` (`results`, the default, or `dmf`), `withinHours` (default 48), `metrics` (at most 50 limits: `{ metric, column, min, max }`).

- **Volume against the reading before.** Each reading is kept (at most one per 5 minutes, for 8 days – `dp_volume_readings`) and compared with the newest reading at least `windowMinutes` old: fewer than `minRows`, grown by less than `minGrowth` (1 finds a load that stopped) or changed by more than `maxChangePct` in either direction is `failAs`. The first reading only knows `minRows`. Row counts and sizes are metadata (decision 13); each verdict is also a completeness quality result of the element.
- **Data Metric Functions** are Snowflake's own quality measurements, scheduled on a table by its owners: Steward reads their results (a view of measured values – counts and percentages, never the table's rows) with a bound table name, at most 500 metrics, and judges them by the check's `metrics` – without any, the null, blank and duplicate counts and percentages (`NULL_COUNT`, `NULL_PERCENT`, `BLANK_COUNT`, `BLANK_PERCENT`, `DUPLICATE_COUNT`) must be 0, other metrics need a limit. The platform's role needs the application role `SNOWFLAKE.DATA_QUALITY_MONITORING_VIEWER` ([SNOWFLAKE.md](SNOWFLAKE.md#data-products-discovery)). Each judged metric becomes a quality result (source `dmf`).
- **Recorded results** (`qualitySource: results`) are read from Steward's own database – no platform is read, and saving the check needs no use right on one: the latest result of each test of the element in the window (dbt, contract tests, DMF; not the volume checks) – a failed test is `failAs`, a warning `warningAs`, none is unknown.

### Quality results

Steward keeps **quality results** of elements (0.24.0, `dp_quality_results`): a test or metric (`not_null_orders_id`, `SNOWFLAKE.CORE.NULL_COUNT(EMAIL)`, `field_required:orders.id`), its source – `dbt` (tests from `run_results.json`), `dmf` (Data Metric Functions), `contract` (a test of a bound [data contract](DATA-CONTRACTS.md#testing)), `check` (the volume mode) –, its result (passed, warning, failed, error), a measured value (a count, never a data value), when it was measured and the run it belongs to. Each is mapped to an **ISO/IEC 25012 dimension**: the ODCS quality `dimension` (accuracy; conformity → accuracy; completeness; coverage → completeness; consistency; uniqueness → consistency; timeliness → currentness), else the test's name (`not_null`, null counts, row counts → completeness; `unique`, duplicates, `relationships` → consistency; `accepted_values`, ranges, patterns, types → accuracy). At most one row per element, source, test and hour is kept (the newest of the hour), for 400 days.

- **One write function** – `recordQualityResults` in `server/src/dataproducts/quality.ts` – stores them for every source: the dbt import, the contract test hook, the checks. Unknown elements are skipped and counted; the caller checks rights.
- An element's page shows its results of the last 30 days – per dimension the latest of each test, and the newest results (`GET /api/data-elements/:id/quality`) – for who may see the element.
- The scorecard reads them in bulk for its _accuracy_, _completeness_ and _consistency_ dimensions and the completeness objective ([Scorecard](#scorecard)); the report section counts them per product.

- **Freshness is metadata.** `LAST_ALTERED` also moves on DDL and background maintenance (automatic clustering): the check says _changed_, not _loaded_. For the newest value of a timestamp column, write a `database` check of your own (query mode) and link it to the element.
- **Schema** compares the element with the schema object of the ODCS contract its **output port** is bound to in the element's environment (the binding's version, else the current one) – matched by physical name, else by name, else the contract's only object – with the [contract comparison](DATA-CONTRACTS.md#breaking-changes) on names (case ignored), logical types and required fields: a field of the contract that is missing or of another type family **fails** (`failAs`); required ↔ nullable and columns the contract does not name are **warnings** (`warningAs`). Without a bound contract the check is unknown.
- **Saving or trying** the check needs the editor to see the element and use its platform (one they may not see answers like a missing one: `DATA_ELEMENT_NOT_FOUND`); a mode the element's kind does not support is refused (`DATA_ELEMENT_CHECK_MODE`). The editor offers the type while the module is on.

### Checks linked to an element

An element shows the checks that watch it (`GET /api/data-elements/{id}/checks`), with their stored status:

- **made** by _Create monitor_, **linked** by a person (_Link_ – who may change the element or a product it is part of; at most 100 per element), or **found** – a check whose fields name the element: a `data-element` check, a `snowflake` freshness or tasks check on its name, a `kafka` check on its topic. Found checks are offered until someone links them; _Create monitor_ links them.
- A check of a monitor the reader may not see is shown by **its status only** – _a check you may not see_: no monitor, check name, message or author.

### Health

- **An element:** the worst status of its linked checks that run (an enabled monitor and check; maintenance counts as up); checks the reader may not see count by their status. No running check: **No data** – never OK.
- **A product, per environment:** its monitor's status while that monitor runs (a disabled draft does not count yet); else the worst of its **output-port elements** there, **internal elements at most degrading** (a down internal element makes the product degraded); input elements are other products' or sources'. Nothing linked: **No data**.
- **Worst to best:** _down_, _degraded_, _unknown_, _blocked_, _maintenance_, _up_, _off_, _No data_ – the monitors' order (a switched-off check does not make a product off) with _No data_ last, as any status of a check says more than none. One order for product health, the scorecard's _healthy_ part, collapsed parts of the pictures, the legends and the list's sort and filter (`DATA_HEALTH_ORDER`).
- Health is computed **in bulk** from the stored states: a fixed number of statements whatever the number of products (`GET /api/data-products/health?ids=…&environment=…` for a page, `GET /api/data-products/{id}/health` per environment, production first).

### Create monitor and Update monitor

_Create monitor_ on a product (`GET /api/data-products/{id}/monitor` shows the proposal, `POST` applies it; per environment, production first) makes a **disabled draft monitor** for review – Steward never enables it:

- a `data-element` **freshness** check per element that has one: output-port elements are **critical** with the port's freshness objective as maximum age (the binding's objective overrides the port's); the pipeline's internal and input elements only degrade the monitor (`aggregate.rule: critical`), with the smallest output objective (else a day);
- a **schema** check per element of an output binding with a contract, and a `data-contract` check in `findings` mode per contract (while the data contracts module is on);
- elements already watched elsewhere (linked or found) are **not repeated**, elements without a supported kind are left out, elements the person may not see are left out and counted, never named;
- **soft links along the steps**: an element's check → the next checked element downstream (through elements without a check of their own), so a stale raw table is the **root cause** of a stale mart and only the root cause alerts;
- at most 100 checks: beyond, the output ports and the layer before them first; the proposal says what was left out and why.

The product remembers the monitor per environment. **Update monitor** – the same action once the product has a monitor – proposes the checks for elements added since, with soft links to the existing checks, and adds them when a person applies it (it never rewires what is there). It needs the right to change the product and to create (or edit) the monitor; audited as `dataproduct.monitor_created`.

### Contract drafts from an element

A contract draft from an element (`POST /api/data-contracts/draft` with `{ "source": "element", "elementId": … }`; in the app _Draft a contract_ on the element's page – the draft is shown to review and becomes a contract only when you create it) is made from a Snowflake table's columns (`INFORMATION_SCHEMA.COLUMNS`, read now, with a `snowflake` server), a Kafka topic's latest `<topic>-value` schema (with a `kafka` server), or a database table's latest schema snapshot – for whoever sees the element and may use its platform.

## Pictures

Three pictures show how data moves – each with a **table** that says the same in words (_Picture_ / _Table_ above it; phones start with the table, the picture is a tap away), a **legend**, a **search** that finds and selects a part, _Fit to screen_, and **export** as SVG or PNG (for documents: the theme's colours, statuses in words, no animation). They follow the conventions of the plant registry's pictures:

- **Pipeline of a product** (`/data-products/{id}/views/pipeline`, the product's _Pipeline_ tab): its elements in columns by role – input ports, internal elements by layer (_source_, _raw_, _staged_, _curated_, _mart_), output ports – inside the product's area, and the elements outside it that its steps read from (upstream, left) or write to (downstream, right), greyed and named with their product. Steps are labelled edges (a step with many inputs and outputs is a node of its own); ports are named on their elements. Switch the **environment** above the picture (production first; `?environment=` in the address).
- **Product map** (`/data-products/map`): the products in their domains and sub-domains, an edge from the product of an output port to every product whose input port consumes it (dashed and red when that port is deprecated or retired). Filter by domain (with its sub-domains), type, lifecycle and environment – the filters are in the address; the products a match depends on or feeds are drawn greyed.
- **Lineage of an element** (`/data-elements/{id}/views/lineage`): the elements 1 to 4 steps upstream and downstream (`?depth=`, 2 by default) across products – products as areas –, the steps between them, and plant registry data collections that write to an element (named where you may see the site).

**Health** on a node comes only from checks linked to the element or the product's monitor; without one it says _No data_ – never OK. It is shown as an icon and a word with its colour, never colour alone.

**Keyboard and screen readers.** Every part is a stop of its own with its kind, name, role, layer and health in words; **Enter** opens its page, **Space** (or a click) selects it and highlights everything upstream and downstream of it – _Show only what is connected_ keeps only that (on the map: what depends on a product and what it depends on) – and **Escape** clears the selection. The moving dashes on the edges show the direction of the data; they stop with your system's _reduce motion_ setting or with _Animation off_, and the arrows stay.

**What you may not see** stays in the picture without a name or an id – _An element you may not see_ – so the picture stays true; a note counts such elements and products.

**Large pictures.** A response holds at most 2,000 elements (the rest are counted). The pipeline's picture draws at most 150 parts: beyond, elements collapse by their parent (_40 elements of STAGED_), then a column becomes one part; the map draws at most 300 products, beyond, each domain becomes one part with its count; a lineage stops at 200 elements (left out counted per direction). The table always lists everything the response holds. Each picture is read with a fixed number of statements whatever its size.

## Who may do what

| Who                                             | Rights                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Every working role (items without owning teams) | See products, data objects, elements and domains                            |
| Designers                                       | Create and change them; delete, owning teams and retiring (_manage_)        |
| Product owners, domain owners                   | See, change and manage their product or domain                              |
| Data owner and stewards                         | See their data object                                                       |
| With owning teams                               | Only members of those teams (with their roles), auditors and administrators |

| Shares of a product | View, Edit or Manage on that product (Manage: also its shares, owning teams, deleting and retiring) |

**Module access** ([ADR 0034](adr/0034-module-access.md)): the module is restrictable – an administrator gives it to selected teams and users under _Settings → Modules → Data products → Who may use it_ (administrators and auditors always use it). Who does not use it meets it like a switched-off module: its pages and routes answer 404 `MODULE_NO_ACCESS`, its MCP tools are not listed, its notifications are not sent, links to its products and data objects are counted without a name, a share of a product is refused for them, and they have no right on any of its items – also as a product owner. Within the module the core roles give the module's roles: designer → **manager**, every other working role → **viewer** (team roles included). Since 0.23.0 ([MODULES.md](MODULES.md#module-roles)) an administrator also gives a person, a team or a single sign-on mapping a module role – **viewer**, **contributor** (creates products and data objects) or **manager** (changes and manages them, domains and layers); the highest counts, and a granted role works like an instance role: on items without owning teams that are not _Restricted_, and on the items of an owning team its members.

**Shares** ([ADR 0033](adr/0033-one-access-model.md)): a product's _Access_ tab gives a person, a team or a role View, Edit or Manage on it, optionally until a date; a share adds to what the owning teams and roles give, never takes away. A product switched to **Restricted** (administrators) gives the instance roles nothing – only its owning teams' members, its shares, its owners, auditors (View) and administrators reach it. Sharing needs Manage; a share that gives someone a right they did not have asks for the confirmation (`access.grant`) and is recorded as an access change (`access.share_added` …). Data objects, elements and domains keep their owning teams; shares of them follow in phase 2.

Team scope applies everywhere: what a reader may not see is neither listed nor counted nor named (_Part of 1 product you may not see_). Naming an owner from outside the owning teams asks for a confirmation (control point `access.grant`) and is recorded as an access change.

## File format, import and export

Steward's own format holds domains, data objects, the element catalogue and data products with their ports, members and steps – YAML or JSON in snake_case, `version: 1`, described by the JSON Schema [data-products-1.schema.json](data-products/data-products-1.schema.json) (also at `GET /api/data-products/schema`). A file holds any of `domains`, `data_objects`, `elements` and `data_products`, or a single `domain:`, `data_object:` or `data_product:`; several files are one import. Since 0.24.0 an **ODPS** document is read too ([ODPS](#odps)).

```yaml
version: 1
elements:
  - { platform: Snowflake (demo), kind: snowflake.table, qualified_name: SHOP.CURATED.ORDERS, environment: prod, layer: curated }
data_products:
  - key: orders
    name: Orders
    type: sourceAligned
    domain: sales
    owners: [user:dana]
    members:
      - element: { platform: Kafka (demo), kind: kafka.topic, qualified_name: orders.v1, environment: prod }
        role: input
    ports:
      - direction: output
        name: orders
        version: v2
        data_objects: [order]
        objectives: { freshness_minutes: 60 }
        bindings:
          - environment: prod
            elements: [{ platform: Snowflake (demo), kind: snowflake.table, qualified_name: SHOP.CURATED.ORDERS, environment: prod }]
            contract: shop-orders # the data contract's ODCS id
        consumers:
          - { kind: team, subject: team:finance, purpose: Monthly close }
    steps:
      - name: Curate orders
        kind: curate
        runner: { kind: tool, tool: dbt }
        inputs: [{ platform: Snowflake (demo), kind: snowflake.table, qualified_name: SHOP.RAW.ORDERS, environment: prod }]
        outputs: [{ platform: Snowflake (demo), kind: snowflake.table, qualified_name: SHOP.CURATED.ORDERS, environment: prod }]
```

**Identity.** Domains, data objects and products by `key`; elements by platform (its name, or `platform_id`), kind, qualified name (case does not matter) and environment; ports by direction, name and version; bindings by environment; consumers by kind and whom they name; steps by name within their product (a step is documented with the product whose element it writes). Platforms, applications, services and processes are named by their names, data contracts by their ODCS id, other products by their key.

**Never in a file:** credentials, platform variables, discovery run-as users, readouts, check states and costs. Owning teams are not part of the format either – a new entry gets the importer's teams, as when it is made on a page.

### Export

**In the app:** _Import and export_ on the products list downloads everything you may see – or the domain the list is filtered by – as YAML or JSON; a product's menu (⋯) downloads the product as YAML, JSON or ODPS.

_Export_ writes a product (`GET /api/data-products/:id/export`), a domain with its sub-domains (`GET /api/data-products/export?domain=`) or everything you may see (`GET /api/data-products/export`) – optionally for one environment (`?environment=prod`) and as JSON (`?format=json`). Everything means the domains, data objects and products, the elements they name, and the entered and imported elements of the catalogue; discovered elements no product names come back with their discovery. At most 20,000 elements are written (the header `x-steward-elements-left-out` counts the rest). Empty values are left out.

The file holds only what you may see: a domain, successor, data object, contract, consumer or element you may not see is left out of the entry that names it – imported again, the entry keeps what Steward has there.

### Import

**In the app:** _Import and export → Import files…_ on the products list (who may create products): choose the files (at most 500 and 8 MB together) and the mode, **Preview** shows the counts, the problems that stop the import, hints, and every entry it adds, changes (field by field) or skips; **Import** sends the preview's fingerprint, so a change in between is refused. Owners named from outside the owning teams ask for your confirmation as on the forms.

`POST /api/data-products/import/preview` shows what an import adds, changes (field by field: `ports[output orders v2].bindings[prod].objectives`), leaves unchanged and skips, and the problems that stop it – the format, names that are not known, and what the services would refuse (the preview runs the same writes in a transaction that is rolled back). `POST /api/data-products/import` imports, in one transaction:

| Mode             | What exists                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `add` (Add only) | is skipped; new entries are added                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `update`         | takes what the files say; **a key a file leaves out keeps Steward's value**, list items (ports, members, consumers, steps …) are matched and added or changed, never removed                                                     |
| `replace`        | (0.24.0) as `update`, and the files are **the whole of an earlier import of the same `origin`**: its entries the files no longer hold are removed, and the products' members, ports, steps and consumers the files leave out too |

An export imported again changes nothing.

**Origin and _Replace an earlier import_** (0.24.0). An import may name an **origin** (`origin`: letters, digits and `. _ - : /`, at most 100 – _Origin_ in the dialog): every entry its files name is kept under it (Git sync uses `git:<source>`). An import in mode `replace` names the origin it replaces and is the whole of it:

- **Entries** of the origin – products, data objects, domains, elements – that the files no longer hold are **removed**: products first, then elements and data objects, then domains (sub-domains first). A discovered element is discovery's and never removed; an element still part of a product that stays and a domain still used are kept, with a hint.
- **Lists**: the members, ports, steps and consumers of the products the files hold are the files' – an item the file leaves out is removed (a list the file leaves out entirely is empty).
- **Earlier origins are suggested** in the dialog's _Origin_ with how many of their entries you see and when they were last imported (`GET /api/data-products/import-origins`, session-only, the 50 newest); an origin none of whose entries you may see is neither listed nor counted.
- **Never what you may not see:** an entry of the origin, a member on a platform or a consumer you may not see is kept and only counted (_3 kept: not yours to see_), never named – so is an output port the files leave out while it has consumers you may not see (removing it would remove them). Each removal needs the delete right the page would need (_Manage_); a refusal is a problem of the preview.
- **The preview says it all** before anything happens: every entry it removes (action _Remove_), every list item (`members[…]: present → removed`), the counts, and – when more than one entry or list item is removed – that importing asks for the step-up **`dataproduct.bulkChange`** ([ASSURANCE.md](ASSURANCE.md)). Audited in `dataproduct.imported` with the mode, the origin and what was removed. _Import_ then asks once more, naming the entries it removes (the first ten) and counting the list items.

- **Your rights, the module's rules.** Every write goes through the services of the pages: creating needs the module's contributor or manager role, changing an entry its edit right; the limits, the lifecycle rules (an _active_ product needs an owner and an output port), the bindings' environments and the rating floor apply; naming an owner from outside the owning teams asks for your confirmation (`access.grant`). Lifecycle, rating and successor are written last, after ports and bindings.
- **What you may not see does not exist** for an import: a key, element, platform, contract, application or product you may not see is refused like an unknown one, never named; a key taken by a product you may not see answers _taken_, as on the page.
- **Elements** a file adds are marked _imported_; for elements that exist, an import changes the people's fields (display name, layer, description, type label, API reference) – not the parent, the flags or what discovery reads.
- **Limits** as the other YAML importers: 8 MB and 500 files together, keys bounded before parsing, duplicate keys refused, at most 100 aliases and 12 levels, 20,000 elements per import.
- **No credentials.** A value under a key that looks secret (password, token, API key …) or a password in an address is refused with its place – the value itself is never shown or kept.
- **Session only.** Imports are made by a person signed in (API tokens are refused); the preview's `fingerprint` passed to the import refuses it when the data changed since (`DATA_PRODUCT_IMPORT_CHANGED`).
- **Audit:** each write as on the pages, and `dataproduct.imported` with the mode, the counts and each file's SHA-256.

### ODPS

`GET /api/data-products/:id/odps` (and `GET /api/v1/data-products/{id}/odps` as JSON) exports a product as an [Open Data Product Standard](https://github.com/bitol-io/open-data-product-standard) v1.0.0 document, checked in the tests against the official JSON Schema (vendored with its Apache 2.0 licence in `server/assets/odps/`):

| ODPS                               | From                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `id`, `name`, `version`, `status`  | key, name, version, lifecycle                                                                                                                     |
| `domain`                           | the domain's name (its key under `customProperties`)                                                                                              |
| `description`                      | `purpose` and `usage` (the description)                                                                                                           |
| `team`, `support`                  | owning teams and product owners (`role: productOwner`); the support channel with its address                                                      |
| `outputPorts`                      | name, type, version, description and `contractId` – the bound contract's ODCS id in one environment (production unless `?environment=`)           |
| `inputPorts`                       | input ports that consume an output port with a contract (ODPS requires a `contractId`); the others under `customProperties.inputsWithoutContract` |
| `tags`, `authoritativeDefinitions` | tags, external references                                                                                                                         |
| `managementPorts`                  | the product's page and its `/api/v1` address, where `SM_PUBLIC_URL` is set                                                                        |
| `customProperties`                 | Steward's id, type, rating and classification, criticality, support tier, sunset date, data objects, environments                                 |

**ODPS import** (0.24.0): an ODPS v1 document (`apiVersion: v1.…`, `kind: DataProduct`) given to the import among the files is checked against the vendored schema and read as a `data_product:` of the format – so it gets the same preview, modes, rights and audit (the preview counts the ODPS documents). It creates or updates the product (`id` → key, name, version, `status` → lifecycle, domain – Steward's key from `customProperties` or a visible domain of that name –, description with purpose, usage and limitations, tags, authoritative definitions as external references, team members with the role `productOwner` and owner teams as product owners, support) and its ports: an output port's `contractId` binds it to the data contract of that ODCS id in the environment the document names under `customProperties` (as Steward exports it), else production; an input port's source is the output port its `customProperties` name, else the visible output port bound to the same contract, else a text. Steward's extras under `customProperties` (type, criticality, support tier, sunset date, rating, data objects, environments, objectives) are read back, so an exported product imports unchanged. What cannot be mapped is a hint of the preview; ODPS carries no elements or steps.

## Git sync of products and data objects

Since 0.24.0 ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) part 16) a Git sync source ([GIT-SYNC.md](GIT-SYNC.md)) can bring in **data products** and **data objects** (_What to sync_: _Data products_, _Data objects_): one entry per file in the module's format – `version: 1` with one `data_product:` or one `data_object:` (a `kind: dataProduct` / `kind: dataObject` at the top is allowed) – matched by `key`. The sync engine's rules apply: every change is a pending update, accepted by who may change the entry (or create one: the module's contributors and managers), and a change made in Steward since the last sync is flagged before it is overwritten. Accepting imports the file as _add and update_, as the reviewer, under the origin `git:<source id>` – the product's members, ports, steps and consumers are the file's (an item the file leaves out is removed; what the reviewer may not see stays). Elements are not synced (they are discovered or imported): a product file names them, and one Steward does not know is a problem of the update. Libraries carry no data products.

## Proposals from assistants

Since 0.24.0 ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) part 14) an assistant connected through MCP proposes new or changed **products, elements, ports, steps, data objects and domains** – documenting a product from a Snowflake schema it read with `search_data_elements`, or from a dbt manifest it has locally. `get_data_products_format` returns the JSON Schema; `propose_data_product_changes` takes a document of the format (with a title and a rationale; large ones in up to 20 parts of 48 KB, together one import). Steward previews it with the import's preview as the token owner – **nothing is written** – and keeps it under **AI requests** ([MCP.md](MCP.md)), where a person who uses the module sees what it adds and changes (field by field, the preview of now as them) and **applies** it – the import, mode _add and update_, in one transaction, with their rights – or rejects it. An assistant never applies anything. A proposal only adds and changes (nothing is removed, no product retired), values that look like credentials refuse it with their places, a document that would not apply is refused with its problems; a preview that changed since it was proposed needs the reviewer's confirmation. Entries the reviewer may not see are left out of the review and counted. Audited as `dataproduct.proposal_created`, `dataproduct.proposal_applied` (with the import's `dataproduct.imported`) and `dataproduct.proposal_rejected`, besides the `mcp.request_*` entries.

## Ossie export

Since 0.24.0 ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) part 2, decision 5) the data objects – the module's business vocabulary – export as an **Apache Ossie semantic model**, like Steward's own model ([SEMANTIC-MODEL.md](SEMANTIC-MODEL.md)), in the revisions 0.1.1 and 0.2.0.dev0: _Ossie export_ on the data objects list, `GET /api/data-objects/semantic-model?version=&format=` and `GET /api/v1/data-objects/semantic-model` (JSON or YAML). Each data object is a dataset (its key with `_` for `.` and `-` as the name – a trailing `_` where two keys would give the same name –, `steward.data_objects.<key>` as the logical source, its name and definition as the description, its synonyms in `ai_context`) with its **key attributes as fields** (a personal one marked in a custom extension) and Steward's facts – classification, personal data, lifecycle, domain, its page – in a custom extension; its primary key is the attribute named `id`, `<key>_id` or `<key>Id`. **Relationships** follow from the attributes: one named after another object's key (`customer`, `customer_id`, `customerId`) refers to that object. Unlike Steward's own model it holds only the data objects the reader may see – a relationship to one they may not see is left out.

## Performance

The reads cost a **fixed number of database statements** whatever the size of the catalogue – one per kind of fact for a page (IN lists chunked by 500 ids), one per depth level and direction for lineage, never one per row – and the tests hold them to it (`server/test/dataproducts-perf.test.ts`; the product page at most 20 and the scorecard at most 37 beyond what every request costs – 0.25.0, from 26 and 51). The test writes the volume of the ADR – 250,000 elements, 2,000 products, 30,000 memberships, 10,000 steps, 5,000 check links – with `SM_PERF=1` (`cd server && SM_PERF=1 npx vitest run test/dataproducts-perf.test.ts`): the product list, the catalogue (prefix and text search), a product page, its members, ports, pipeline, scorecard and costs, the list's facts and lineage four steps deep each answer within 300 ms on SQLite; the product map of all 2,000 products within a second. Without it, a tenth of the volume and bounds that catch a read that scans or loops.

- **The list:** a page of 100 products, then its health (`GET /api/data-products/health?ids=`) and its maturity and last month's costs (`GET /api/data-products/facts?ids=&environment=`) in one request each.
- **The catalogue:** keyset paging by name; prefix search by index; text search a bounded scan with counts capped at 10,000.
- **Discovery:** batches of 250 elements per transaction with a turn of the event loop between them; 50,000 objects within 60 s ([Discovery](#discovery)).

## Consumers, access and governance

Since 0.24.0 ([ADR 0036](adr/0036-data-products-module.md) phase 2): who really reads a port beside who is registered, access requests and periodic port access reviews, promotion between environments, endorsement and the GDPR Art. 30 view. Every change below is made in a signed-in session (API tokens are refused, `SESSION_REQUIRED`) and is in the audit log.

### Declared against actual consumers

_Ports → an output port → Who really reads_ shows what the platforms say about who read the port in an environment, beside the registered consumers:

| Platform      | What Steward reads                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Snowflake** | `SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.ACCESS_HISTORY` (Enterprise edition, up to 3 hours behind): the users who read the port's bound tables and views (a schema or database: everything below it) in the last 30 days, with the role of each query (`QUERY_HISTORY.ROLE_NAME`) – aggregated per user and per role into reads and the last read. _Reads_ counts queries per object: a query that reads two of the port's objects counts once for each, so a user's or role's reads over several objects can be more than the queries they ran – a measure of use, not a query count. No query text is read or kept. One fixed, read-only statement; the object names go in as bound JSON arrays. |
| **Kafka**     | The consumer groups with committed offsets on the port's topics (a bundle's topics one by one) and their state – the metadata and offset requests the `kafka` check already makes, never a message                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

- **Read now** – who may change the product and use one of its bound platforms; the read covers the platforms that person may use, with their use right (checked again), through the outbound policy – the others keep what their last reader found. The job `data-products-governance` reads each platform of the port again daily as the person who read it last (their use right checked again before every read); a person who lost it gets _failed_ with the reason.
- **Not available:** an account without `ACCESS_HISTORY` (Standard edition) or a role that may not read it answers _not available_ – the port's registrations then stay _not known_, never _silent_. A failed read keeps what the last good one found.
- **Matching:** a registration names the **platform identities** it reads with – `user:NAME` (a Snowflake user), `role:NAME` (a Snowflake role), `group:NAME` (a Kafka consumer group); a registered _user_ is also matched by their user name and e-mail address. A registration is **seen** when one of them read, **registered but silent** when it names identities and the read succeeded without them, **not known** otherwise; a reader no registration matches is **not registered**.
- **Who sees what:** who may see the product sees the comparison; the names of readers only on platforms they may see (the others are counted). The registrations' identities are changed by who may change the product; the [file format](#file-format-import-and-export) carries them as a consumer's `identities` (exported with the product, imported with it – a file that leaves them out keeps them).
- _API:_ `GET /api/data-products/{id}/ports/{portId}/actual?environment=`, `POST …/actual/read` `{ environment }`, `PUT …/consumers/{consumerId}/identities` `{ identities }`.

**Snowflake setup:** the role Steward reads with needs the database role `SNOWFLAKE.GOVERNANCE_VIEWER` (or `IMPORTED PRIVILEGES` on the `SNOWFLAKE` database) to read `ACCESS_HISTORY` and `QUERY_HISTORY` ([SNOWFLAKE.md](SNOWFLAKE.md#data-products-discovery)). **Kafka:** _Describe_ on the consumer groups ([KAFKA.md](KAFKA.md#data-products-discovery)).

### Access requests

_Request access_ on an output port – anyone who may see the product – names the environment (one the port is bound in), who consumes (themselves, a team, or a text), the **purpose**, **until when**, an agreement and the platform identities that will read. The request waits on the product's _Access_ tab and under _Data → Access requests_ (_Waiting for me_, _Mine_, _All_); the people who decide are told (`dataproduct.access_requested`).

- **Who decides:** the product owners; without any, who manages the product (and administrators). Nobody decides their own request.
- **Four eyes** when the product's CIA rating is **high or above** in a dimension (the top two levels of a scale of three or more) when the request is made or decided: two different people approve; the second is told when the first approved.
- **Approving** widens access: it asks for the confirmation (control point `access.grant`) and is recorded as an access change (`access.data_port_consumer_granted`) beside `dataproduct.access_approved`. The last approval **registers the consumer** (purpose, until when as its end date, the agreement, the identities, the requester as its contact). **Refusing** needs a reason. The requester is told either way (`dataproduct.access_decided`) and may withdraw an open request.
- **Steward never grants on a platform.** The product's **access settings** (who manages the product) say what follows an approval: a **process** the platform team owns, started live as the approver with the request as its inputs (`dataProduct`, `port`, `portVersion`, `environment`, `consumer`, `identities`, `until`, `purpose`, `requestId`) – its own approvals apply and the approver needs its run right; and/or a **ticket source**: the approver gives the number of the platform team's ticket and it is recorded as a ticket reference linked to the product.
- At most 100 open requests per port; asking twice for the same is refused.
- _API:_ `POST /api/data-products/{id}/ports/{portId}/access-requests`, `GET /api/data-products/access-requests?scope=decide|mine|all&status=`, `GET /api/data-products/{id}/access-requests`, `GET /api/data-products/access-requests/{requestId}`, `POST …/{requestId}/decision` `{ decision: approve | refuse, comment, ticket }`, `POST …/{requestId}/withdraw`, `GET|PUT /api/data-products/{id}/access-settings` `{ processId, ticketSource, reviewMonths }`.

### Port access reviews

A **port access review** is the periodic review of who consumes an output port in an environment, built on the [person access reviews](COMPLIANCE.md#person-access-reviews)' way of working:

1. **Start** (who decides access requests of the product): what is found is **frozen** into the review – the registered consumers with what the platforms said of them (_seen_, _registered but silent_, _not known_), the readers without a registration, and – optionally – the **grants** of an [access check](COMPLIANCE.md#access-reconciliation) on the port's objects (read now as for person reviews, else its latest snapshot; a check scoped to the port's objects, such as a Snowflake check on `SHOP.SALES.ORDERS_V`, gives all its grants).
2. **Decide** each entry: keep or remove; a removal needs a reason.
3. **Submit** with every entry decided. With **four eyes** (the default) a second person – who decides for the product, an approver or an administrator, never the submitter nor who decided an entry – **approves** or sends it back with a comment.
4. **Approved** reviews are **sealed** (SHA-256 over the review and its decisions, in the audit log; _Verify the seal_ recomputes it) and registrations decided _remove_ end that day. Readers and grants to remove are the platform team's work: the review is the evidence. Each entry keeps what was frozen as facts – purpose, end date, identities and the names it was seen as of a registration; platform, objects, reads and the last read of a reader; the subject's type of a grant – shown in your language (`facts`; reviews started before kept their text in `detail`).

The product's **review interval** (access settings, 1–24 months) makes a review due that long after the last approved one (never reviewed: due now); `dataproduct.access_review_due` reminds who decides once per port, environment and due date, and the scorecard's _confidentiality_ evidence says whether a review was approved within 12 months. _API:_ `GET /api/data-products/{id}/port-reviews` (reviews and due dates), `POST /api/data-products/{id}/ports/{portId}/reviews` `{ environment, checkId, fourEyes }`, `GET /api/data-products/port-reviews/{reviewId}[/verify]`, `PUT …/decisions`, `POST …/submit`, `POST …/approval` `{ approve, comment }`.

### Promotion between environments

_Promote to another environment_ (the product's menu) proposes the members and port bindings of the next environment from the current one (dev → test → prod) by the **environment rules** of the discovery sources of the platforms involved: the rule of the current environment is taken off the database (Snowflake, SQL) or topic name and the next one's put on – `DEV_DB.SALES.ORDERS` → `PROD_DB.SALES.ORDERS`, `SHOP_DEV.RAW.ORDERS` → `SHOP.RAW.ORDERS` – and the same name is looked for as well (one platform per environment). The **preview** shows per member and per bound element what was **found** there, what is **missing** and what is **there already**; elements found that you may not see are counted and left out.

**Promote** (who may change the product) adds the members found with their role and binds the ports in the next environment through the same services as the pages – what is bound there stays, the elements found join, a new binding takes the contract, its version and the objectives of the current one. It records the promotion with its **evidence** – a change record, a ticket, a process run (a process _Promote a data product_ with its approvals), an address or a text – in the product's history and the audit log (`dataproduct.promoted`). The preview's fingerprint is sent along: a product that changed since is refused (`DATA_PROMOTION_INVALID`). Steward deploys nothing. _API:_ `GET /api/data-products/{id}/promotion?from=&to=`, `POST /api/data-products/{id}/promotion` `{ from, to, fingerprint, evidence: { kind, ref }, note }`, `GET /api/data-products/{id}/promotions`.

### Endorsement and maturity 5

The **owners of the product's domain** (and of the domain above it; a domain without owners: the module's managers) **endorse** a product they trust – a badge with who and when, valid until a **review date** (12 months by default, never later). Nobody endorses a product they own. An endorsement **needs a review** – and no longer counts – when its review date passed, after a breaking change of a bound data contract (the contract hook flags it), or while the product's rating is below its data objects without a reason; 30 days before the date it is _due for review_ and still counts. `dataproduct.endorsement_review` tells the endorsers, who endorsed it and the product owners once per review date and reason. **Maturity 5** (_endorsed_) is a valid endorsement on top of level 4. Endorse, endorse again and revoke are audited (`dataproduct.endorsed`, `dataproduct.endorsement_revoked`, `dataproduct.endorsement_flagged`). _API:_ `GET|PUT|DELETE /api/data-products/{id}/endorsement` (`PUT { reviewDate, note }`).

### Records of processing (GDPR Art. 30)

_Data → Records of processing_ is a **working view** for the data protection officer – not the record of processing activities itself. Per data object with personal data (filter by domain, personal or special categories, search):

| Column                    | From                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Categories, data subjects | The data object: categories of data, special categories (Art. 9), data subjects                                                                                                    |
| Legal basis, purposes     | The data object's legal basis (Art. 6(1)) and note, its purposes, and the purposes of the products that process it                                                                 |
| Processed in              | The products whose member elements implement the object or whose output ports declare it                                                                                           |
| Recipients                | The registered consumers still in force and the consuming products of those products' output ports that expose the object (declared, or through the elements bound to them)        |
| Platforms and processors  | The platforms of the implementing elements with the supplier contracts that cover them: the contract's (else its master's, else the supplier's) personal-data answer and DPA state |
| Retention, deletion rule  | The data object                                                                                                                                                                    |

Only data objects you may see are listed; products, recipients and platforms you may not see are counted, never named; suppliers and contracts only where you may see them. **CSV** (formulas defused, your language) and **PDF/A** (drawn by the report infrastructure's PDF worker) exports carry the same, and each export is audited (`dataproduct.ropa_exported`). The view reads a fixed number of statements however many data objects there are. Transfers to third countries are not recorded on suppliers yet. _API:_ `GET /api/data-products/records-of-processing[.csv|.pdf]?domain=&personalData=&q=&lang=`.
