# Price books, calculators and estimates

What will a pipeline, a topic or a new SaaS cost per month – with the prices that apply to you? Steward keeps **price books** of item prices in versions, **calculators** that turn typed inputs into quantities, and **estimates** of a service or a platform that price those quantities exactly and become plan lines when a person confirms them ([ADR 0035](adr/0035-price-books-and-calculators.md), phase 1 in 0.21.0). Since 0.23.0 (phase 2) supplier contracts carry their **conditions**, providers with a fee on top have **derived books**, platforms' contracted books can be **read from a source**, and estimates are **recalculated in the background** when prices change. Everything here belongs to the Costs module: switched off, the pages, routes, API endpoints and MCP tools go with it.

## Items, drivers and units

A book entry is an **item** with its price: an id unique in the book (the provider's SKU where there is one), optionally a price id when one SKU has several prices, a name, service and category, the **driver** (what is counted) with its **unit**, the **charge** (_usage_, _recurring_ per month, _one-off_), a **price per block** of 1, 1,000 or 1,000,000 units with up to 12 decimals – in an ISO 4217 currency **or in credits** of another item of the same book – optional **tiers** (graduated: each band at its price; volume: the whole quantity at the band reached), an **included** quantity and a **minimum** per month, a validity (_valid from_, _valid to_), attributes and a source note.

- **Units** are UCUM codes: `By` with SI and binary prefixes (`GBy` and `GiBy` differ), `bit`, `s`, `min`, `h`, `d`, `wk`, `mo`, `a`, `1`; counted things in braces (`{request}`, `{msg}`, `{credit}`, `{eCKU}`); products with `.` and one `/`: `GBy.mo`, `MBy/s`, `{msg}/s`, `{eCKU}.h`. A quantity converts only within its dimension: prefixes (decimal and binary apart) and time with **730 hours per month** (the convention of the cloud price lists, stated on every estimate). Annotations never convert – a quantity whose unit cannot become the item's is refused (`COST_PRICE_UNIT_MISMATCH`). The forms suggest drivers and units; any other may be used.
- **Prices in credits**: _warehouse M_ costs 4 `{credit}` per `h` of the item _compute-credit_, which has a money price. At most two steps, never a loop, the credit item in the same book (`COST_PRICE_CHAIN`). A step of a chain applies the credit item's tiers, not its included quantity or minimum (those belong to the account's whole use).
- **Exact arithmetic**: quantities are millionths of their unit, prices 10⁻¹² of their currency, everything whole numbers (`BigInt`); included quantity first, then tiers, then the block, then the minimum (only when anything is used); the amount is rounded half away from zero to millionths once, at the end, and shown with the currency's minor units. The same functions run in the browser and on the server (`shared/src/costPrices.ts`).

## Price books

_Costs → Price books_ lists the books you may see; a book's page has its items (500 per page, searchable), **Add item**, **Import CSV**, **Mark as checked**, its **versions** with _What changed_ and its owning teams.

- **Kinds**: **list** (published prices – everyone using Costs reads them), **contracted** (the prices of one contract – only its readers) and, since 0.23.0, **derived** (a provider's customer prices computed from a base book – [Derived books](#derived-books)). A contracted book may belong to a **platform** (its managers maintain it, who sees the platform's amounts reads it) or name a supplier contract (the book's dialog: _Supplier_ and _Contract_). A book names its supplier and contract only to readers who may see them, and accepts only ones its author may see; saving a book keeps the supplier and contract its editor may not see.
- **Shares** (0.23.0, [ADR 0033](adr/0033-one-access-model.md)): a list or derived book has the _Access_ tab – owning teams and _Shared with_ (View, Edit, Manage). Giving someone a share is a widening: the step-up and `access.shares_changed`. A contracted book's readers follow its contract or platform; shares do not count for it.
- **Versions**: every change – an item added, changed or removed, a CSV import – writes a new, immutable version with the SHA-256 of its normalised entries and its **difference** to the one before (items added and removed, prices changed with before, after and the change in percent, other fields changed). An unchanged save writes nothing. Fifty versions are kept per book, and every version an estimate uses. Every version is audited (`cost.price_book_version_added`) with the prices changed, before and after.
- **CSV import**: a header row, comma, semicolon or tab, up to 5 MB and 20,000 rows; map the columns (item, name, unit, price are required; price id, service, category, driver, charge, block, currency or one currency for the file, credit item, tiers as `0:0.10;1000:0.08`, included, minimum, valid from and to, source), choose the decimal separator, _Replace the book_ or _Add to the book_, **Preview** (nothing stored: rows read, problems by line, what would change) and **Import** – all or nothing: a file with problems is refused with the first of them (`COST_PRICE_IMPORT`). One pass over the characters, no regular expression on the content, every count and size capped.
- **Review reminder**: a book kept by hand or from CSV has _review every … days_ (default 90). When the time since it was made or last checked is up, its maintainers get **Prices to review** in the app (`cost.price_review_due`, once per book and due date; administrators only when nobody else maintains it). _Mark as checked_ records the check without a new version (`cost.price_book_checked`).
- **The Snowflake fallback's price per credit** lives in the platform's contracted book since 0.21.0: the upgrade moved every price per credit set on a Snowflake cost source into the item `compute-credit` of a contracted book of that platform (version 1, source _Upgrade_). The fallback (`METERING_DAILY_HISTORY` credits × price per credit) reads it from there – the price valid today – with the same arithmetic as before; the cost source's form still sets it (a new version of that book).
- **Shipped examples**: `examples/costs/price-books/` holds the **technical factors** only – the credits per hour of the Snowflake warehouse sizes (checked 2026-11-12) and the items the Confluent calculator names – with no money prices (decision 19). The demo's books are invented and say so.

## Contract conditions

A supplier contract's page has a section **Prices** (Costs module on) – only for who sees the contract **with its amounts**: administrators, auditors, who may change the contract, and every other reader of it unless _Hide amounts from viewers_ is on. Everyone else sees no section, and estimates show them list prices. Who may change the contract changes the conditions (a signed-in session; audited as `supplier.contract_conditions_changed` with before and after). Up to 100 per contract:

| Condition              | What                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Percent off list       | A percentage off the list price – for the whole provider, one book, a category, a service or one item                                                                                                   |
| Negotiated unit price  | A price per block (with its currency and, optionally, its own tiers) for one item, replacing the list price                                                                                             |
| Commitment             | Bands of a percentage by the **committed amount per year** of a volume schedule of the same contract (_15 % off from 100,000 USD a year committed_) – read from the schedule, its year amounts included |
| Terms of derived books | Discount on the base, markup, fee per unit, fixed fee – [Derived books](#derived-books)                                                                                                                 |

- **Scope**: a condition names the **provider** as the books name it and what it is for. The most specific wins: an item, then a service, then a category (or a driver, for fees), then a book, then the whole provider. Two conditions of one group (percentages and commitments; negotiated prices; each kind of term) on the same scope whose validity overlaps are refused (`COST_CONDITION_OVERLAP`). Without a validity of its own a condition follows the contract's term (a contract that renews keeps it).
- **Inheritance**: an order form without conditions of its own applies its master agreement's – its Prices section shows them read-only, and estimate lines say _from its master agreement_. Readers of the order form see them, as they see the terms it inherits (ADR 0031).
- **Which price applies**, per item and month, the most specific first: a **negotiated unit price** → the **book** of the estimate (a contracted book of the contract – it includes the discount, so a percentage that would apply on top is left out and flagged, on the line and in the Prices section) → the **list price less the most specific percentage** (percent off, or the commitment's band) → the **list price**. An item a contracted book does not have takes the list price with the contract's conditions. Each estimate line says how: _List price of Invented Cloud list (v7) – 12 % off by contract ICA-2026-001_.
- **The contract per provider**: an estimate chooses it per provider (_Contract for …_), or takes the default – a contract whose prices its editor sees, one covering the estimate's service or platform first, an order form before its master – or _No contract_. The choice is kept with the estimate; a contract the reader may not see is not named to them.

## Derived books

A provider that buys from a vendor and sells on with a fee – a central data platform unit, a reseller – has a **derived book**: a **base book** (list, contracted or another derived book; at most two levels, never a loop, the base's provider – `COST_PRICE_DERIVATION`) and the provider's **terms**:

- **Where the terms are**: with a **contract** (the customers' contract with the provider, a Steward supplier) its conditions of the kinds _discount on the base_ (the share of the provider's own discount it passes on), _markup_, _fee per unit_ (an amount per unit of an item or driver, _0.005 USD per GBy.mo_) and _fixed fee_ (an amount a month). Without a contract the book is a **rate card** – a platform team's internal prices – and keeps its terms itself (_Edit terms_, by its maintainers).
- **The customer price** per item: base × (1 − discount) × (1 + markup) + fee per unit, each tier alike, the minimum without the fee, exact to twelve decimals; items priced in credits keep their credits (the terms apply to the credit item's money price). The fixed fee becomes a recurring item _Service fee – {provider}_ in `{subscription}.mo` that an estimate adds once per subscription, not per estimate. A fee whose currency or unit does not fit an item is left out and recorded with the version.
- **Versions follow the base**: a new version of the base book, or a changed term, writes a new version of the derived book in the same step, its cause recorded (_Invented Cloud list v2_, _terms of the contract changed_; audited `cost.price_book_derived`). Its prices are never written by hand. Terms that start or end on a day are applied by the job `costs` that day.
- **Transparent where the reader may see it**: an estimate line priced from a derived book shows **base, discount, markup and fee** (_Base: 120.00 USD (Invented Cloud list v2) · Discount 10 %: −12.00 USD · Markup 9 %: 9.72 USD_) to who sees the terms – the contract's conditions, or a rate card's maintainers – **and** the base book. Everyone else who sees the derived book sees the **customer price only**: one line per item, no base, discount, markup, fee or the version's cause – with a public base book any one of them would give the provider's discount away by subtraction. Who does not see the derived book at all sees the list price.
- **Publishing** a rate card to other teams is a **share** of the derived book (View); it gives the customer prices, never the base or the terms. The maintainers of a derived book must see its base book.

## Sources

A **contracted book of a platform** can be read from what the platform reports, the discount included (the book's _Source_; who maintains the book sets it, and it is read with their rights on the platform):

- **Snowflake rate sheet**: `SNOWFLAKE.ORGANIZATION_USAGE.RATE_SHEET_DAILY` of the platform's account through the SQL API (one read-only statement) – the latest effective rate per service type, usage type and currency: compute per credit (`compute-credit`, which the cost source's fallback reads), storage per TB-month, data transfer per TB, other service types per credit. Its `CONTRACT_NUMBER` links the book to the Steward contract with that reference. Once a day, with the costs job. Not for reseller contracts (no organisation usage views).
- **Confluent, observed**: the Costs API's lines of the **last complete month** (read from the 4th of the next month): per product, line type, unit and network access type the effective price Σ amount ÷ Σ quantity, the list price and the discount as attributes (`listPrice`, `discountPercent`). Labelled _observed_ – only what was used has a price; keep a list book by hand for the rest.
- A read goes through the platform's own client and so through the outbound policy, at most 25 MB and 20,000 entries (`COST_PRICE_SOURCE_TOO_LARGE`). It replaces what it brought the time before and keeps items written by hand (the credits per hour of the warehouse sizes, say); an unchanged read writes no version. A failed read keeps the last version and says why on the book. _Read now_ reads at once (audited `cost.price_book_read`).

## Calculators

_Costs → Calculators_: templates of typed inputs, named intermediate values and lines that each name an item (provider and SKU) and a **JSONata expression for its quantity – never money**. Steward prices each line with the book an estimate uses, so one calculator serves list prices, contracted prices and internal rates.

```yaml
kind: costCalculator
id: snowflake-pipeline
name: Snowflake pipeline
inputs:
  - { key: size, label: Warehouse size, type: select, options: [XS, S, M, L, XL], default: S }
  - { key: minutes, label: Runtime per run, type: number, unit: min, default: 12, min: 0, max: 1440 }
  - { key: runs, label: Runs per day, type: integer, default: 24, min: 0, max: 1440 }
let:
  hours: inputs.minutes / 60 * inputs.runs * const.hoursPerMonth / 24
lines:
  - { key: compute, item: { provider: Snowflake, sku: "{{ 'warehouse-' & inputs.size }}" }, quantity: let.hours, unit: h }
```

- **Inputs**: `number`, `integer`, `select`, `boolean` and `table` (rows of columns – one line per row with `each:`), each with a label, unit, default, bounds and help. A `number` or `integer` may be given as a **range** – low, expected, high – unless it says `range: false`.
- **Expressions** see `inputs`, `let` (the named values before), `const` (`hoursPerMonth` 730, `daysPerMonth`), `row` (in a line with `each`) and `$attr(provider, sku, key)` (an attribute of an item of the estimate's book). SKU, provider and unit may be templates (`{{ … }}`). `when:` leaves a line out.
- **Sandbox**: the engine's guard rails, tightened – 500 ms per calculation, sequences of at most 10,000, at most 200 resulting lines, quantities finite and not below zero; no clock (`$now`, `$millis`), randomness, platform variables, secrets, datasets or network. A broken formula answers `COST_CALCULATOR_FORMULA` with its line; a quantity that is not a number, below zero or too large `COST_CALCULATOR_OUTPUT`.
- **Versions**: the editor keeps a **draft**, tried with **Calculate** (the dry run, nothing stored – only for who may write calculators, as it runs formulas); **Publish** makes a new, immutable version. **Export YAML** for Git; **Git sync** brings `kind: costCalculator` files in like processes ([GIT-SYNC.md](GIT-SYNC.md#files-and-formats)): accepting an update publishes a new version. Libraries do not carry calculators.
- **Examples** (`examples/costs/calculators/`): _Snowflake pipeline_, _Confluent topic and cluster_ (eCKU- or CKU-hours, ingress and egress from the throughput, storage from throughput × retention × the copies billed, partitions, connector tasks and throughput) and _Throughput × price_ (one line per row: provider, SKU, a rate per s, min, h or d, the hours a day – the generic calculator for anything else).

## Estimates

An estimate belongs to a **service or a platform** (the owners of cost plans) and is optionally for an application or system of the service, a platform, or – since 0.24.0, with the data products module – a **data product** its editor may see (_For a data product_ on its page): the product's _Costs_ tab then sets it against what is attributed to the product ([DATA-PRODUCTS.md](DATA-PRODUCTS.md#estimates)); plan lines from it stay the service's or platform's, never the product's. Its plan's tab lists them (_Estimates_, _New estimate_); its page has the inputs, lines by hand, the books, ad-hoc adjustments, the result, the 36 months ahead, _Compare_ and the plan lines.

- **Prices**: per provider the book the estimate chose, else a **contracted book** the calculating person may see (the one of the contract that applies, then the estimate's platform's), a **derived book**, else the **list book** – and the [contract conditions](#contract-conditions) of the contract chosen for the provider. Each line says how its price was found (_Contracted prices: Snowflake (demo) – contracted prices (v3)_, _List price of … – 12 % off by contract …_), its unit price per block, the credits it took, the exchange rate where converted, and – priced from a derived book, for who sees its terms – base, discount, markup and fee.
- **Lines by hand**: an item of a visible book and a quantity – anything with a price can be estimated without a formula.
- **Ranges**: with ranged inputs the estimate is calculated three times – all inputs at low, at expected, at high – and every line and total shows low, expected and high (low the smallest and high the largest of the three, expected the expected run). A span of the inputs, not a probability; the expected figures drive plan lines (`COST_RANGE_ORDER` when low ≤ expected ≤ high does not hold).
- **Ad-hoc adjustments** (_Anpassung_): on the whole estimate or one line, a **percentage** (−90 to +500 %) or, for a line, a **fixed unit price** (block and currency), each with a **reason** (up to 200 characters). Applied last, after the conversion; shown on the line beside the price found; the totals say _with ad-hoc adjustments_; never written back. A line's own adjustment replaces the estimate's. A fixed price whose price found has moved since it was set is flagged (_Fixed – the book now says …_).
- **Exchange rates**: an estimate has one currency; a price in another is converted with the **manual rate** of the latest date on or before the calculation (direct, else the inverse of the opposite pair), set by the costs managers under _Costs → Price books → Exchange rates_ (`cost.fx_rate_set`). The rate, its date and source are on every converted line and stored with the result (`COST_FX_RATE_MISSING` without one). Only estimates convert.
- **The result** is stored and never recomputed on read: per line the quantity, the book and version, the unit price, the amount; totals **per month** (usage and recurring) and **one-off**; and, when a price changes within them (a valid-from ahead), **per month over the next 36 months** – a chart with its low–high band and a table. The book versions used are kept.
- **Recalculate with current prices** keeps the new result beside the current one (_New prices: +8 %_) until **Accept new prices** (`cost.estimate_accepted`); a change of inputs calculates at once. The last 20 results stay as the history. Since 0.23.0 the job does it on its own – [below](#recalculation-and-estimate-changed).
- **Compare**: up to four more variants of the inputs with the same prices – a dry run, nothing stored – as bars with their low–high span and a table: per month and one-off, the difference to the first, the lines that make it, the cheapest.
- **Plan lines** (proposed per fiscal year, confirmed by a person): usage and recurring amounts as a monthly line per calculator group from the start month – one line per stretch of equal months where a price changes in the year – and one-off amounts as a once line, category _Cloud_ by default; the line remembers the estimate and what it was made from, says _with ad-hoc adjustments_ where it is so, and shows **Estimate changed** in the plan when the estimate no longer proposes the same. **Update lines** brings the lines up to date; a line changed by hand keeps its amount. An estimate priced with contracted rates asks first: _Readers of this plan will see these amounts_ (`COST_ESTIMATE_HIDDEN_RATES`). Removing an estimate keeps its plan lines.

## Recalculation and Estimate changed

- A new version of a book an estimate used (by hand, CSV, a source read, a derivation), or a changed condition of a contract it used (an order form's master too), marks the estimate; the job `costs` recalculates the marked ones – at most 500 a run, one after the other – with **the same books and contracts** its result used (the job sees every book; it never picks one its editor did not), and keeps the new result **beside** the current one until a person chooses _Accept new prices_.
- **Estimate changed** (`cost.estimate_changed`, in the app and by e-mail by default, quiet-hours group _Budgets and costs_) goes to the estimate's owners – a service's named owners and owning teams' members, a platform's managers and owning teams' members (administrators when there are none) – once per estimate and price version, when the monthly total moves by at least the threshold (_Costs → Settings_, 5 % by default). Each recipient is told **in their own view**: a line priced with rates they may not see is at list price for them, so a change of a hidden rate alone tells them nothing and sends them nothing; the cause (a book and version, a contract) is named only to who may see it.

## Who sees and changes what

| Action                                                                  | Who (phase 1)                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Read a list book, a calculator; run the dry run with stored calculators | everyone who uses the Costs module                                                                                                                                                        |
| Maintain a book or calculator without owning teams; set exchange rates  | the **costs managers**: administrators and designers (until the Costs module's manager role of ADR 0034)                                                                                  |
| Maintain a book or calculator with owning teams                         | the designers among the owning teams' members; a designer's new book or calculator gets their team                                                                                        |
| Maintain a platform's contracted book                                   | who may manage the platform and its owning teams' members                                                                                                                                 |
| Read a contracted book                                                  | a platform's: who sees the platform's amounts; another: its owning teams' members, administrators, auditors and who sees the contract it names                                            |
| Read and change a contract's conditions (_Prices_)                      | who sees the contract with its amounts; changed by who may change the contract (0.23.0)                                                                                                   |
| Read a derived book; its terms                                          | its owning teams and shares (a platform's rate card: who sees the platform's amounts); the terms: who sees its contract's conditions – a rate card's: its maintainers – and its base book |
| Share a list or derived book                                            | its Manage holders (who may maintain it), with the step-up                                                                                                                                |
| Set or read a book's source                                             | who maintains the platform's contracted book                                                                                                                                              |
| Try a calculator's draft (formulas)                                     | who may write calculators                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Create, change, recalculate, accept an estimate; plan lines from it     | who may change the owner's cost plan                                                                                                                                                      |
| Read an estimate                                                        | who may read the owner's cost plan; amounts by _Hide amounts from viewers_                                                                                                                |

- **Contracted prices stay with their readers.** A reader of an estimate who may not see a book it used – or the conditions of the contract that priced a line – sees that line **at the list price**, marked _Priced with rates you may not see – shown at list price_ (`priceHidden`), the totals as _at list price for some lines_, the 36-month projection left out and the book and contract not named; without a list price the line's amount is left out and the total marked incomplete. Nothing is derived from a hidden price – also not by division or subtraction: the same view goes to the list of estimates, the plan proposals, the dry run, `/api/v1`, MCP and _Estimate changed_. Plan lines from an estimate priced with such rates ask first (`COST_ESTIMATE_HIDDEN_RATES`).
- Every write needs a signed-in session; API tokens and MCP read. Audited: `cost.price_book_added|changed|removed`, `cost.price_book_version_added`, `cost.price_book_derived`, `cost.price_book_read`, `supplier.contract_conditions_changed`, `cost.price_book_checked`, `cost.calculator_added|changed|published|removed`, `cost.estimate_added|changed|accepted|removed` (adjustments with their reason, before and after), `cost.estimate_lines_added|updated`, `cost.fx_rate_set`. Owning teams of books and calculators are set in their pages' _Owning teams_ panel (owned kinds `cost_price_book`, `cost_calculator`).

## API, MCP and limits

- **Internal**: `/api/costs/price-books[/:bookId[/entries|/versions/:n|/import/preview|/import|/check|/terms|/source|/read]]`, `/api/costs/contracts` (the contracts whose conditions you see), `/api/costs/contracts/:contractId/conditions` (GET, PUT – under `/api/costs`, so it switches off with the module), `/api/costs/calculators[/:calculatorId[/export|/versions/:n]]`, `POST /api/costs/calculate` (the dry run: up to five variants with a comparison), `/api/costs/estimates?ownerKind=&ownerId=`, `…/:estimateId[/recalculate|/accept|/proposals|/update-lines]`, `/api/costs/fx-rates`.
- **Public, read-only** ([API.md](API.md)): `GET /api/v1/price-books`, `…/{bookId}`, `…/{bookId}/items`, `GET /api/v1/cost-estimates` (`serviceId`, `platformId`), `…/{estimateId}`; the cost plans' lines carry `estimate`. A derived book's items are its customer prices; estimate lines carry `foundBy` and – for who sees the terms – `breakdown`; contract conditions are never part of the API.
- **MCP** ([MCP.md](MCP.md)): `list_price_books`, `get_price_book`, `list_cost_calculators`, `get_cost_calculator`, `list_cost_units`, `list_cost_estimates`, `get_cost_estimate` and the dry run **`calculate_cost_estimate`** – up to five variants with ranges, books and adjustments, compared with the first; it stores nothing, takes stored calculators only, and is limited to 30 calculations a minute per token (each variant counts).
- **Limits**: 100 conditions per contract (and terms per rate card); a derived book two levels above its base at most; 25 MB and 20,000 entries per source read; the job recalculates at most 500 estimates a run; 500 books; 20,000 entries per version; 50 versions kept per book (and those in use); 200 calculators; 50 inputs, 100 lines and 50 named values per calculator, 4,000 characters per expression, 64 KB per definition; 200 lines and 50 adjustments per estimate; 200 estimates per owner; 20 results kept per estimate; 5 variants per dry run; 2,000 exchange rates.
- **Performance**: lists and pages read stored results and never calculate; a book's page and an estimate's page take a fixed number of statements whatever their size; a calculation reads each book once per round of credit items.

## Not yet

Of phase 2: ECB exchange rates (manual rates only), shares of calculators, _Size a volume_, estimate against actual with _Over the estimate_, the Costs module's manager role. Later: Azure, AWS and Databricks price lists, Infracost import, measured inputs.
